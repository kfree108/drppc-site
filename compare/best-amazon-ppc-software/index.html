Start with the number this category avoids printing

Every roundup compares features. Almost none state the size of the problem the features exist to solve, so here is one, taken from our own database rather than a survey.

Walkize is a brand we manage and are permitted to name. Across their Amazon search-term data, $89,885 of ad spend — 33.6% of everything they spent — went to search terms that produced zero orders. Not underperforming terms. Terms with no order at all behind them.

That is the number a software purchase has to be measured against. A platform that reclaims a third of that waste is trivially worth a few hundred a month. One that produces a nicer chart of the same waste is not. Keep that test in mind below, including where we describe our own product.

The ranked picks, by the job

Different tools genuinely win for different readers. Ordered by how many sellers each is the right answer for, not by how much we like them.

  • 1. Best for a mid-market brand running its own ads — Teikametrics. Goal-based rather than rule-based: you state the objective, it sets the targets. Its own pricing page lists Essentials at $179/month billed monthly or $149/month billed annually up to $10,000 in monthly ad spend, with Advanced and Enterprise quoted individually and described as plus 3% of ad spend over $10K. Also the pick if you sell on Walmart, where it is genuinely strong rather than nominally supported.
  • 2. Best if you already pay for Helium 10 — its Ads module. Not the strongest bidder, but the marginal cost is low and the keyword and listing work already lives there. Helium 10 publishes Platinum at $129/month or $99 billed annually, Diamond at $359 or $279 annually, and Enterprise from $1,499/month billed annually. Read the footnote: Diamond carries a 2% management fee on PPC spend managed through Helium 10 Ads. That fee, not the subscription, is your real cost above modest spend.
  • 3. Best for enterprise and multi-retailer portfolios — Pacvue. Built for many brands and retailers under one roof, with the operational reporting large teams need. No published price; every route leads to a demo. Budget for an annual contract and for real implementation time.
  • 4. Best for people who want to write the rules — Scale Insights or Zon.Tools. If you have a firm opinion on how bids should move and resent an algorithm overruling you, this is your shelf. Scale Insights prices by the number of ASINs you automate rather than by spend, which is unusual and, for a narrow catalogue, cheap.
  • 5. Best for a brand with an analyst — DataHawk. Built to hand you clean data and get out of the way, with export into your own warehouse as the point. It publishes no price at all: custom annual plans and a demo. Excellent with someone to write the queries; a wasted annual contract without.
  • 6. Best cheap starting point — SellerApp. A real free tier at $0, paid plans from $99 a month, and an agency arm quoted from $300 plus 0.5%-2.5% of ad spend if you later want people rather than software. Few vendors show you that whole ladder on one page.

How to compare total cost, which is not the headline fee

The subscription is the number everyone benchmarks and the least important part of the bill above modest spend. The variable rate decides it.

At $100,000 a month in ad spend, one percentage point is $12,000 a year. That single point usually dwarfs the difference between two subscription tiers you spent a week agonising over. Make every vendor answer four questions in writing:

  • Is there a percentage of ad spend, and what is the exact figure at my spend level?
  • Is it charged on all spend, or only above a threshold?
  • Does it apply to every marketplace and ad type, including DSP?
  • Is it capped, and at what?

None of this argues against variable pricing. We charge a percentage ourselves, as do several tools above, and it aligns a vendor with your growth in a way a flat fee does not. The problem is never the model — it is a vendor who will not put the whole formula on one line.

What none of this software fixes

Bid automation optimises inside a box drawn by four decisions no platform makes for you: your landed cost, your price, whether you have stock, and whether the creative converts. Get those wrong and the best bidder in the category steers you efficiently toward a worse outcome.

Stock wrecks accounts most often and gets discussed least. A campaign that has spent eight weeks building rank does not pause politely when the warehouse empties; it burns budget, loses position, and hands the placement to whoever did forecast properly. If that is your recurring failure, better bidding software is the wrong purchase — Dr. Stock is built for inventory and supply chain, and fixing it usually returns more than any bid change.

The second thing software does not fix is attention. Every tool here waits for you to open it. The waste in the Walkize account was visible in the data the whole time. Visibility was never the constraint.

The right pick depends on how many hours a week the account will actually get. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. Common questions

What is the best Amazon PPC software overall?

For most mid-market brands running their own advertising, Teikametrics, and it is the clear choice if you also sell on Walmart. Enterprise portfolios are better served by Pacvue, rule-writers by Scale Insights, data teams by DataHawk. Anyone naming one winner for all readers is ranking by affiliate commission.

Is expensive PPC software worth it?

Measure it against your waste, not against other subscriptions. In one account we manage, a third of all ad spend went to search terms that returned no orders. Against that, a few hundred a month is noise — provided the tool reduces it rather than reporting it more attractively.

Should I avoid tools that charge a percentage of ad spend?

No. Percentage pricing aligns a vendor with your growth, and plenty of good platforms use it, including ours. What matters is disclosure: the exact rate, whether it applies to all spend or only above a threshold, whether it covers DSP, and whether it is capped. One undisclosed point at $100,000 a month is $12,000 a year.

Can PPC software replace an agency?

It replaces the mechanics, not the judgement. Software will not decide your price, catch a stockout six weeks out, or tell you the main image is why conversion is poor. If your problems are structural, automation optimises toward the wrong destination.

What should a smaller seller buy first?

A profitability tool showing true net margin per order, plus the discipline to review search terms weekly. SellerApp's free tier starts that without a commitment.
