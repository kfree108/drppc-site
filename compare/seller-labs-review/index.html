Seller Labs review: what is actually on sale in 2026

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1984 words · Written against what currently ranked for “seller labs review”
The short answer

Seller Labs today is a software-only, owner-operated business selling three à la carte tools — Feedback Genius, Ad Genius and Profit Genius — plus a bundle. It sold its managed services arm and changed ownership in 2023, and retired two tools. Most reviews still ranking describe the older company.

Most reviews of this company describe a company that no longer exists

Three changes happened in quick succession and almost none of the ranking coverage caught up with any of them. All three are documented on Seller Labs' own about page.

  • May 2023 — the services arm was sold. Seller Labs had a managed advertising and services business. It does not any more. Articles that recommend Seller Labs as an Amazon PPC agency are recommending a business that was divested three years ago.
  • July 2023 — the company itself was acquired by an eight-figure Amazon seller, and now trades under new ownership with the buyer writing on the company blog. This is not a private-equity roll-up; it is an operator buying a tool set.
  • The product line was cut. Scope and Quantify are gone, their URLs redirecting away. Ignite was renamed Ad Genius, and its page still carries the old name in the title. Seller Labs PRO, the all-in-one bundle most reviews price, has no product page at all.

A related tell about the state of the coverage: an employee-review site ranks on the first page for this buying query. When a workplace-reviews page outranks commercial analysis for a software purchase, it means nobody has written serious current analysis, not that the product is obscure.

Judge it product by product, because that is how it is sold

The à la carte structure is the point of this company, so a single verdict is the wrong shape. Take each in turn.

Feedback Genius is the most consistently praised piece, and it is the one with the clearest job: automating Amazon's own review and feedback requests, centrally managing product reviews and handling seller feedback. Reviewers specifically praise how quickly templates can be built. If your operation is losing reviews simply because nobody remembers to ask, this is a small purchase with a direct effect.

Ad Genius is the advertising product, and its defining commercial feature is what it does not charge: their page states there is no ad spend requirement, no ad spend cost and no spend limit. In a category where percentage-of-spend is the norm — including for us — that is a genuine differentiator for anyone whose ad spend is large relative to their revenue.

Profit Genius handles analytics and profitability, with a free stretch for small sellers. It answers the question most sellers cannot answer quickly: what did I actually make on this ASIN after everything.

They also ship an Amazon data connector for AI tools inside the bundle, which is a more modern move than the company's public profile suggests and puts them roughly in step with much larger rivals on that specific capability.

What owner-operation means for you, in both directions

An eight-figure Amazon seller owning your software is an unusual situation and it deserves a fair reading rather than a reflex.

The genuine upside is that the person setting the roadmap runs an Amazon business of the kind you run. Software built by people who feel the problem tends to fix the annoying things first. It also means the company is not being steered toward an enterprise segment you are not in — a fate that has quietly made several tools in this category worse for small sellers.

The fair question is capacity. A bootstrapped, owner-operated business has a different pace and a different resilience profile from a venture-backed one, and you should ask about it directly rather than assume in either direction. The right diligence questions are the same ones we would want asked of us: what shipped in the last six months, where is it documented, what is the export path for my data, and what are the change-of-control terms.

Those questions are not a warning about this company. They are the standing questions for a category that consolidates constantly — several tools discussed on our other pages have been absorbed into suites and retired while their reviews carried on ranking.

A thin review base, and the one complaint that recurs

Seller Labs' public rating sits in respectable territory, but on a review base small enough that it should be read as directional rather than statistical. Fourteen reviews is not a sample; it is a handful of experiences. One strongly worded account moves that average more than a real product change would.

How to read a thin base usefully: ignore the average entirely and read the individual reviews, checking the date and the reviewer's business size against yours. Three reviews from businesses that look like yours are worth more than a hundred that do not.

The one theme that recurs often enough to mention: reviewers report being charged after a trial without having registered that it would convert. We report that as what it is — customer accounts rather than verified fact — and the useful response is procedural rather than judgemental. Before any trial, on any vendor including us, note the conversion date, note exactly what action stops billing, and ask for written notice of fee changes. That last ask has come out of enough of our competitor research that we now treat it as standard advice.

The praise clusters are equally consistent: the way data is presented, the speed of building review-request templates, and the flexibility of paying for one tool rather than a suite.

What it does not do, and what that costs

Seller Labs is software and has deliberately stopped being anything else. There is no strategist layer, no account team, no managed option. That is a clean position and we respect it, but it means the tool inherits whatever attention you can give it.

Here is the shape of what goes unattended. Walkize is a client account we are cleared to name publicly. From December 2025 to August 2026 it spent $267,131 against $601,614 in attributed sales, a 2.25x return at a 44.4% ACOS. Inside that account, $89,885 — 33.6% of every dollar spent — went to search terms that produced zero orders, across 89,045 individual search terms, while the top 1% of terms drove 75.5% of all sales.

Nothing about that requires better software to see. It requires somebody to look, at a level of detail that a busy owner running fulfilment, supply and customer service will not reach on a Thursday afternoon. That is the honest gap between buying a tool and having the work done, and it is worth pricing when you compare a modest monthly subscription against a service.

The second thing it does not do is research at the depth of the larger suites. If discovery is your bottleneck, this is not the purchase.

Which one you should actually pick

Seller Labs suits owner-operators who want to buy one capability at a time, keep their own hands on the account and avoid any percentage of ad spend. Feedback Genius is the strongest piece. It is the wrong purchase if you need deep research, a strategist, or somebody to actually run the account — it sold that business deliberately and does not pretend otherwise.

What to do with this

Judge this on the job you actually need done, not the feature list. Pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — on the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Then ask whether the thing you are about to buy closes that gap, or just shows it to you.

Common questions

Is Seller Labs still an agency?

No. Their own about page records that the managed services business was sold in May 2023, and the company itself was acquired in July 2023 by an eight-figure Amazon seller. Seller Labs today is software only. Any article recommending them as an Amazon PPC agency is describing a business that no longer sits inside the company.

What happened to Ignite, Scope and Quantify?

Ignite was renamed Ad Genius and its old URL redirects to the new page, which still carries the former name in its title. Scope and Quantify have been retired and their URLs redirect away. The current line-up is Feedback Genius, Ad Genius, Profit Genius and a bundle of the three.

Is Seller Labs good for Amazon PPC?

It is a reasonable self-serve advertising tool for a hands-on seller, and its commercial advantage is that it takes no percentage of your ad spend. It is not a research platform and it is not a managed service. If the problem is that nobody is reading the account rather than that the software is missing, a cheaper tool will not close that gap.

How reliable are Seller Labs reviews?

Treat them as directional. The public review base is small enough that a couple of strong opinions move the average more than the product does. Read individual reviews with their dates and the reviewer's business size, and discount anything describing the old four-tool suite or the agency arm, since both predate the 2023 changes.

What should I check before starting the free trial?

The exact date it converts, what action stops billing, and whether you will receive written notice of any fee change. Reviewers report post-trial charges they had not registered, which is a common experience across this category rather than something specific to one vendor. Ask all three questions of any vendor, including us.
