You cannot buy it, so review the standard instead

Every ranking result for this term still reads as a buying guide, several of them stamped with the current year. The product behind them was retired at the end of 2021 and its users were moved into Helium 10's advertising tooling. The old site serves a hosting placeholder.

So this page does something different. What Prestozon was actually praised for

Strip away the nostalgia and the reputation rested on four things.

It worked at search-term level. Prestozon's core loop was the search-term report: find the terms taking money without producing orders, negate them, find the terms converting inside a broad match and promote them. That is still the highest-yield loop in Amazon advertising, and a surprising number of tools built since then present keyword-level summaries and call it done.

You could see why it did what it did. The logic was legible. When a bid moved, there was a stated reason attached, and that made it possible to disagree with the tool rather than merely tolerate it. Trust in automation is built by being able to audit it, and that lesson has been partially forgotten.

It priced against what you had actually spent. The archived pricing page set your tier from your trailing thirty days of Sponsored Products spend, with a single-month adjustment if you spiked, rather than locking you into a band you had forecast.

It let you start for free, permanently. Not a fourteen-day trial — an indefinite free tier limited to one campaign, so you could watch it work on something real before paying.

None of those are exotic capabilities. They are simply a coherent point of view about what a bidding tool owes its user, and that point of view is the actual legacy.

The successor scorecard: five things to demand

Take that point of view and turn it into questions you can ask on a demo. We use a version of this ourselves when we assess tooling.

  • Does it act at search-term level, or only at keyword level? Ask to see the screen where a wasteful search term becomes a negative. If the answer is a report you export and act on manually, that is a report, not automation.
  • Can I see the reason for every change it made? Not a log of what changed — the reason it changed. Ask for a screenshot of a change history from a live account.
  • Can I preview a change before it fires? The ability to see what a rule would do, before it does it, is the difference between confidence and hope.
  • Does the fee follow my actual spend, and is the variable part capped? Percentage pricing is normal here and we charge it ourselves. What matters is whether the number is knowable at every spend level, not just at the one on the quote.
  • What happens to my rules, my negatives and my history if you are acquired? Prestozon's own shutdown notice promised that data, settings and rules would carry across. Ask for that promise in writing before you need it, from any vendor including us.

How the realistic replacements score

Four tools sit in the space Prestozon vacated. Each is genuinely good at something, and none of them is good at all five points above.

Helium 10 Ads is the direct descendant, since Prestozon's capability was merged into it. Its real strength is elsewhere though: the keyword and product research underneath it is the reference standard in this category, and if discovery is also on your list, nothing else here competes. Note that advertising sits on the Diamond plan rather than the entry tier, and that their pricing FAQ states Diamond customers incur a 2% management fee on PPC spend managed through the ads product.

Scale Insights is the closest match to Prestozon's philosophy. Roughly a dozen configurable algorithms covering bidding, harvesting, negatives, dayparting, placements and budgets, and — the standout — the ability to preview what a rule will change before it executes. Its licensing is per automated ASIN, and the fine print worth catching is that one licence covers one product in one country, so a multi-marketplace catalogue consumes more than you expect. Reviewers are consistent that it is not a beginner's tool and that rules need a human to keep them current.

Teikametrics, rebuilt around a platform it calls ARI as of December 2025, is the choice if advertising is entangled with listings and inventory across Amazon, Walmart and TikTok Shop. It also makes Amazon Marketing Cloud audiences usable without SQL, which is a real and under-praised strength.

Perpetua takes the opposite view to Prestozon: set a target ACOS and a budget, and let it optimise continuously across every ad format including DSP. It is well built and suits teams that do not want to operate a platform, but it scores lowest of the four on the auditability question, which was Prestozon's signature.

The limit Prestozon never solved, and nor has its successor

Here is the honest weakness in the thing everyone remembers fondly. A rule acts on a case you thought to describe. The account does not restrict itself to those cases.

Walkize is a client account we are cleared to name publicly. From December 2025 to August 2026 it ran $267,131 of ad spend against $601,614 in attributed sales — 2.25x, a 44.4% ACOS. Underneath that, $89,885, or 33.6% of every dollar the account spent, went to search terms that produced zero orders. That was 89,045 distinct terms, out of 30,921 unique terms tracked across 244,510 clicks at a $1.09 blended cost per click, while the top 1% of terms drove 75.5% of the sales.

You cannot write a rule per term at that resolution, and no rules engine — Prestozon's or anyone's — discovers a problem nobody described to it. That is not a failure of the tools. It is the ceiling of the approach, and it is why the interesting question moved from what rules should I write to who is reading this account.

What to do with this

Judge this on the job you actually need done, not the feature list. Pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — on the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Then ask whether the thing you are about to buy closes that gap, or just shows it to you.

Common questions

Is Prestozon still available?

No. Helium 10 acquired it and subscriber access ended at the close of 2021, with users migrated into the advertising product now sold as Helium 10 Ads. The old domain serves a hosting placeholder. Reviews and comparison pages describing it as a current option are simply out of date.

What is the closest tool to Prestozon today?

In philosophy, Scale Insights — configurable algorithms, search-term level action, and the ability to preview what a change will do before it executes. In lineage, Helium 10 Ads, since Prestozon's capability was folded directly into it. Those are different answers because the product that inherited the code did not inherit the design point of view.

Was Prestozon good?

By the standards of its time, yes, and specifically for four reasons: it worked at search-term level, its logic was legible, its fee followed what you had actually spent, and it had a permanent free tier rather than a countdown trial. Those four things are a reasonable scorecard for whatever you buy in its place.

Why do so many tools in this category disappear?

Advertising tooling keeps getting folded into larger suites — a bidding tool is far more valuable inside a research platform or a commerce cloud than it is alone. The brand is retired, the pricing page comes down, and the reviews keep ranking. It is worth asking every vendor what happens to your configuration and history if that happens to them.

Can automation replace a person on the account?

It can replace the repetitive parts and it cannot replace judgement about what to try next. The test is whether anything in your setup would notice a problem you never described to it. In one account we can name, 89,045 separate search terms spent money and produced no orders — a scale no rule set reaches, and the reason the useful question is who reads the account, not what rules exist.
