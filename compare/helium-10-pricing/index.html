What Helium 10 costs right now

These are the figures on Helium 10's own pricing page, read on 20 August 2026. Monthly and annual rates differ enough to change the decision, so both are listed.

  • Free: limited Chrome extension access. Enough to see what the data looks like, not enough to run on.
  • Platinum — $129/month, or $99/month billed annually. Helium 10 puts the annual saving at $360. Aimed at $0-$100K in yearly sales. The caps that actually bite: 100 keyword searches a month, 20 ASINs tracked, two Seller Central connections, one extra user.
  • Diamond — $359/month, or $279/month billed annually (a $960 saving). Aimed at $100K-$10M. Adds the AI Listing Builder, rules-based ad automation and dayparting, P&L reporting, a managed refund service, 1,000 ASINs, ten Seller Central connections and six users.
  • Enterprise — from $1,499/month, billed annually. For sellers past $10M. Dedicated success manager, up to 5,000 ASINs, 15+ seller accounts, and ad budget support quoted against $25K-$1M in monthly spend.

Two things the headline numbers hide. Helium 10's pricing page advertises no free trial and no money-back window. And add-ons — market share tracking, the training tier — are quoted separately and sit outside the plan price.

The 2% fee most pricing pages skip

Read the Diamond column properly and there is a line that says 2% managed ad spend fee. Helium 10's ads module bills a percentage of the spend that flows through it, on top of the subscription.

That is not a knock. We charge a percentage of spend ourselves, and for anything touching bids at scale it is a sensible way to price — the vendor's cost genuinely rises with the account. The problem is only that a pricing article quotes you $279 a month and stops.

Do the arithmetic on your own account. Push $40,000 of monthly ad spend through the ads tool and 2% is another $800 a month, roughly three times the subscription you were told about. Helium 10's help documentation also describes spend thresholds above which the fee applies, and those thresholds are written differently there than on the pricing page. Get the current terms in writing from them before you budget.

What $279 a month does not include

The subscription buys software. It does not buy anyone to run it. That distinction is the whole cost question, and almost no pricing roundup goes near it.

Budget for the hours honestly. A Diamond seat with rules-based automation still needs a person to decide which rules exist, watch what they do, harvest search terms into exact-match campaigns, cut the losers, and notice when a rule is quietly draining budget into a product that is three weeks from going out of stock. Four to ten hours a week once an account has real breadth. At any defensible hourly rate, that operator time is the larger line item.

The second thing missing is judgement. Rules fire on thresholds. A threshold does not know that a SKU has six weeks of cover left, that the new colourway launches Tuesday, or that the bundle carries thinner margin than the parent ASIN. Someone has to hold those facts, and the software does not.

Who should pay Helium 10's price

Plenty of people should, and we would say so to their face. If your week is product research, listing writing and keyword work, Helium 10 has the deepest toolset in the category and Diamond is good value measured against what a research contractor charges. If you sell on Amazon and Walmart and want one index to work from, it is the obvious pick. The listing tooling in particular is better than anything we would build ourselves.

Where the price turns sour is the seller who bought Diamond mainly to fix advertising. They end up with a dashboard, a 2% fee, and a weekly job they do not have time to do. That is a different purchase from the one they thought they were making, and no amount of feature depth fixes it.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. On the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Whatever you buy — a seat, a service, or nothing — that number is the one it has to move, and a cheaper tool nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

Does Helium 10 charge a percentage of ad spend?

Yes. The Diamond plan lists a 2% managed ad spend fee for spend run through its ads module, on top of the subscription. Their help documentation adds spend thresholds that the pricing page does not mention, so confirm the current terms directly before you build a budget around them.

Is there a Helium 10 free trial?

Their pricing page lists a limited free plan — mostly Chrome extension access — but advertises no time-limited trial and no money-back window. That is worth weighing against annual billing, which is where the better rate lives. Check their site for current terms before committing to twelve months.

Is Platinum enough if I am running ads?

For a small catalogue, possibly. The caps are what decide it: 20 tracked ASINs and 100 keyword searches a month go quickly once you are working a real listing set. Most sellers running meaningful ad spend end up on Diamond, which is where the ad automation and the 2% fee both live.
