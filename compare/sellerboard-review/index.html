Sellerboard review: excellent at one job, honest about the rest

Sellerboard is the best-value profit analytics tool for Amazon sellers. It calculates true net profit per order with every fee, refund, ad cost and COGS accounted for. Its limits are real: the setup work is on you, non-Amazon channels are thinner, and it reports on advertising rather than running it.

The one thing it does better than almost anything

Most sellers think they know their margins. Then they load their real costs into sellerboard and find out the product they have been pushing hardest loses eleven cents an order.

That is the whole value here. Sellerboard reconstructs net profit per order — referral fee, FBA fee, storage, returns, refund costs, ad spend, promotions and your landed cost — and shows the result per product, per day, in something close to real time. Doing that properly in a spreadsheet is a week of work and it goes stale immediately. Doing it wrong is the most common reason a growing Amazon business quietly stops making money.

The related pieces are strong too. Reimbursement tracking flags stock Amazon lost or damaged and never paid you for, which for a mid-sized seller often covers the subscription several times over. Cash-flow forecasting takes the inbound purchase orders and the payout schedule and tells you the month you go tight. And the lifetime-value view answers a question most Amazon sellers cannot answer at all: whether a repeat-purchase product justifies losing money on the first sale.

The work nobody mentions in the first paragraph of a review

The accuracy is only as good as the costs you feed it, and feeding it is your job.

You enter cost of goods per product, and to be accurate you enter it per batch, because the units you landed in March cost something different from the ones on the water now. Add freight, duty, prep, packaging inserts. For a catalogue of fifteen SKUs this is an afternoon. For several hundred, with variations, it is a project — and reviewers consistently name COGS entry as the most tedious part of the tool.

It is worth doing properly rather than approximately. A blended cost guess produces a dashboard that looks precise and is quietly wrong, which is worse than no dashboard. Set aside the free month specifically for this, and reconcile one product against your own bookkeeping before trusting the rest.

The interface is the other adjustment. It is dense — lots of numbers, small type, little hand-holding about which of them matters today. Experienced operators tend to like that. People who wanted a clean summary screen often bounce off it.

Where it thins out

  • Beyond Amazon. Shopify, Walmart and eBay connections exist but are noticeably less developed than the Amazon core. If most of your revenue is off-Amazon, this is not the tool built for you.
  • Big catalogues. The per-batch cost model that makes it accurate is the same thing that makes it heavy at scale.
  • Analysis, not advice. It surfaces that margin fell. Working out why, and what to change, remains entirely human.
  • API access sits on a higher tier, which matters if you want the data in your own warehouse.

None of these are surprising at the price. They are simply the shape of the product: deep on one job, deliberately shallow elsewhere.

The hard boundary: it reports ads, it does not run them

This is the most important line in any sellerboard review and it is often blurred. There is a PPC dashboard. It shows spend against profit, and it will show you which products are underwater once advertising is counted properly — which is genuinely valuable and more than most ad tools show you.

There is no bid management. No campaign creation. No search-term harvesting or negation. Sellerboard is a profit analytics service, and calling it an ads platform is a category error that anyone using it spots in about four minutes.

So the practical position for most sellers is two subscriptions: sellerboard for the truth about margins, and something else for the advertising. That combination works fine. It just means the honest cost comparison is both lines together, plus the hours of whoever sits between the two screens deciding what to do.

Common questions

Is sellerboard accurate?

Yes, provided you enter costs properly. It pulls fees, refunds and ad spend automatically, but landed cost per batch is manual. Blended or estimated COGS produces a confident-looking dashboard that is quietly wrong, so reconcile one product against your books before trusting the rest.

Is sellerboard worth it?

For most FBA sellers, yes. Knowing true per-SKU profit changes decisions immediately, and the reimbursement recovery alone frequently exceeds the cost. The one-month trial with no card makes it a low-risk thing to test properly.

Can sellerboard manage my Amazon PPC?

No. It has a PPC dashboard for reporting profit after ad spend, but no bid management, campaign creation or search-term work. Treating it as an ads platform is the single most common mistake in write-ups about it.

What are sellerboard's biggest weaknesses?

The manual COGS workload on large catalogues, a data-dense interface with a real learning curve, thinner support for non-Amazon channels, and API access gated to a higher tier.

Do I still need an advertising tool alongside it?

Almost certainly. Sellerboard tells you which products lose money after ads; something else has to change the bids. Budget for both, or look at a suite that covers profit and advertising together.
