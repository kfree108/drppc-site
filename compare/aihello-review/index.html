AiHello review: the bidding is real, the documentation is not

AiHello sells AutoPilot, an AI bidding and campaign-creation layer for Amazon ads, with an optional managed team behind it. The software has genuine strengths. The company is unusually hard to research: one web page, no product page, and a blog that quotes a different price from the homepage.

The whole company is one page

Most reviews of AiHello read like reviews of a normal SaaS vendor. It is not one, and the shape of the website is the first thing a buyer should know.

We checked on 20 August 2026. aihello.com is a single page of roughly 6,600 words of visible text. The navigation items labelled Features and Pricing are anchor links that scroll you down that same page — #features and #pricing. There is no product page. There is no pricing page. Requesting /pricing, /product, or a URL we invented on the spot all return byte-for-byte the same homepage, because the site runs a catch-all.

Behind that sits a small blog. The visible posts are mostly competitor-alternative pages — Adtomic alternatives, Pacvue alternatives, Quartile alternatives, Teikametrics alternatives — plus one long listicle of Amazon PPC tools. That listicle is doing most of the heavy lifting in search for the whole domain.

None of this makes the software bad. It does mean the usual review method fails: there is no documentation to read, no feature matrix to check, no changelog, no security page. Everything you learn, you learn on a call or in the product. Weigh that against a vendor whose entire spec is public before you sign.

What the price actually is

The pricing block on the homepage lists, as printed:

  • Smart — "$199 /month/marketplace + 2% of Ad-spend". The page shows a dollar sign and does not name the currency anywhere near it.
  • Advanced features — "Advanced features like Repricing and Budgeting at only $10/SKU/month each". Note the word each: two modules on the same SKU is $20 a month for that SKU.
  • Expert-managed — custom, book a call.

Now read their own blog. In their listicle, AiHello lists AiHello as "Pro: $175/month for up to $5k monthly ad spend. Hybrid: $1200/month for full account management and up to $10k monthly ad spend. Agency: $1000/month for up to 10 accounts. Enterprise: custom for $200k+ monthly ad spend", summarised in the table as a range of $175–$1200. No mention of per-marketplace billing. No mention of the 2%.

Two different pricing models, both published by AiHello, on the same domain. We have no way to tell which the sales team will quote you. Get it in writing, and get the 2% defined — what spend it applies to, whether it is capped, and what happens when you add a marketplace.

The per-marketplace structure is the part to model carefully. If you sell in the US, Canada, the UK and Germany, that is four times the base fee before a single percentage point of spend is counted.

What the product does well

AutoPilot bidding. This is the reason people buy it. Bids are adjusted continuously toward a target, with each change logged so you can see what moved and why. Users consistently single out the intraday behaviour — pushing bids up in peak hours and down in dead ones. That is genuine dayparting, not a scheduler that pauses campaigns.

Bulk campaign creation. Building hundreds of campaigns across match types and targeting types is where the tool visibly earns a subscription. If you are launching a wide catalogue, this is the strongest single feature.

Keyword harvesting and negation. Automated, and competent.

A managed option that exists. They employ PPC specialists and will run the account for you. Plenty of software vendors say this and mean a support ticket. AiHello sells it as a tier.

Marketplace listing. The app is listed in the Amazon Selling Partner Appstore across several regions, which at least means the integration is a sanctioned one rather than a scraped connection.

Where it falls short

No custom rules. Their own comparison table records this as the con, and it is a fair one. If your account has an idiosyncrasy that needs a specific condition handled a specific way, you are asking the algorithm to infer it. Sellers who want deterministic control tend to prefer a rules engine like Scale Insights.

The AI is a bidder, not an analyst. It moves bids toward targets you set. It does not tell you the target is wrong for that product, or that the product should not be advertised at all this quarter.

That distinction has teeth. We pulled a live read from our own Orbit database for Walkize, a brand we work with and can name. The account showed $267,131 of ad spend against $601,614 of attributed sales — 2.25x, a 44.4% ACOS, which no bidder would flag as an emergency. Underneath it, $89,885 — 33.6% of all spend — had gone to search terms that produced zero orders, across 89,045 individual search terms, while the top 1% of terms drove 75.5% of the sales. Optimising bids on that structure improves the wrong thing efficiently.

Thin third-party evidence. Independent review volume is low compared with Helium 10, Perpetua or Teikametrics. The most quotable testimonial about AiHello that we could find in search is one AiHello published about itself.

Who it suits, and what we would do instead

AiHello is a good fit for a single-marketplace seller with a wide catalogue who wants hands-off bidding and dayparting without an enterprise contract, and who is comfortable buying from a small vendor on the strength of a trial rather than a document. The campaign-creation engine alone can justify the fee during a launch phase. If you want a human involved without hiring one, the managed tier is a reasonable middle.

Look elsewhere if you run several marketplaces — the per-marketplace base multiplies fast — or if you need auditable rules, or if procurement will ask for documentation that does not exist.

Common questions

How much does AiHello cost?

The homepage lists a Smart tier at $199 a month per marketplace plus 2% of ad spend, with Repricing and Budgeting at $10 per SKU per month each. Their own blog lists a completely different ladder starting at $175 a month. Ask for a written quote and confirm which applies.

Why does AiHello quote two different prices?

We do not know. The homepage block and the blog listicle were both published by AiHello and they do not agree — the blog version has no per-marketplace fee and no percentage of ad spend. Treat the homepage as the more current of the two and verify before signing.

Is AiHello legitimate?

Yes. The application is listed in the Amazon Selling Partner Appstore in multiple regions, which means the data connection is a sanctioned one. The concern is not legitimacy, it is how little the company publishes about what you are buying.

AiHello or a rules-based tool?

Depends on how much control you want. AiHello leans on its algorithm and does not offer custom rules. If you have specific conditions you need enforced exactly, a rules engine will frustrate you less. If you would rather not write rules at all, that is precisely what AiHello is selling.

Does AiHello work outside the US?

Yes, and that is the part to price carefully — the base fee is charged per marketplace. Four marketplaces means four base fees before the percentage of ad spend is applied. Model your actual marketplace count before comparing it with a flat-rate competitor.
