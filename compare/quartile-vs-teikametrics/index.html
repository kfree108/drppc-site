Two platforms solving differently shaped problems

These get compared because they land in the same searches, but they are not built to the same brief.

Quartile is built wide. Its site carries dedicated product pages for nine channels: Amazon Sponsored Ads, Amazon DSP, Walmart Connect, Instacart, Criteo, Google, Meta, Microsoft and TikTok. It sells to brands and agencies running paid media across retail media networks and the open web at once, in more than thirty countries, and it holds an ISO/IEC 27001 certification dated May 2026 — a detail nobody in the comparison results mentions and one that matters enormously if your procurement team runs a security review.

One fairness caveat we would want stated about us, so we will state it about them: a meaningful part of that retailer reach is mediated through Criteo rather than held as direct integrations, and Quartile publishes no single count of retail media networks. Any comparison page quoting you a big number has inferred it.

Teikametrics is built deep on fewer surfaces. Amazon, Walmart and TikTok Shop, and inside those it goes past advertising into listing edits informed by campaign performance, one-click listing porting between marketplaces, and unified inventory. It also puts Amazon Marketing Cloud audiences within reach without requiring SQL, which is a genuine strength given AMC normally assumes a data team.

Note the platform name if you are reading older reviews: Teikametrics rebuilt around a product it calls ARI, announced December 2025. The name Flywheel now survives mainly in legacy sign-in URLs. A review that calls the platform Flywheel was written before the rebrand.

The fee shapes differ more than the fees

Teikametrics publishes. On their own pricing page, read 20 August 2026, Essentials is listed at $179 a month on the monthly billing tab and $149 a month on the annual tab, covering accounts up to $10,000 a month in ad spend, with a free trial offered. The two tiers above it are custom-priced, and the page states an additional 3% of ad spend over $10,000. So: a listed price for small accounts, a quote plus a percentage for everyone else.

Quartile does not publish. There is no pricing page — the pricing URL resolves to the homepage and the sitemap carries no pricing route. The path is a demo request. We state that neutrally: they quote on a call, which is a choice a large share of this category makes, and it is a finding rather than an accusation.

What they do publish is better than a price card, and not one ranking comparison quotes it. Quartile's terms define their standard pricing tiers as being set by the client's aggregate monthly advertising spend, the number of advertising channels Quartile manages, and the level of service agreed. That is a three-variable tier, not a flat percentage of spend, and the distinction matters because plenty of articles describe it as the latter. The same terms add that fees may include, on top of a fixed monthly amount, a variable component based on media spend volume — so a percentage-shaped element can exist without the tier itself being a percentage.

If you are modelling this, the useful move is to price the same account at two channel counts. Because channels are one of the tier variables, adding a marketplace is a pricing event at Quartile in a way it is not at a spend-banded vendor.

The section nobody writes: what you are signing

We read Quartile's terms because no page ranking for this comparison had, and they carry the most decision-relevant facts available on the open web for this vendor. As published, and last updated in March 2026, they describe a one-year initial term with automatic renewal, sixty days' written notice to prevent renewal, fees that are non-cancellable and non-refundable, and early termination that leaves the remainder of the term payable.

None of that is unusual for enterprise software and none of it is a red flag. It is simply the shape of the commitment, and it should sit in the comparison alongside the feature grid, because it is the part you will actually live inside.

Teikametrics is month-to-month at the listed tier with a free trial, which is a genuinely lighter commitment. The counterweight is that reviewers report the percentage fee continuing after a cancellation request unless the Amazon account itself is disconnected, and describe that step as under-explained. Those are individual accounts rather than verified facts and we report them as such — but they point at exactly the right question.

Three asks worth making of any vendor here, ourselves included:

  • Written notice of any fee change, with a stated notice period.
  • The exact mechanics that stop billing — what must be disconnected, cancelled or emailed, and by when.
  • Change-of-control and data portability terms. This category consolidates constantly, and you want the export path agreed before it is relevant.

What the review data supports, and what it cannot

Both platforms have real review bases, which is more than several competitors can say.

Quartile carries a strong Capterra score across a review base in the mid-nineties. The praise clusters on the bidding automation and on a granular campaign architecture that isolates single ASINs and single keywords; the complaints cluster on complexity, a rigid interface with limited customisable views, and difficulty reaching implementation specialists. That is a coherent picture of a powerful platform that expects expertise.

Teikametrics is rated highly on G2 across a base of over a hundred reviews and noticeably lower on Trustpilot across a smaller one, where the distribution is bimodal rather than mediocre — lots of five stars and a hard cluster of one stars. When two sites disagree that sharply it is usually the sample doing the work: G2's base skews to product users, Trustpilot's skews to people motivated by a billing experience. Read both, and read the dates.

The dates are the real caution. The page ranking first for Teikametrics reviews was last substantially updated in 2023, which is before the ARI rebrand — it reviews a platform under a name the vendor has moved on from. And the comparison SERP for these two vendors specifically contains no independent editorial comparison at all: it is vendor-affiliated content, auto-generated directory listings and review-site widgets. That is not a criticism of either company. It is a warning about what you are reading.

The thing both platforms leave to you

Both of these are configuration platforms. They are excellent at executing a strategy and they assume you have one, expressed as targets, structures and rules. In most brands we meet, that assumption is where the money leaks.

Walkize is a client account we are cleared to name publicly. From December 2025 to August 2026 it spent $267,131 against $601,614 in attributed sales — 2.25x, a 44.4% ACOS. That headline reads like a tuning problem. It was not. $89,885 of the spend, 33.6% of the account's entire budget, went to search terms that produced zero orders, across 89,045 individual terms, while the top 1% of terms drove 75.5% of all sales.

A platform will execute against whatever structure you give it. It will not tell you that a third of your money is landing outside the part of the account that works, because nobody asked it that question. You can test the same thing in your own account this week with a search-term export and a sort by spend against zero orders.

Which one you should actually pick

Quartile suits brands and agencies running paid media across retail media, search and social at once, and its security posture makes enterprise procurement easier. Teikametrics suits Amazon-plus-Walmart sellers whose listings and inventory move with their ads, and it publishes an entry price. Dr. PPC suits Amazon-only brands where the strategy itself, not the execution tooling, is the gap.

Common questions

Which is cheaper, Quartile or Teikametrics?

Unanswerable from published information, and any page that answers it confidently is guessing. Teikametrics lists an entry tier for accounts under $10,000 a month in ad spend and quotes above it with a stated 3% of spend over that threshold. Quartile publishes no price at all and quotes on a call. Below the threshold Teikametrics is the only one of the two with a number.

Does Quartile charge a percentage of ad spend?

Not as its tier structure. Their published terms define standard pricing tiers by aggregate monthly ad spend, the number of channels managed and the level of service agreed. Separately, the terms allow a variable component based on media spend volume on top of a fixed monthly amount. So a spend-linked element can exist, but describing the whole model as a flat percentage of spend would be wrong.

Is Teikametrics still called Flywheel?

The platform is now presented as ARI, announced in December 2025. Flywheel persists mostly in legacy sign-in URLs. There has been no explicit retirement announcement for the old name, so the accurate statement is that the platform rebranded around ARI rather than that Flywheel was discontinued. Reviews still using the old name predate the change.

What contract terms should I check before signing either?

Initial term length and whether it auto-renews, the notice period to prevent renewal, whether fees are refundable, what happens on early termination, and precisely what action stops billing. Also ask for written notice of fee changes and for change-of-control and data export terms. Ask all of it of any vendor, including us.

Do I need either if I only sell on Amazon?

Quartile's main advantage is breadth across nine channels, so a single-marketplace seller pays for reach they will not use. Teikametrics is a better fit at that size, particularly if listings and inventory are entangled with your advertising. If the gap is that nobody in the building owns advertising strategy, neither platform closes it — that is a managed service question.
