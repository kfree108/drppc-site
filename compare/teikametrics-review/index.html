The short answer

Teikametrics is ad automation for Amazon and Walmart built around goals rather than raw rules. You pick an objective and it translates that into targets. Its clearest advantage is genuine Walmart support alongside Amazon. Its clearest limits are customisation for complex catalogues and multi-account reporting for agencies.

What you are actually buying

Teikametrics has been in this category longer than most of the names it competes with, and the current product is sold as a strategic suite spanning ads, catalogue, inventory and insights, with refunds recovery bundled in. The advertising half is the reason people arrive.

The design choice that defines it is goal-based control. Rather than handing you a rule builder and wishing you luck, it asks what you are trying to do with a product — grow it, milk it for profit, launch it — and converts that into bid targets and campaign structure. For a seller who knows their business but does not want to become a bid-management specialist, that framing is the product's real value, and reviewers say so consistently.

Pricing is unusually candid for this category: Essentials is listed at $179 a month billed monthly or $149 billed annually for accounts up to $10,000 in monthly ad spend, with Advanced and Enterprise quoted individually and described as plus 3% of ad spend over $10K. We have written about that structure separately. This page is about whether the software is any good.

The strongest reason to choose it: Walmart is not an afterthought

If you sell meaningfully on Walmart as well as Amazon, Teikametrics is a better buy than our software and we are happy to say so. Walmart support here is not a checkbox added late; it is built into the same workflows, with Walmart onsite display available on the higher tier. Most Amazon-native tools treat Walmart as a port, and it shows.

We are Amazon-first and Amazon-deep. That is a deliberate choice, not a gap we are embarrassed about, but it makes the recommendation easy: two real marketplaces, one team, one console — go and look at Teikametrics before you look at us.

Where reviewers keep hitting the same walls

Three complaints recur often enough to plan around.

  • Customisation for complex catalogues. The goal-based model that makes it approachable is the same thing that constrains it. Brands spanning several categories with different margin structures and different competitive dynamics report wanting levers the interface does not expose.
  • Agency and multi-account use. Isolated client reporting and the operational plumbing an agency needs are not the platform's strength. If you manage several unrelated accounts, test this hard before you commit.
  • The economics change at $10,000. Below that threshold you have a flat, predictable fee. Above it you are on a quoted base plus a variable that grows as you do. That is not a criticism of variable pricing — we charge a percentage ourselves — but it means the tier you qualify for today is not the tier you should be modelling.

Set against that, the interface is widely praised as clear, and sellers in the roughly $2,000 to $50,000 monthly ad spend range are where positive reports cluster most densely. That is the sweet spot.

What automated bidding decides, and what it never touches

Any platform in this class optimises inside a boundary drawn by decisions it does not make. Bids and placements are inside the boundary. Outside it: what your product costs to make and land, what you charge, whether you have stock to sell in three weeks, and whether the main image is doing its job. Those four decide most of the outcome, and no bidding algorithm has an opinion on them.

This is why raw efficiency numbers mislead. A brand can hold a respectable ACOS while its overall return quietly sits well below where the category should be. Walkize, a brand we manage and can name, came to us with $267,131 of Amazon ad spend against $601,614 in attributed sales — a 2.25x return, or 44.4% ACOS. Nothing in that account looked broken campaign by campaign. The problem was structural, and structure is not what a bid optimiser is built to see.

Which one you should actually pick

Teikametrics suits a seller between roughly $2,000 and $50,000 in monthly ad spend who wants goal-based automation without becoming a bid specialist, and it is the right call for anyone running Amazon and Walmart together. It suits agencies and complex multi-category catalogues less well. Dr. PPC suits brands who would rather the strategy and the weekly execution both belonged to someone else.

What to do with this

Judge this on the job you actually need done, not the feature list. Pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — on the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Then ask whether the thing you are about to buy closes that gap, or just shows it to you.

Common questions

Is Teikametrics good for beginners?

Yes, more than most. The goal-based workflow means you state an objective rather than write bidding rules, and the interface is regularly praised as clear. Sellers spending roughly $2,000 to $50,000 a month on ads are where the positive reports concentrate.

Does Teikametrics work for Walmart as well as Amazon?

Yes, and it is the single best reason to choose it. Walmart is handled as a first-class marketplace rather than a bolt-on, with Walmart onsite display available on the higher tier. If you run both marketplaces, this is a genuine advantage over Amazon-only tools including ours.

What are the most common Teikametrics complaints?

Limited customisation for brands spanning several categories with different margin profiles, weak isolated reporting for agencies running multiple client accounts, and the shift in economics once you cross $10,000 in monthly ad spend and move from a flat fee to a quoted base plus a percentage.

Is Teikametrics better than hiring someone to run ads?

It depends what breaks first. Software fixes the mechanics of bidding. It does not decide your price, protect you from a stockout, or rewrite a listing that is not converting. If your account problems are structural rather than mechanical, a platform of any brand will optimise steadily toward the wrong destination.
