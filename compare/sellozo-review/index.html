Sellozo review: a good visual campaign builder with a pricing trail gone cold

Sellozo is Amazon PPC automation, owned by the ecommerce accelerator Netrush since October 2021. Its Campaign Studio drag-and-drop builder is the most-praised part. Its weakness for a buyer today is verification: the company does not present readable pricing publicly, and third-party figures are years old and contradictory.

Who owns it, and why that matters to you

Netrush, a Portland-based ecommerce accelerator, acquired Sellozo on 28 October 2021. That is a matter of public record rather than rumour, and it changes how you should read the product.

Sellozo is no longer an independent software company whose only business is selling you a subscription. It sits inside a group that also sells services to brands. That can be a strength — service teams using their own tooling every day tend to keep it grounded — and it can be a distraction, if the roadmap starts serving the parent's accounts before it serves yours. Ask directly on the call: who is the product team now, how many people, and what shipped in the last two quarters.

Campaign Studio is the reason people like it

Strip away the marketing and the distinctive thing Sellozo built is a visual campaign builder. You lay campaigns out on a canvas, connect them, and see the structure of your account as a shape rather than as a spreadsheet with four hundred rows. Users describe it as one of the more intuitive interfaces in a category not famous for them.

That matters more than it sounds. Most Amazon accounts are a mess of structure inherited from whoever ran them two agencies ago, and the single hardest thing to hold in your head is how campaigns relate to each other — which one is harvesting, which one is defending, which one has been quietly duplicating another for a year. A tool that draws that picture earns its place.

Around it sit the expected pieces: automated bid adjustments, keyword and negative management, dayparting, and reporting. Reviewers on service-focused directories are generally warm, citing sales up and ACOS down, and support gets consistently decent marks.

The pricing you cannot verify, and what to do about it

Here is the honest problem with recommending Sellozo in 2026: we could not read a price.

Sellozo's own site did not present pricing to a plain page read. The third-party record does not help. One widely-cited review carries figures explicitly dated to May 2023 and describes a five-step ladder — a base platform subscription, then tiers adding monthly and weekly consultation, then managed ads, then a fully managed service. More recent roundups describe something quite different: a single flat monthly fee charged per marketplace, with no percentage of ad spend attached.

Those are not two versions of the same price list. They are two different products. We are not going to print either set of numbers, because publishing a stale price is how a buyer ends up budgeting for a plan that no longer exists. Get the current rate card in writing, confirm whether it is per marketplace or per account, confirm what is software and what is a person's time, and confirm the contract length.

The wider point for any tool in this category: the headline fee is the part everyone compares, and the variable rate is the part that decides the bill. At $100,000 a month in ad spend, a single undisclosed percentage point is $12,000 a year. Whichever way a vendor prices, make them say the whole formula out loud.

Flat fee versus percentage, done honestly

Sellozo has historically positioned on a flat fee, and for a high-spending seller that positioning is genuinely attractive: your software cost stops moving while your spend grows. We charge a percentage and we are not going to pretend that is costless — at scale a flat fee is cheaper on paper, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling.

What the paper comparison misses is what the fee buys. A flat software fee buys you a tool that waits for you to open it. Nobody is reviewing your search terms on the Tuesday you spend in a warehouse. If your bottleneck is a person's attention rather than a licence, the cheaper line item is the more expensive decision, and the reverse is equally true if you have the attention and just need better instruments.

Work out which one you are before you compare prices. It is the only comparison that actually decides anything.

Which one you should actually pick

Sellozo suits a hands-on seller with real spend who wants a clear visual builder and a fee that stays put as they grow — Campaign Studio is a genuine strength. It suits nobody who needs the work done rather than the interface improved, and its unverifiable public pricing means you must get terms in writing. Common questions

How much does Sellozo cost?

We could not verify a current figure and will not repeat an old one. A 2023 review describes a ladder from a base subscription up to a fully managed service; newer roundups describe a single flat fee per marketplace. Ask for the current rate card in writing and confirm whether it is charged per marketplace or per account.

Is Sellozo still actively developed?

It is still marketed and sold. It has been part of Netrush since October 2021, and the independent review and pricing trail around it has thinned since then. That is not proof of neglect, but it is a reason to ask what shipped recently and to ask for references from customers onboarded in the last year.

What is Campaign Studio?

A visual, drag-and-drop interface for building and connecting campaigns. It is the feature users single out most often, and it is genuinely useful for seeing account structure as a diagram instead of a long list — particularly on accounts that have been through several owners.

Does Sellozo charge a percentage of ad spend?

Its historical positioning is a flat monthly fee without a spend percentage, which is a real advantage for high-spending sellers. Confirm it applies to the plan you are quoted, and confirm whether consultation or managed-service time is inside that fee or billed separately.

Who is Sellozo best for?

A seller with meaningful ad spend who wants to hold the controls, values a visual campaign builder, and prefers a fee that does not move as spend grows. It is a weaker fit for anyone who needs the weekly execution done for them rather than made easier.
