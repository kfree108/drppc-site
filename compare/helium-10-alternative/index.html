Reason one: the 2026 price rise

This is the most common reason and the easiest to solve. Helium 10 raised prices in 2026 and retired its cheap entry plan. Platinum is now $129/month, or $99/month billed annually. Diamond is $359/month, or $279 annually. Enterprise starts at $1,499/month on annual billing.

If you are paying Diamond money and using three tools, the fix is not a different suite — it is a smaller one. SmartScout, SellerSprite, ZonGuru and Jungle Scout all sit well below Helium 10's entry tier. Jungle Scout's Starter runs $348 for the year on annual billing; the others publish comparable numbers. We are deliberately not quoting the rest from memory, because prices in this category move and the roundups repeating them do not update. Open the vendor's own pricing page.

The honest caveat: you will lose Cerebro. Reverse-ASIN keyword research is the single thing Helium 10 does better than everyone, and cheaper suites approximate it rather than match it. Some people leave, miss it, and come back. Know that going in.

Reason two: you bought it to fix advertising and it did not

This is the reason people rarely say out loud, and it is not really a Helium 10 problem. Its ads module is competent rules-based automation with dayparting and historical data behind it. It also carries a 2% managed ad spend fee on Diamond, on top of the subscription — check the current thresholds with them, because their help docs and pricing page describe it differently.

The gap is that rules are not decisions. A rule cuts a bid when ACOS passes a number you chose. It has no view on whether that number should be different for a 60% margin SKU than a 20% one, or that a product is three weeks from a stockout, or that the launch on Tuesday needs air cover.

Dedicated bid platforms do that piece better. Ad Badger charges a flat monthly fee banded by ad spend — $275 up to $5,000 of monthly spend, $440 up to $25,000, $660 up to $75,000, rising to $1,830 up to $1.5M — and is genuinely good at daily bids and search term harvesting. Quartile goes wider, covering Amazon, Walmart, Instacart and DTC channels, though it publishes no pricing at all. Both are still tools you point.

Reason three: you need account analytics, not research tools

Here is what every alternatives roundup misses. They compare research suites to research suites, because that is what Helium 10 is famous for. But a large share of people searching this term already have products selling. They do not need to find another one. They need to know which ASIN is actually profitable after fees, which search terms are burning budget, what inventory is about to go short, and where the buy box went.

Orbit is built for that: sales and advertising analytics, search terms, campaign profitability, keyword and traffic tracking, inventory, finance, ASIN-level profitability, plus BSR, buy box, price and fee trackers and the Dr PPC Console. It is a fair like-for-like against a Helium 10 subscription — software you read and act on — and it comes at no additional charge with Dr. PPC.

What Orbit will not do is find you a product to launch. If that is your week, Helium 10 is still the better purchase and we would tell you so.

When leaving is the wrong move

Stay if you use Cerebro or Magnet weekly. Stay if the AI Listing Builder is doing work you would otherwise pay a copywriter for. Stay if you sell across Amazon and Walmart and want one index. In those cases Diamond at $279 on annual billing is defensible value and switching to save $200 a month will cost you more in lost time than it saves.

Leave if you are paying for thirty tools to use four, or if you bought a suite hoping it would do a job that no suite does.

Which one you should actually pick

Leaving to save money points at a smaller research suite. Leaving because ads are not being run points at a bid platform or a managed service, not at another dashboard. If you use Cerebro or the listing builder weekly, stay — nothing matches them. Orbit is the like-for-like on account analytics; Dr. PPC is the tier that does the work.

What to do with this

Before you switch, write down the one number the switch has to change. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. A tool that reports that number and a service that removes it are different purchases; pick the one you have hours for.

Common questions

What is the cheapest Helium 10 alternative?

Smaller research suites like SmartScout, SellerSprite and ZonGuru all sit below Helium 10's entry tier, and Keepa's free tier covers price and rank history. Verify current figures on each vendor's own pricing page — this category's roundups quote prices that are months or years out of date.

Is there a Helium 10 alternative that actually manages ads?

Bid platforms like Ad Badger execute your instructions well. A managed service makes the decisions too. Dr. PPC is $300 a month plus 3% of ad spend, capped, month-to-month with the first 30 days free, and every change carries evidence, a measurement plan and a rollback trigger before it runs.

Will I lose keyword research if I leave Helium 10?

Some of it, yes. Reverse-ASIN research through Cerebro is the strongest thing in the suite and alternatives approximate rather than match it. If you touch it weekly, the honest advice is to stay. If you touched it twice last quarter, you are paying a lot for a tool you are not using.

Plenty of brands do, and it is a sensible split. Helium 10 for product and keyword research, Dr. PPC for running the advertising, with Orbit included for account analytics. The two overlap far less than the feature tables suggest, because one is a research tool and the other is an operator.
