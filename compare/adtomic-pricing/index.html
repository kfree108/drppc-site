What Helium 10 publishes today

We read helium10.com/pricing on 20 August 2026. Three plans are listed, in US dollars:

  • Platinum — $129 a month billed monthly, $99 a month billed yearly. 20 ASINs lifetime.
  • Diamond — $359 a month billed monthly, $279 a month billed yearly. 1,000 ASINs lifetime.
  • Enterprise — from $1,499 a month, billed annually. Up to 5,000 ASINs.

The advertising tooling lives on Diamond. Platinum gets a look at the dashboard and the analytics screens and nothing more. Next to the feature row labelled Managed spend fee the page prints one line: "Pay for managed ad spend. 2%". The FAQ underneath says the same thing in a sentence — Diamond customers pay a 2% management fee on PPC spend run through Helium 10 Ads.

One detail is worth noticing before anything else. On that pricing page, the word Adtomic appears zero times. The phrase Helium 10 Ads appears eight. Adtomic is not a company and has not been a product name for a while; it is the former name of Helium 10's advertising module. If a page is still selling you "Adtomic pricing", check when it was written.

The $199 add-on, the Starter plan, and the $10,000 threshold

Search the keyword and you will be told, confidently, that Adtomic costs $199 a month on top of your subscription, that a 2% fee kicks in above $10,000 of monthly ad spend, and that you can pair it with a $39 Starter plan. The article ranking near the top of that search says exactly this, illustrates it with a worked example, and carries no publication date anywhere on the page.

None of it matches the vendor. There is no Starter plan on the pricing page. There is no $199. There is no $10,000 threshold — the 2% is printed flat, with no floor stated. And the two URLs those articles point at have both moved on:

  • helium10.com/adtomic-pricing/ returns a 404. The dedicated Adtomic pricing page is gone.
  • helium10.com/tools/seller/adtomic/ no longer resolves to a product page at all. It redirects three times and lands on a blog post about Helium 10 Ads.

This is the ordinary condition of this category rather than a scandal. Vendors rename modules, fold add-ons into tiers, and quietly retire threshold pricing. Affiliate roundups do not get re-read. The practical rule: treat every price you see in an article about Amazon software, including this one, as a claim with a date on it, and open the vendor's own page before you budget against it.

The 2% is the line that decides the bill

Sellers compare the subscription and skip the percentage, which is backwards. The subscription is fixed and knowable. The percentage grows with the thing you are trying to grow.

  • At $10,000 a month of managed spend, 2% is $200 a month — $2,400 a year.
  • At $50,000, it is $1,000 a month — $12,000 a year.
  • At $100,000, it is $2,000 a month — $24,000 a year, against a $3,348 annual subscription.

Past roughly $12,000 of monthly spend the variable line is bigger than the fixed one, and it keeps going. That is not an argument against charging on spend — we charge on spend too, and so do Teikametrics and AiHello and several others. It is an argument for reading the variable rate first and the headline second, and for asking whether it is capped. Helium 10's page does not mention a cap. Ours is capped; ask any vendor the same question and make them answer it in writing.

The second question worth asking: what does the fee buy? On Diamond the 2% buys software you still operate. The rules are yours to write, the bids are yours to approve, and the hours are yours to spend.

When the Helium 10 bill is the right bill

Buy Diamond if the job is research. Cerebro and Magnet are genuinely good, the keyword and listing side is the mature half of the platform, and $279 a month billed yearly is a fair price for it. Treat the ads module as a bonus you may or may not use, and treat the 2% as a fee you only start paying when you route spend through it.

Do not buy Diamond to fix advertising. If you do, you are paying a subscription, a fee on spend, and your own evenings — and the third one is the expensive line. Sellers reach that conclusion about eight months in, usually after a quarter where ACOS moved and nobody could say which rule moved it.

If your real problem turns out to be that campaigns keep going dark because units run out, no bidding tool of any price will fix it — that is an inventory problem, and Dr. Stock is the one to look at instead.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. On the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Whatever you buy — a seat, a service, or nothing — that number is the one it has to move, and a cheaper tool nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

Is Adtomic still called Adtomic?

No. It is Helium 10 Ads. The old product URL redirects to a blog post about Helium 10 Ads, and the word Adtomic does not appear on the Helium 10 pricing page at all. Articles still using the old name are usually running on old pricing too.

Can I buy Adtomic on its own?

Not as a separate $199 line item any more. The advertising features are part of the Diamond plan. Platinum users get restricted access to the dashboard and analytics screens only. Check Helium 10's current pricing page before you plan around any of this.

Does the 2% fee only apply over $10,000 of ad spend?

Several ranking articles say so. The pricing page does not — it lists the managed spend fee as a flat 2% with no threshold named. Ask Helium 10 to confirm in writing what the fee applies to before you route spend through the tool.

How does 2% compare with Dr. PPC's 3%?

Ours is a point higher and capped, and it includes the work. Dr. PPC is $300 a month plus 3% of ad spend, month-to-month, first 30 days free, with Orbit thrown in. Helium 10's 2% buys a tool you operate yourself. Compare what the fee buys, not just the rate.

What should I put in a budget for the first year?

Three lines, not one: the subscription, the fee on spend at your realistic run rate, and the hours somebody on your team will spend writing and babysitting rules. The third line is the one most spreadsheets omit and the one most sellers end up paying twice.
