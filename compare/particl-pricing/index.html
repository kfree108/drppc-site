Particl pricing: what the plan costs, and what the team costs

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1948 words · Written against what currently ranked for “particl pricing”
The short answer

Particl publishes three named plans on its own pricing page plus a custom tier, all billed monthly, with a 14-day free trial and a stated 40% discount for paying yearly. The plan price is a base fee: seats beyond the included ones are published as separate monthly add-ons, and that is where most published comparisons go wrong.

What Particl publishes, read on 20 August 2026

Particl is one of the vendors in this category that does publish, and it deserves credit for that. On its pricing page today, on the monthly billing view that loads by default:

  • Starter — $250 a month. One user included, three months of historical data, a capped number of views per user.
  • Essential — $500 a month. Three users included, six months of history, unlimited views.
  • Growth — $1,000 a month. Three users included, twelve months of history, unlimited views.
  • Custom — quoted. Banded by competitor count, history depth and user count, with bulk export, API access and a dedicated account manager.

A 14-day free trial is offered on the published plans. The page also carries an annual option advertised as 40% off, and this is the first place to be careful: the annual figures are not printed. The discount is stated, the resulting monthly rate is computed in the browser rather than published as text. That means any article quoting you a Particl annual price has calculated it, not read it. Ask for the annual number in writing.

One more precision point: the page prints bare dollar signs and does not state a currency code anywhere we could find. For a US company selling to US retail teams that almost certainly means US dollars, but if you are buying from outside the US, confirm it on the order form rather than assuming.

The seat add-ons are the line that changes the answer

This is the part worth the page. Particl publishes per-seat pricing above the included users, and the published add-on rates rise with the plan: a Starter seat, an Essential seat and a Growth seat are all priced differently, at $40, $60 and $80 a month respectively, with a capped number of extra seats per plan.

Now run the arithmetic that the roundups do not. Starter includes exactly one user. A merchandising lead, a category manager, an analyst and a buyer is four people. On Starter that is the $250 base plus three additional seats at $40, which is $370 a month, not $250 — and Starter also caps how many extra seats you may add, so a larger team is pushed up a plan rather than sideways into more seats.

None of this is hidden. It is published on the vendor's own page. It is simply absent from nearly every third-party comparison, which quotes the plan price as though it were the bill. The general rule is worth adopting for any vendor in this category, ourselves included: get the number with your actual headcount, your actual marketplaces and your actual channel count on it, then compare.

Why the figures you find elsewhere disagree so violently

Search this term and you will meet three separate sources of wrong numbers.

An unrelated cryptocurrency shares the name. Most of the highest-ranking results for the pricing query are token trackers and exchanges for a coin called Particl, not the retail intelligence company. A price scraped from those pages is a coin quote. The company's own pricing page ranks well below them.

The company used to be called Luz. It rebranded in November 2022, and data marketplaces and company databases still carry the profile under the old name. Some third-party listings therefore price a dataset licence rather than the SaaS product, which is why you will see figures in the tens of thousands attached to the same brand.

Some reviews simply predate the current page. We found third-party write-ups quoting plan bands that match nothing on the live pricing page.

The fix is boring and reliable. Open the vendor's own pricing page, note which billing tab is selected, and write down the date you read it. We do this on every page we publish, and it is the single practice that has caught the most errors in our own work.

A flat software fee and a spend-linked fee are not comparable by size

Buyers routinely line up a market intelligence subscription against an advertising fee and pick the smaller number. That comparison is meaningless, because the two are priced against different things and bought for different reasons.

A flat software fee is predictable and does not move when your business does. That is a genuine advantage: at $1,000 a month, a Growth plan costs the same whether you had a good quarter or a bad one. The corresponding disadvantage is that it also does not shrink when you do, and it charges you nothing for being wrong.

A fee tied to advertising spend moves with the account. That is how a large part of this category prices — Helium 10 charges 2% of ad spend managed through its ads product on its Diamond plan, and SellerApp's published agency structure is a base fee plus a band of 0.5% to 2.5% of ad spend. Our own Dr. PPC is $300 a month plus 3% of ad spend. We are not going to argue against a model we use ourselves. What we will argue for is that any percentage should be capped and printed, so the number is knowable at every spend level rather than only at the one on the quote.

The useful discipline: decide what each fee is buying. Intelligence spending buys better decisions about what to sell. Advertising fees buy execution on what you already sell. Compare inside a category, never across one.

What the subscription does not buy, and what that costs

Particl does not manage advertising. Nothing on its pricing page or product pages describes bids, campaigns, placements, search terms or negatives. That is a category boundary rather than a gap, but it matters when you are deciding where the next thousand dollars a month should go.

Here is the comparison we would actually run. Across the 47 brands we manage, in Amazon search data from 1 May 2026 onward, 48.5% of all search spend — $4,962,963 of $10,243,379 — went to search terms that produced zero orders, while 0.9% of 891,585 terms produced 80% of the sales.

Fixing a leak of that shape is worth more than any subscription in this article, and it needs account data, not market data. If your advertising has never been read at search-term level, that is where the money is, and it is a cheaper problem to solve than a category strategy problem.

To be fair in the other direction: if you are choosing what to launch next quarter, competitor assortment and price movement genuinely is the better input, and no amount of search-term analysis will tell you what your category is about to want.

Which one you should actually pick

Particl is fairly priced for what it is and publishes more than most of this category, which is worth something. Buy it if cross-retailer assortment and price movement drives your merchandising decisions, and budget the seats, not just the plan. If your problem is advertising performance rather than category strategy, no plan here will help, and Orbit or Dr. PPC is the honest comparison instead.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. Across the book above that runs at 48.5% of everything spent. Whatever you buy — a seat, a service, or nothing — that number is the one it has to move, and a cheaper tool nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

What are Particl's published plan prices?

On their own pricing page, read on 20 August 2026 with the monthly view selected, the named plans are Starter at $250 a month, Essential at $500 and Growth at $1,000, plus a quoted custom tier. Each includes a set number of users and a set depth of historical data, and a 14-day free trial is offered.

Does Particl charge per user?

Partly. Each plan includes users — one on the entry plan, three on the next two — and additional seats are published as monthly add-ons that get more expensive as the plan gets larger, with a cap on how many you can add. The correct way to describe the model is a flat monthly base fee plus per-seat overage, which is why the headline plan price is rarely the bill.

How much is Particl if I pay annually?

Their page advertises 40% off for paying yearly but does not print the resulting figures — the number is computed in the browser rather than published. Any annual price you find in a comparison article has been calculated by the author. Ask the vendor for it in writing, and note which billing tab a quoted figure came from, on any vendor including us.

Why do some sites list completely different Particl prices?

Three reasons, all avoidable. An unrelated cryptocurrency shares the name and dominates the pricing search results. The company was previously called Luz and some database listings still price a dataset licence under that profile. And some reviews simply predate the current page. Check the vendor's own page and record the date you read it.

Is a market intelligence subscription better value than paying for ad management?

They buy different things and are not comparable by price alone. Intelligence improves decisions about what to sell; ad management improves execution on what you already sell. If you have never read your own search-term report, that is usually the cheaper win — across the 47 brands we manage, nearly half of all search spend lands on terms that produce no orders at all.
