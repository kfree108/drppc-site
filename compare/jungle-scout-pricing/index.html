Jungle Scout pricing: the numbers we could verify, and the ones nobody can

Jungle Scout sells two products: Catalyst, self-serve for sellers under $1M a year, and Cobalt, quote-only above it. Its own pricing page does not render tier prices in the page HTML, and the third-party figures in circulation contradict each other. Check the checkout total yourself before you budget.

What is verifiable on Jungle Scout's own page

Read Jungle Scout's pricing page as plain HTML and four things are unambiguous.

  • Two products, not one. Catalyst is the self-serve product, positioned for sellers between $0 and $1M in annual revenue. Cobalt is the enterprise product for $1M+, and it is demo-request only. No price is published for Cobalt anywhere.
  • Catalyst has three named tiers — Starter, Growth Accelerator and Brand Owner — plus a custom option above them.
  • Extra seats are priced openly: $49 per month, or $459 per year, per additional user.
  • Annual billing is discounted, described on the page as "Save up to $360 on annual plans".

The terms are clear too: no free trial, a 7-day money-back window on standard Catalyst plans, and cancellation with no obligation beyond your current billing cycle. That last one matters more than people give it credit for.

What is not plainly readable is the one thing you came here for — what Starter, Growth Accelerator and Brand Owner actually cost each month.

Why every page quoting tier prices disagrees with the next

We checked, repeatedly. Reading Jungle Scout's pricing four separate times in a single day returned four different sets of figures. The prices are assembled in JavaScript and the offer appears to be tested, so what lands on your screen can depend on when you look and where you arrived from.

Now look at the pages ranking for this query. One roundup lists Brand Owner at $149 a month and adds, to its credit, that "some sources cite a substantially higher Brand Owner + CI price, as high as $399/month". Others print $29, $49, $79 and $129 in various arrangements. They cannot all be right. None of them carries a date on which the figure was verified.

There is a tidy explanation for part of the mess. Three numbers are printed plainly on Jungle Scout's page — $49, $459 and $360 — and not one of them is a tier price. They are the monthly seat fee, the annual seat fee, and the maximum annual saving. Lift them into a comparison table without reading the labels and you have manufactured a price list that never existed.

So we are not going to print tier prices here. A wrong number on a page like this becomes a wrong budget line in somebody's spreadsheet. We would rather be the page that tells you to go and look.

How to price Jungle Scout properly, in about five minutes

Do this yourself rather than trusting anyone's table, ours included.

  • Open the pricing page logged out, in a private window. Screenshot it with the date visible.
  • Carry the plan you want all the way to checkout. The charged total, including tax, is the only number that is binding.
  • Read the renewal rate, not the promotional rate. Introductory pricing on annual plans is common in this category and the second year is the real cost.
  • Count seats honestly. At $49 a month each, three people on the account changes the arithmetic considerably.
  • If a salesperson mentions Cobalt, get the annual figure and the contract length in writing before the demo ends.

If the price you are quoted differs from what a comparison page told you, the comparison page is wrong. That includes recently published ones.

Common questions

Does Jungle Scout publish its prices?

Partly. It publishes plan names, the $49/month or $459/year additional-seat fee, and an annual saving of up to $360. The monthly cost of Starter, Growth Accelerator and Brand Owner is assembled in JavaScript rather than written in the page, and Cobalt carries no published price at all.

Why do different sites quote different Jungle Scout prices?

Because the page is dynamic and appears to be tested. We read it four times on the same day and got four different sets of figures. Roundups also copy each other, and some have mistaken the seat fee and the annual saving for tier prices. Treat every quoted figure, including any you find here, as unverified until you see it at checkout.

Is annual billing cheaper than monthly?

Yes, and Jungle Scout says so on its own page — up to $360 saved on annual plans. Before committing, confirm the renewal rate for year two rather than the introductory rate for year one, because they are frequently different numbers.

What is Jungle Scout Cobalt and what does it cost?

Cobalt is the enterprise product, aimed at businesses above $1M in annual revenue, sold through a demo request. No price is published. Expect an annual contract and expect the figure to move with the number of brands, categories and users you need.

Is there a free trial?

No. Jungle Scout offers a 7-day money-back window on standard Catalyst plans instead, which means you pay first and claim afterwards. Diarise the deadline the day you sign up.
