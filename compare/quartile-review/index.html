Quartile review: the case for it, and the case against

Quartile is a retail media optimization platform that automates bidding across Amazon, Walmart, Instacart, Google and Meta. It is strong on breadth and hourly bidding, weaker on profit-based decisions and negative keywords. It does not publish pricing — every quote comes through a demo.

What Quartile actually is

Quartile calls itself the world's largest retail media optimization platform. Underneath the phrase, it is software plus a service layer that runs paid campaigns across a lot of surfaces at once: Amazon Sponsored Ads and DSP, Walmart, Instacart, Criteo inventory such as Target and Best Buy, and then Google, Meta and Microsoft on the direct-to-consumer side.

Quartile publishes some scale figures on its site — $2B+ in annual retail ad spend, $25B+ in annual managed sales, 5,300+ customers across more than 32 countries. It also publishes average performance gains for brands that partner with it: +41% ROAS, +38% revenue, +33% conversion rate, +18% orders, +11% impressions. Those are the company's own averages, presented as averages, which is more transparent than most vendors manage.

Technically, the interesting parts are the Amazon Marketing Stream integration driving hourly bidding, Amazon Marketing Cloud data used to build interest-based personas, and a heavy lean on single-keyword campaigns as the unit of control.

Where Quartile genuinely wins

Breadth is the real argument for it, and it is a good argument. If you are a brand selling on Amazon, Walmart and Instacart while also running Google Shopping and Meta, having one platform and one team looking across all of it is worth something no Amazon-only tool can offer. Most competitors in this space stop at Amazon and Walmart.

Hourly bidding off Amazon Marketing Stream is the right architecture. Daily bid cycles miss intraday swings, and Stream is how you see them. Building on it rather than on next-day reports is a real technical choice, not marketing.

The single-keyword campaign approach is divisive but defensible. It gives you clean, isolated control over what each term costs and where it shows, at the price of an enormous campaign count that no human is going to hand-manage — which is rather the point of buying automation.

Reviews on aggregate sites also land consistently on one strength: the people. Support and account management come up repeatedly as better than expected for a platform product.

The criticisms that keep recurring

The most detailed published hands-on review we found ran Quartile for five months and went back to managing campaigns manually. Their specific complaints are worth repeating because they are structural, not gripes about a bad week.

  • Negative keywords were not added automatically, which left a manual job in a platform bought to remove manual jobs.
  • Account-level targeting by default pushed spend toward products that were not the most profitable ones.
  • No easy way to set a different target ACOS per keyword, so everything got pulled toward one number.
  • COGS could be uploaded but did not drive optimisation. The platform accepted profit data and then optimised on ad metrics anyway.

That last one is the important one, and it generalises well beyond Quartile. A system that optimises to ROAS or ACOS without margin in the loop will confidently push budget at your worst-margin SKU because it converts. On the interface, aggregate reviewers are split — the analytics are praised, the day-to-day UI less so.

The pricing you cannot look up

Quartile does not publish pricing anywhere on its site. Every number is quoted after a demo.

That is a legitimate choice for a platform with a service layer, but it has a consequence worth knowing about before you start the process. Third-party articles reporting Quartile's pricing disagree wildly with each other — one cites a flat $1,495 a month for up to $35,000 of ad spend, another describes a range from roughly $895 to $9,995 depending on channel and spend, others mention a minimum monthly ad spend around $3,000 and per-extra-marketplace charges. We could not verify any of those against Quartile, so we are not going to repeat them as fact. Ask Quartile for current pricing and get the fee structure, the minimum spend and the contract length in writing.

One thing to raise on that call: whether the quote is a flat fee, a percentage of spend, or both, and what happens to it in your peak season.

Which one you should actually pick

Quartile suits multi-marketplace brands who want Amazon, Walmart, Instacart and DTC channels run from one platform, and who are comfortable buying without a published price. It suits you less if you sell mainly on Amazon and your problem is margin-aware decisions on a specific catalogue. For that, Orbit covers the analytics and Dr. PPC covers the operating, at a price you can read before the call.

Common questions

How much does Quartile cost?

Quartile does not publish pricing. Third-party figures circulating online disagree with each other by an order of magnitude, so none of them should be planned around. Request a quote and ask specifically for the fee structure, any minimum monthly ad spend, charges per additional marketplace, and the contract term.

Is Quartile good for Amazon PPC specifically?

It is capable — hourly bidding on Amazon Marketing Stream and Amazon Marketing Cloud personas are the right foundations. The recurring reservation from users is that optimisation runs on ad metrics rather than product margin, and that some negative keyword work stays manual. Worth testing against your own worst-margin ASIN.

Who is Quartile clearly the better choice for?

Brands running serious budget across several retail media networks at once — Amazon plus Walmart plus Instacart plus Criteo inventory — who want one platform and one team across all of it. If that describes you, an Amazon-only tool or an Amazon-only service is solving a smaller problem than the one you have.

How is Dr. PPC different from Quartile?

Price is published and the strategy is per product rather than per account. Dr. PPC is $300 a month plus 3% of ad spend, capped, month-to-month, with the first 30 days free and Orbit included. Every change carries evidence, a measurement plan and a rollback trigger, and you choose how much runs without a human click.

Does Quartile handle Amazon DSP?

Yes — Quartile lists Amazon DSP alongside Sponsored Ads, and uses Amazon Marketing Cloud data to build interest-based personas. If DSP is a meaningful share of your budget, get specifics on the call about how DSP and Sponsored Ads decisions are coordinated rather than optimised in isolation.
