The page you were looking for does not exist

There is no Quartile pricing page. The site is well built and says a great deal about capability, and nothing at all about cost. The button is Request Demo, everywhere.

That is a choice, not a gap. Quartile sells a spend-tiered service across many channels, and any single published figure would misrepresent most buyers. Fine. But it means a marketing director cannot put a rough number in a budget line without booking a call and disclosing their ad spend, which is a real cost in time and in negotiating position.

So the useful thing this page can do is tell you what shapes the quote before you take the call.

Know what you are being quoted for

Quartile is broader than an Amazon PPC tool, and the breadth is most of the price. Its own site describes coverage across Amazon Sponsored Ads and Amazon DSP, Walmart, Instacart and Criteo — which brings Target and Best Buy — alongside Google Ads, Meta Ads and Microsoft Ads. Quartile claims more than 5,300 customers across 32 countries and over $2B in annual retail ad spend on the platform.

The positioning is retail media optimisation across the whole mix, with expert strategy layered on the software. So a Quartile quote is rarely a pure software quote. It usually bundles some amount of managed service, and the ratio between the two is negotiable and rarely itemised unless you ask.

If you only sell on Amazon, note that you are being priced by a company whose core argument is multi-channel. That is not automatically bad, but it should shape what you push back on.

The figures in circulation, and how much weight to give them

Third-party sites report several things. Treat each with the confidence it has earned.

  • A percentage of ad spend, commonly described in the 2–5% range, tiering down as spend grows. Widely and consistently reported. Plausible as a structure, unconfirmed as a rate.
  • A spend-tiered monthly fee running from several hundred to several thousand dollars per channel. Reported figures vary enough between sources that we would not budget from any single one.
  • An ad-spend minimum in the low thousands per month. Frequently mentioned. Worth asking about directly, because it decides whether you are a customer at all.
  • A performance component on top of the base. Mentioned by several sources. If true, it is the term you most need in writing, because "performance" needs a definition and an attribution window.

We could not verify any of these against Quartile. That is the honest state of the public record, and anyone presenting a precise Quartile price list as fact is guessing with confidence.

The multiplier that catches people out

The most useful thing in the third-party reporting is not the headline rate. It is that additional accounts and additional marketplaces are commonly described as separate charges — an added monthly amount for each one you connect.

That structure is easy to underestimate. A brand that sells on Amazon US, Amazon Canada, Amazon UK and Walmart is four connections, not one, and the quote you were given for "Amazon" was for one of them. Add Google and Meta and the shape of the bill changes again.

Before the call, write down every account and marketplace you would want connected in the next twelve months, including the ones you are only considering. Ask for the quote against that full list, not against today's footprint. If the model is per-connection, you want to see the whole curve while you still have leverage.

Where Quartile is genuinely the right answer

If your media really is spread across Amazon, Walmart, Instacart, Criteo retailers, Google, Meta and Microsoft, and you want one system optimising across all of it with a strategist attached, Quartile is built for exactly that and there are not many credible alternatives. The cross-channel optimisation argument only works if you actually run cross-channel, but when you do, it is a strong one.

It also suits brands that want a hybrid — software plus people — without hiring an agency separately from a platform. That bundling is a legitimate reason to accept a quote-based price, because you are buying an arrangement rather than a SKU.

It suits you worse if Amazon is 90% of your revenue, if you need a defensible budget number before a procurement conversation, or if a performance-based component would be hard to audit on your side.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. On the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Whatever you buy — a seat, a service, or nothing — that number is the one it has to move, and a cheaper tool nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

How much does Quartile cost?

Quartile does not publish pricing. Third-party sources describe a spend-tiered monthly fee, a percentage of ad spend commonly cited in the 2–5% range, and in some accounts a performance component. None of these are confirmed by Quartile, and reported figures differ substantially between sources.

Does Quartile charge extra for additional marketplaces?

Several third-party sources describe an added monthly charge for each additional account or marketplace connected. Ask for a quote covering every marketplace you expect to run in the next twelve months rather than just your current footprint.

Is there a minimum ad spend for Quartile?

A minimum in the low thousands per month is frequently mentioned by third parties. Ask directly on the first call — it determines whether you are a viable customer before you invest time in an evaluation.

Why won't Quartile publish a price?

Because the offer bundles software with managed strategy across many channels, and the cost genuinely varies by spend, channel count and service level. That is a reasonable position for the vendor. It is still an obstacle for a buyer trying to build a budget.
