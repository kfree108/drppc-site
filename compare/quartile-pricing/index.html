Its terms of service are the only place it describes how fees work: pricing tiers set by your aggregate monthly ad spend, the number of channels it manages for you, and the service level agreed. The page you were looking for does not exist

There is no Quartile pricing page. Requesting one lands you back on the home page. The site is well built and says a great deal about capability, and nothing at all about cost. The button is Request Demo, everywhere.

That is a choice, not a gap, and a defensible one: Quartile sells a tiered service across many channels, so any single published figure would misrepresent most buyers. But it means a marketing director cannot put a rough number in a budget line without booking a call and disclosing their ad spend, which is a real cost in time and in negotiating position.

So the useful thing this page can do is tell you what shapes the quote before you take the call — and, unusually for a vendor that publishes no prices, Quartile has written that down.

What Quartile's own terms say about how the fee is set

The terms of service on Quartile's site are the only pricing text the company publishes, and they are more informative than the marketing pages. They define a set of standard pricing tiers keyed to your level of usage, and they name what that takes into account, collectively:

  • your aggregate monthly advertising spend in the channels Quartile manages;
  • the number of advertising platforms — channels — that Quartile manages on your behalf;
  • and/or the level of service agreed between you.

The actual figures, the terms say, are the ones set out in your order form. No amount and no rate appears anywhere in the document.

Read that carefully, because it is easy to translate into something it does not say. A fee that is keyed to your spend level is not the same thing as a percentage of your spend. A tier is a step: you sit in a band, and the fee for that band is the fee, whether you spend at the bottom of it or the top. A percentage is a rate that moves with every dollar. The two behave very differently at the edges — a tier can make a small increase in budget expensive if it moves you up a step, and can make a large one cheap if it does not. Quartile does not publish which shape applies to your number, and a good deal of third-party writing has quietly filled that gap with a percentage range. Nothing Quartile publishes supports it. Ask them, and get the answer in the order form rather than in an email.

Channel count is the multiplier people underestimate

The most useful thing in that definition is not the spend input, which everyone expects. It is that the number of channels Quartile manages is named as its own factor.

That structure is easy to underestimate. A brand selling on Amazon US, Amazon Canada, Amazon UK and Walmart is several connections, not one, and the figure you were given for "Amazon" may have been for a narrower footprint than the one you are planning. Add Google and Meta and the shape of the bill changes again.

Before the call, write down every account, marketplace and platform you would want managed in the next twelve months, including the ones you are only considering. Ask for the quote against that full list, not against today's footprint, and ask what happens to the tier when you add one mid-term. If channel count is a pricing input, you want to see the whole curve while you still have leverage.

Five things to force into the order form

Because the amounts live in the order form rather than on the site, the order form is the document that matters. Get these settled inside it:

  • The shape of the fee. A tier keyed to spend, a rate applied to spend, a fixed platform charge, or a blend — and which spend it is measured against.
  • The tier boundaries, and what happens when a peak month pushes you over one. Specifically: does it come back down in January, and how quickly.
  • The channel count the quote assumes, and the price of adding one.
  • The service level the quote assumes, since the terms name it as an input — what is included, and what becomes a separate line.
  • The minimum and the term. Whether there is a spend floor to be a customer at all, how long the commitment runs, and the renewal uplift.

None of these are unreasonable asks. A vendor pricing on three named variables should be able to state all three of yours.

Where Quartile is genuinely the right answer

If your media really is spread across Amazon, Walmart, Instacart, Criteo retailers, Google, Meta and Microsoft, and you want one system optimising across all of it with a strategist attached, Quartile is built for exactly that and there are not many credible alternatives. Its own site describes coverage across Amazon Sponsored Ads and Amazon DSP, Walmart, Instacart and Criteo — which brings retailers such as Target and Best Buy — alongside Google Ads, Meta Ads and Microsoft Ads, with more than 5,300 customers across 32 countries and over $2B in annual retail ad spend on the platform. The cross-channel argument only works if you actually run cross-channel, but when you do, it is a strong one.

It also suits brands that want a hybrid — software plus people — without hiring an agency separately from a platform. That bundling is a legitimate reason to accept a quote-based price, because you are buying an arrangement rather than a SKU.

It suits you worse if Amazon is 90% of your revenue, if you need a defensible budget number before a procurement conversation, or if you cannot get the fee mechanics stated plainly enough to model.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. On the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Whatever you buy — a seat, a service, or nothing — that number is the one it has to move, and a cheaper tool nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

How much does Quartile cost?

Quartile publishes no amount. Its terms of service describe pricing tiers set by your aggregate monthly ad spend, the number of channels it manages for you, and the service level agreed, with the figures themselves recorded in your order form. Comparison sites that print a specific Quartile price or rate are not quoting anything the company publishes.

Does Quartile charge a percentage of ad spend?

Not that they state. Their terms describe fees keyed to your level of spend rather than a rate applied to it, and no percentage appears anywhere in the document — the ranges quoted in third-party articles are not sourced to Quartile. It is a fair question to ask on the call, and the answer belongs in the order form.

Does Quartile charge more for additional marketplaces?

Channel count is one of the three factors their terms name, so it is reasonable to expect the quote to move with it. Ask for pricing covering every marketplace and platform you expect to run in the next twelve months rather than just your current footprint.

Is there a minimum ad spend for Quartile?

Nothing is published either way. Ask directly on the first call — it determines whether you are a viable customer before you invest time in an evaluation, and it is the cheapest question in the conversation to get answered.

Why won't Quartile publish a price?

Because the offer bundles software with managed strategy across many channels, and by their own account the cost varies by spend level, channel count and service level. That is a reasonable position for the vendor. It is still an obstacle for a buyer trying to build a budget, which is why the terms of service are worth reading first.
