They are not selling the same thing

Feature grids for these two produce a near-tie, which is a good sign that the grid is measuring the wrong thing. The companies are shaped differently.

Helium 10 is a research suite with advertising attached. Its centre of gravity is keyword and product discovery, and its reverse-ASIN work is the reference standard that other tools benchmark against — we say that as competitors, because it is true. It also carries genuine breadth beyond Amazon into Walmart and TikTok Shop, ships a data connector for AI tools, and bundles substantial education that is otherwise sold separately.

SellerApp is a seller platform with services attached. Its strongest software work is keyword discovery and listing quality scoring, it covers multiple marketplaces, and — the part almost no comparison mentions — it also sells managed advertising and publishes an agency rate. It has a permanent free plan requiring no card, which is rarer than it sounds.

So the honest first question is not which is better. It is whether you are buying research, software, or somebody's time. All three are on offer between these two companies, at very different prices.

The three facts every comparison leaves out

We read what currently ranks for this query. These three points were absent from all of it, and each of them can change the answer.

1. Helium 10's advertising sits on the Diamond plan. Their pricing FAQ states that Helium 10 Ads is available in Diamond plans. Several ranking comparisons imply PPC automation is reachable from the entry tier. It is not, so the real entry cost for running ads on Helium 10 is the Diamond price, not the Platinum one.

2. There is a 2% management fee on managed ad spend. Their pricing FAQ states plainly that Diamond plan customers incur a 2% management fee on PPC spend managed through Helium 10 Ads. It sits in the FAQ rather than on the plan card and is not asterisked against the plan price, so a buyer reading only the pricing table will not encounter it. We searched their page for a spend threshold on that fee and found none published — do not assume one exists.

3. SellerApp's service ladder is invisible in these comparisons. Their managed advertising plan starts at $750 a month, listing work starts at $200 per parent ASIN, and their agency rate is published as a starting $300 plus a band of 0.5% to 2.5% of Amazon ad spend. That is the entire services half of the company, absent from every head-to-head we found.

None of this is a criticism of either vendor. Both publish these facts on their own sites. It is a criticism of how the comparison gets written.

The numbers, with the billing tab named

Read from each vendor's own pricing page on 20 August 2026. The billing basis is stated because that is where most published figures go wrong.

Helium 10. Platinum is $129 a month on the pay-monthly tab and $99 a month on the pay-yearly tab. Diamond is $359 a month on pay-monthly and $279 a month on pay-yearly. Enterprise starts at $1,499 a month billed annually. A free plan exists with limited access. There is no Starter plan and no lower entry tier — any comparison quoting a much cheaper Helium 10 entry price is quoting a plan that is not on the page.

SellerApp. Freemium is $0 with no card. Pro starts with $99 a month. The Smart plan with automation is $250 a month, displayed against $149 with a footnote stating the discount covers the first three months. Custom is quoted. There is no monthly-versus-annual toggle on their page — every figure is monthly — and while their FAQ describes a 50% annual saving, no annual figures are published. The plan names sometimes quoted elsewhere are no longer on the page.

The reusable discipline, which we apply to our own page too: check which billing tab you are reading, name it when you quote a figure, and record the date. Two of the four prices above differ by more than 25% depending on which tab is selected.

Where the answer flips

Now the arithmetic, which is the part worth your attention.

At modest ad spend, the subscriptions dominate the comparison and the 2% is a rounding error. Helium 10 Diamond on the annual tab is the more expensive subscription, and if research is a real part of your week it is comfortably worth the difference.

At serious ad spend, the percentage becomes the price. At $50,000 a month in managed ad spend, 2% is $1,000 a month, which is roughly four times the Diamond subscription itself. The subscription comparison that every article runs is measuring the smaller number.

This is not an argument against percentage pricing — we charge one. It is an argument for two things. Cap it, so that a growing budget does not silently become a growing fee with no ceiling. And print it, so a buyer can model the year without a call. Where competitors are ahead of us on transparency we will say so; on this specific point we think a printed, capped rate is the better practice, and it is what we publish.

The question neither subscription answers

Both platforms give you better instruments. Neither gives you the hours or the judgement to use them, and that is usually the binding constraint.

Walkize is a client account we are cleared to name publicly. From December 2025 to August 2026 it spent $267,131 against $601,614 in attributed sales — 2.25x at a 44.4% ACOS, across 30,921 unique search terms and 244,510 clicks at a $1.09 blended cost per click. The finding underneath: $89,885, or 33.6% of every dollar spent, went to search terms that produced zero orders, spread across 89,045 individual terms, while the top 1% of terms drove 75.5% of all sales.

Both tools on this page can show you a search-term report. Neither will sit down and work through a tail with eighty-nine thousand entries in it, and neither will tell you that your account's problem is concentration rather than efficiency. That is a reading job, and whether you buy it as software or as a service is the actual decision on this page.

You can run the test yourself before you buy anything: export your search-term report, filter to spend with zero orders, and see what share of your budget is sitting there.

Which one you should actually pick

Helium 10 suits research-led sellers who want one suite across discovery, listings and ads, and its keyword work is the best here. SellerApp suits sellers who want a genuine free tier, multi-marketplace coverage, or a flat managed fee. Dr. PPC suits brands where nobody has the hours and the account has outgrown what a dashboard can fix.

What to do with this

Neither of these decides your ACoS on its own — how much of the work gets done each week does. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. On the account above it was 33.6%. Pick the option that leaves someone actually working that list, whether that is you or us.

Common questions

Is Helium 10 or SellerApp better for PPC?

SellerApp puts ads automation on its paid tiers and also sells managed advertising, so the on-ramp is cheaper. Helium 10 gates advertising to its Diamond plan and adds a 2% fee on PPC spend managed through the ads product. If PPC is the whole reason you are buying, price both including the percentage before deciding — at real spend it changes the ranking.

Does Helium 10 charge a fee on ad spend?

Yes. Their pricing FAQ states that Diamond plan customers incur a 2% management fee on PPC spend managed through Helium 10 Ads. It appears in the FAQ rather than on the plan card, and we found no published threshold on their page. Add it to any comparison, because at meaningful spend it is larger than the subscription.

Which plans do these vendors actually sell today?

Helium 10 lists Platinum, Diamond and Enterprise plus a free plan; there is no Starter tier on the page. SellerApp lists Freemium, Pro, Smart with automation, and Custom. Several comparison articles still quote retired plan names and lower entry prices for both, which is worth checking against each vendor's own page before you budget.

Is SellerApp's free plan actually useful?

For research, yes — product intelligence and an advertising overview with no card, and their FAQ describes it as permanent rather than a trial. It is not enough to run anything: campaign manager, listing quality, PPC insights and ads automation sit on the paid tiers. Treat it as a genuine way to test the data quality before spending.

Should I buy either of these, or hire the work out?

Buy a platform if someone in the building owns advertising and wants better instruments. Buy the work if nobody has the hours or the strategy is the gap. The comparison to run is service against service — SellerApp's managed plan against a managed product — and software against software, where Orbit is the like-for-like rather than Dr. PPC.
