SellerApp review: strong keyword work, pricing worth reading twice

The short answer

SellerApp is an Amazon seller platform with a real free tier, paid plans from $99 a month, and an agency arm selling managed services. Its keyword and listing tools are its strongest work. The main frustrations are a steep learning curve and a promotional headline price that expires after three months.

Three businesses under one name

Most SellerApp reviews treat it as a single tool and end up muddled. It is clearer to see it as three things sold together, because which one you are buying changes the verdict completely.

  • A free research tool. Product intelligence and an advertising overview, no credit card. Not a trial — a permanent free plan.
  • A paid SaaS platform. Insights, keyword tracking, listing quality, campaign manager and ads automation as you climb the tiers.
  • An agency. Listing optimisation, and managed advertising under their AMS plans.

The free tier is the part most worth knowing about. For a seller doing early product research, having real search and revenue-estimate data at $0 is a genuine service, and few competitors offer anything comparable without a card on file.

What it does well

Keyword and listing work. This is the consistent theme across independent reviews and it matches what the product is built around. Keyword discovery pulls a wide set of suggestions quickly, and the listing quality scoring gives you a concrete list of fixes rather than a vague grade. If your bottleneck is finding terms and tightening copy, this is the strong part of the platform.

Breadth beyond Amazon. It covers Walmart and other marketplaces, which matters if you are genuinely multi-channel and would rather not run three subscriptions.

The upgrade path is honest about what it is. The higher tier bundles a dedicated account manager and quarterly business reviews. That is a service being sold as a service, not automation pretending to replace one, and it is refreshingly clear about the difference.

Public ratings back this up — the platform sits around 4.5 out of 5 on the main review sites, with the praise clustering on data quality and the reporting views.

What frustrates people

The learning curve. This comes up in nearly every independent review. There is a lot of surface area, and the platform does not do much to tell you which three numbers matter this week. Expect to spend real time before it earns its cost.

The free plan is a taster. Campaign manager, listing quality, PPC insights and ads automation are all excluded from it. Useful for research, not for running anything.

Cost at the top. Reviewers regularly flag the paid tiers as a barrier for smaller sellers, which is fair once you compare $99 a month against tools doing one job well for $19.

Stale numbers everywhere else. Several well-ranked reviews still quote plans at $39 and $49 that no longer appear on SellerApp's pricing page. If you are budgeting from a comparison article, check the vendor page — this category rewrites its pricing faster than the reviews get updated.

The pricing, with the asterisk read out loud

From SellerApp's own pricing page, checked 20 August 2026:

  • Freemium — $0, no card. Product intelligence and advertising overview only.
  • Pro — from $99/month. Adds insights, keyword tracking, listing quality, email support.
  • Smart with Automation — advertised from $149/month, and this is the asterisk: their page notes the discounted rate applies to the first three months, against a regular $250. Budget for $250.
  • Custom — quoted. Data warehousing, priority support, custom development.

Annual billing is advertised at around 50% off, with no minimum contract length. On the managed side, listing optimisation starts at $200 per parent ASIN and the AMS Growth plan starts at $750 a month.

One detail worth noting fairly: SellerApp's agency pricing is listed as starting at $300 plus 0.5% to 2.5% of Amazon ad spend. That is the same shape as our own pricing, and we think it is the right shape — a base fee plus a share of spend keeps the vendor's incentive attached to the account. We are not going to criticise a model we use ourselves.

Which one you should actually pick

SellerApp suits multi-marketplace sellers and anyone doing serious keyword and listing work, and its free tier is a real gift to people starting out. It suits sellers who want to be told what to do less well — the platform hands you data and options. Dr. PPC suits brands who would rather buy the decision than the dashboard.

What to do with this

Judge this on the job you actually need done, not the feature list. Pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — on the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Then ask whether the thing you are about to buy closes that gap, or just shows it to you.

Common questions

Is SellerApp free?

There is a permanent free plan with no credit card, covering product intelligence and an advertising overview. Campaign manager, listing quality, PPC insights and ads automation are not included in it. Paid plans start at $99 a month.

How much does SellerApp cost?

Freemium at $0, Pro from $99/month, and Smart with Automation advertised from $149/month — though their page notes that rate covers the first three months against a regular $250. Custom plans are quoted. Annual billing is advertised at around 50% off.

Is SellerApp good for PPC management?

It gives you automation to configure and reporting to read, plus an account manager on the higher tier. It does not decide your strategy. If you want the ACOS targets and the structure decided against your actual margins, that is a different kind of product.

How accurate is SellerApp's data?

Independent reviews are generally positive on data quality, particularly for keyword volumes and listing scoring. As with any estimate-based tool, treat revenue and search-volume figures as directional and reconcile against your own reports before acting on them.
