The real Ad Badger price list

Straight from Ad Badger's own pricing page, read 20 August 2026. Six tiers, each capped by how much you spend on ads per month. The annual column is what you pay for the year, not per month.

  • Starter — up to $5,000 monthly ad spend: $275/month, or $2,550/year.
  • Basic — up to $25,000: $440/month, or $4,080/year.
  • Professional — up to $75,000: $660/month, or $6,120/year.
  • Platinum — up to $225,000: $920/month, or $8,500/year.
  • Ruby — up to $750,000: $1,375/month, or $12,750/year.
  • Emerald — up to $1,500,000: $1,830/month, or $17,000/year.

Every tier carries the same toolset, which is unusual and to their credit — nothing is held back for the expensive plans. You get daily bid updates on revenue-per-click bidding against a target ACOS, automated search term harvesting, negative keyword automation, hourly performance data, profit tracking that accounts for returns and Amazon fees, organic rank monitoring, a dayparting scheduler and multi-level filtering. The bands buy capacity, not features.

Paying annually works out around 23% cheaper across every tier. Ad Badger does not publish trial length, setup fees or contract terms on the pricing page — several third-party sites state a 14-day trial, but we could not confirm that from Ad Badger themselves, so ask them directly.

How the bands actually price your account

Flat bands behave very differently from a percentage, and the difference decides whether this is cheap or expensive for you.

Run the numbers as an effective rate. Starter at the top of its band — $275 against $5,000 of spend — is 5.5%. The same $275 against $1,500 of spend is over 18%. Basic at the top of its band, $440 against $25,000, is 1.8%. Professional at $75,000 is under 1%. Emerald at $1.5M is about 0.12%.

So the model rewards scale hard, and punishes two situations. One: spending well under a band's ceiling, where you are paying for headroom you never use. Two: crossing a band line by a little. Going from $25,000 to $26,000 of monthly spend moves you from $440 to $660 — a 50% price rise for 4% more spend.

The practical read: if your spend sits comfortably near the top of a band and stays there, Ad Badger is inexpensive per dollar managed. If your spend swings seasonally across a boundary, model the peak month, not the average one.

Where the internet gets Ad Badger pricing wrong

Search this term and you will find confident articles saying Ad Badger charges about 3.5% of ad spend, with better rates negotiable near 2%. Ad Badger's own pricing page shows flat monthly tiers.

We are flagging it because this failure mode is everywhere in Amazon software content, and it is worth naming rather than quietly repeating. Numbers get copied from one roundup into the next, nobody re-checks the vendor, and a figure survives for years after the vendor changed it. We have seen vendors misquote their own pricing in their own blog posts.

The only defence is boring: open the vendor's pricing page yourself before you sign anything, including when the source is us. Prices on this page were read on 20 August 2026 and vendors change them without notice.

What the subscription still leaves on your desk

Ad Badger is good at a specific, narrow job: moving bids daily against a target ACOS, and pulling converting search terms out of the noise. That job matters and doing it by hand is miserable.

It is still a tool you point. Someone decides the target ACOS, and that target should differ by product because margin differs by product. Someone decides campaign structure, when to launch a new ASIN, what to do when a competitor's price drops, and whether to keep pushing a keyword that converts beautifully into a SKU with four weeks of inventory cover. A bid engine optimising toward ACOS will happily spend into a stockout, because ACOS does not know what inventory is.

That is not a flaw in Ad Badger. It is the boundary of what any bid tool does.

Which one you should actually pick

Ad Badger suits a seller with steady spend near the top of a band who wants bids and search terms handled and is happy to steer the rest. It is genuinely cheap per dollar managed at scale. It suits you badly if spend swings across band lines, or if what you actually wanted was somebody to make the decisions rather than execute yours.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. On the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Whatever you buy — a seat, a service, or nothing — that number is the one it has to move, and a cheaper tool nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

Does Ad Badger charge a percentage of ad spend?

No. Their own pricing page shows six flat monthly tiers banded by monthly ad spend, from $275 to $1,830. Several third-party articles describe a percentage model of around 3.5%. Those articles disagree with the vendor's own page, so treat the vendor's page as the source of truth.

Is the annual plan worth it?

Annual billing lands about 23% below twelve monthly payments at every tier — $4,080 versus $5,280 on Basic, for example. The catch is band risk: if your spend grows past the ceiling mid-year you will need to move up anyway. Buy annual when your spend is stable, monthly when it is climbing.

Does Ad Badger offer a free trial?

Ad Badger does not state trial terms on its pricing page or homepage. Third-party sites report 14 days with no card required, but we could not verify that from Ad Badger, so ask them before assuming it. For comparison, Dr. PPC's first 30 days are free and it is month-to-month.
