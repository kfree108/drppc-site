First, the category error most reviews of this make

Particl turns up on lists next to bid-management platforms and PPC tools, and that comparison does not work in either direction. In its own words, Particl "combines product revenue, pricing, promotions, and assortment data across 20,000+ retailers so ecommerce teams can spot best sellers, emerging demand, and market gaps". It claims 600M+ unique SKUs, 40B transactions observed and $500B+ of revenue monitored.

That is a market-intelligence product. It tells you what is selling in your category and what your competitors are doing about price, promotion and assortment. It does not place a bid, write a negative keyword, or touch your advertising console. Reviewing it against advertising software is like reviewing a market research subscription against a sales team.

So the question a Particl review should answer is not "is it better than a PPC tool". It is:

  • What exactly do the published tiers gate, and where is the wall you will hit?
  • Is the data good enough for the decisions you would make with it — and how would you check that before paying?
  • What does it change about how you actually operate?

Public review sites are of limited help here. G2, Capterra, TrustRadius and their peers block automated reading, and quoting a star rating out of a search snippet is how comparison pages end up contradicting the live listing. We would rather give you a method for evaluating it yourself than a number we cannot stand behind, so that is what the middle of this page is.

The published tiers, and exactly what each one gates

Particl publishes its rate card, which is worth crediting — a good part of the intelligence category does not. Read from their own pricing page on 20 August 2026, all figures in USD, billed monthly with a 14-day free trial:

  • Starter — $250/month. 1 included user with 200 views per user, up to 2 additional users at $40/month each, 3 months of historical data, 5 tracked competitors, 1,000 export credits.
  • Essential — $500/month. 3 included users with unlimited views, up to 5 additional users at $60/month each, 6 months of history, 10 tracked competitors, 5,000 export credits.
  • Growth — $1,000/month. 3 included users with unlimited views, up to 10 additional users at $80/month each, 12 months of history, 10 tracked competitors, 10,000 export credits.
  • Custom — quoted. 12 to 48 months of history, 1 to 1,000 competitors, unlimited users, up to a million export credits, with API access and data licensing available.

Annual payment is advertised at a 40% discount across the plans.

Notice what the ladder is actually made of. It is not features — every tier is the same product. It is four meters: competitors tracked, months of history, seats, and export credits. That is a clean and honest way to price a data product, and it makes the buying decision far more tractable than a feature grid would. It also means you can work out the right tier arithmetically instead of by intuition, which is the next section.

Growth costs twice Essential and tracks the same number of competitors

Here is the finding we have not seen in any published review, and it is sitting in plain sight on the pricing page.

Essential tracks 10 competitors. Growth tracks 10 competitors. The step from $500 to $1,000 a month buys six extra months of history, five thousand more export credits, and headroom for more paid seats. It buys no additional coverage at all.

Expressed per tracked competitor, the ladder does something unusual:

  • Starter — $250 for 5 competitors is $50 per competitor per month.
  • Essential — $500 for 10 is $50 per competitor.
  • Growth — $1,000 for 10 is $100 per competitor.

The rate is flat across the first two rungs and then doubles. That is not a criticism — Particl is plainly pricing Growth on history depth and export volume rather than on coverage, and for a team building models that need a year of seasonality it is a rational package. But it does mean the tier choice should be made on a very specific question: do you need twelve months of history, or do you need more competitors? If it is the latter, no published tier answers it and you are into a Custom conversation, where the competitor count runs to 1,000.

There is a second limit worth flagging on the entry tier. Starter includes 200 views per user against 5 tracked competitors — 40 views per competitor per month, or roughly one and a third a day. That is a sampling allowance, not a monitoring allowance. If your intended use is watching a competitor's pricing move week by week, Starter is likely to run short and the honest starting tier is Essential, where views are unlimited.

How to review the data yourself in fourteen days

The only Particl review that should decide your purchase is the one you run. Any data vendor's claims about coverage are unverifiable from outside, and the trial exists precisely so you can check them from inside. Here is the protocol we would use, and it works for any market-data tool.

  1. Point it at yourself first. Your own brand is the one dataset whose truth you already hold. Pull Particl's revenue, price and assortment picture for your own products and compare it against Seller Central. You are not looking for exactness — no third-party estimate is exact — you are looking for whether the direction and the ratios are right.
  2. Then check a competitor you have ground truth on. Most category managers know at least one rival's approximate scale from a distributor, a broker or a former colleague. Two independent checks beat one.
  3. Test a decision, not a dashboard. Take a real question — should we hold price through Q4, is this sub-category growing, did that competitor's promotion actually move volume — and see whether the tool answers it in under ten minutes. A tool that requires an analyst to reconstruct the answer is a different purchase from one that hands it over.
  4. Check history depth against your seasonality. If your category swings hard in Q4, three months of history on Starter will be describing a season you are not in.
  5. Count your export credits against your actual workflow. If your team's habit is to pull data into a spreadsheet, credits are the meter that will bind first.

Run those five and you will know more than any review can tell you, including this one. And apply the same discipline to reviews you read elsewhere: check the publication date against the vendor's current product page before you weigh it. Software in this category moves faster than the articles written about it, and that is true of us as much as anyone.

What it changes, and what it cannot reach

Where a tool like this earns its fee is in decisions made before any advertising exists: what to launch, what to price at, which sub-category has demand nobody is serving, and whether a competitor's move is a promotion or a permanent reposition. Those are expensive decisions made badly with anecdote, and the data is genuinely hard to assemble any other way.

What it cannot reach is inside your own advertising account, and the gap is larger than most people assume. Take a client that has cleared us to publish its numbers. Walkize ran $267,131 of ad spend against $601,614 of attributed sales between December 2025 and August 2026, across 30,921 unique search terms. Within that, the top 1% of search terms drove 75.5% of all sales.

That concentration is the single most actionable fact about the account, and no market-intelligence platform on earth can see it, because it lives in a search term report that belongs to the seller. Conversely, nothing in that report tells you what the competitor two rows down is doing with price off Amazon. The two datasets are genuinely complementary and neither substitutes for the other — which is why treating Particl as an alternative to advertising software gets the shortlist wrong before it starts.

The other honest limit of any observed-transaction dataset is that estimates are estimates. Coverage varies by category, by retailer and by how much of a market sits in channels that are not observable at all. That is not a knock on Particl specifically; it is the physics of the category, and it is exactly why the fourteen-day self-test above matters more than any star rating.

Read the contract before the feature list

For a data subscription the commercial terms deserve a closer read than usual, because what you are buying is access to something you do not own and cannot take with you by default.

Particl's published position is buyer-friendly on the two things that matter most at trial stage: fourteen days free, and monthly billing on the standard tiers, with the 40% reduction attached to annual payment. So the questions worth putting to them, and to any data vendor, are these:

  • What happens to exports on cancellation? Export credits you have paid for and not used are a real asset. Ask whether they expire and whether a final export is permitted after notice.
  • Is historical access retrospective or forward-only? Twelve months of history on the Growth tier means twelve months looking back from today — confirm whether that window travels with you or is rebuilt if you resubscribe.
  • Does the competitor list lock? If you can only change tracked competitors quarterly, five slots on Starter is a tighter constraint than it appears.
  • Written notice of fee changes, from any vendor including us.
  • Annual commitment terms. A 40% discount for paying yearly is real value and it is still a twelve-month obligation.

To show how much heavier terms can be elsewhere in this market, Quartile publishes its contract openly: a one-year initial term renewing automatically for successive one-year periods, written notice of non-renewal required at least sixty days before expiry, fees due in advance and described as non-cancellable and non-refundable, and in-account termination available only during an initial sixty-day evaluation window. Publishing it is to their credit. The reason to read it is arithmetic rather than alarm — an annual term with a sixty-day notice requirement means the window in which you can decide to leave is a fraction of the year, and it is worth putting in a calendar on day one.

Common questions

How much does Particl cost?

Read from Particl's own pricing page on 20 August 2026, in USD and billed monthly: Starter $250, Essential $500, Growth $1,000, and a Custom tier that is quoted. Annual payment is advertised at a 40% discount. All standard tiers include a 14-day free trial, and additional users cost $40, $60 or $80 a month depending on the tier you are on.

Which Particl plan should I choose?

Decide on four meters rather than on features, because the product itself is the same at every tier: how many competitors you need to track, how far back you need history to go, how many people need logins, and how much you will export. Note that Essential and Growth both track 10 competitors, so the step to Growth buys history depth and export volume, not coverage. Above 10 competitors you are into a Custom quote.

Is Particl accurate?

Any observed-transaction dataset produces estimates, and accuracy varies by category and retailer, so the only answer worth having is one you generate. Use the 14-day trial to pull Particl's picture of your own brand and compare it against Seller Central, then repeat with a competitor whose scale you already know from another source. You are checking direction and ratios rather than exact figures — that is what these datasets are for.

Is Particl an alternative to Amazon PPC software?

No, and treating it as one will lead to a disappointing purchase. Particl is competitive and market intelligence across retailers — revenue, pricing, promotions and assortment. It does not place bids, manage campaigns or touch your advertising console. The two categories complement each other: market data tells you what to launch and how to price it, and advertising software or a managed service decides what happens inside your campaigns.
