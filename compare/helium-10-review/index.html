Helium 10 review: which half of it you will actually use

Helium 10 is still the deepest Amazon seller toolset — research, keywords and listings especially. The 2026 price rise to $129/month Platinum and $359 Diamond narrowed who should pay. It is excellent software you operate, not an ad manager that operates for you.

The short version, and what it costs

Helium 10 bundles more than thirty tools across product research, keyword research, listing optimisation, analytics and advertising. Almost nobody uses thirty. Most sellers live in four or five, and whether the subscription is worth it comes down to which four or five those are for you.

Pricing on their site as of 20 August 2026: Platinum is $129/month, or $99/month billed annually. Diamond is $359/month, or $279/month billed annually. Enterprise starts at $1,499/month on annual billing. There is a limited free tier that is essentially Chrome extension access. The pricing page advertises no trial and no money-back window.

The 2026 increase is the backdrop to every review you will read this year. The cheap entry plan is gone and Platinum became the floor, which changed the calculus for pre-launch sellers more than for anyone else.

The tools that earn their keep

Keyword research is the strongest thing here and the honest reason to buy. Cerebro and Magnet against a competitor's ASIN will tell you more in twenty minutes than a week of guessing, and the reverse-ASIN workflow has become the default way most operators build a keyword set — ours included.

Listing work is the second. The 2026 AI Listing Builder ties keyword intelligence to generation: titles, bullets, description, section rewrites, brand voice options and a compliance pass. It is genuinely good, and it is one of the few AI features in this category that saves real hours rather than producing something you rewrite anyway.

Product research is solid, if slightly behind on raw estimate accuracy. One widely quoted third-party test put Helium 10's estimates at 79.3% accurate against Jungle Scout's 84%. Treat that as directional rather than definitive — every such test uses a different sample — but the broad finding, that Jungle Scout edges it on sales estimates while Helium 10 wins on breadth, matches what operators report.

Refund recovery and inventory tools on Diamond quietly pay for themselves in a lot of accounts, and get mentioned far less than they deserve.

The tools you will stop opening

Breadth has a cost that no feature table shows. A suite of thirty tools means a lot of them are thin, and finding out which is a job you do with your own money.

The email follow-up allowance, the TikTok Shop conversion tracking on a three-SKU cap, several of the alert and analyzer modules — these exist, they work, and most sellers open them twice. They are not bad; they are simply not the reason anyone renews. If you are evaluating Platinum versus Diamond, work out which specific tools you will touch weekly and price the gap against those alone. The other twenty-five will not tip it.

The caps are worth reading closely too. Platinum's 20 tracked ASINs and 100 monthly keyword searches sound generous until you are two weeks into a real listing project.

The ads module, reviewed on its own terms

This is where our own bias is relevant, so we will be explicit about it. We manage Amazon advertising for a living, and we still think Helium 10's ads tooling is a reasonable rules-based automation layer. Bid rules, dayparting, two years of historical ad data, campaign build-out — for a seller who knows what they want done and wants it done repeatedly, that is useful.

Two things to price in. First, Diamond carries a 2% managed ad spend fee on spend run through the ads module, on top of the subscription. That is a fair way to price the thing and we charge a percentage too, but it belongs in your budget and most reviews omit it. Confirm the current thresholds with Helium 10 directly, because their help documentation describes them differently from the pricing page.

Second, rules are not strategy. A rule lowers a bid when ACOS crosses a line. It does not know your margin differs by SKU, that a product has four weeks of inventory cover, or that the bundle you are pushing cannibalises the parent listing. Somebody has to hold that, and the tool does not.

Which one you should actually pick

Buy Helium 10 if research, keywords and listings are weekly work — nothing else covers that ground as well, and Diamond is fair value for it. Skip it if you have no product yet, or if you bought it hoping advertising would take care of itself. For the ads half, Orbit gives you the analytics and Dr. PPC does the operating.

Common questions

Is Helium 10 still worth it in 2026?

For sellers doing keyword research and listing work every week, yes — it remains the deepest toolset and Diamond is fair value against hiring that work out. For pre-launch sellers with no product yet, $129 a month for software is hard to justify. The price rise moved that line, it did not erase it.

Platinum or Diamond?

Decide on caps and the ads module, not on tool count. Platinum's 20 tracked ASINs and 100 monthly keyword searches are the binding constraint for most people. Diamond adds ad automation, inventory and P&L reporting, 1,000 ASINs and six users — and the 2% managed ad spend fee.

How accurate is Helium 10's sales data?

Good enough to make decisions with, not good enough to treat as truth. One published comparison put it at 79.3% accurate against Jungle Scout's 84%. Different tests give different answers. Use estimates to rank opportunities against each other, never as an absolute forecast for a business plan.

Does Helium 10 manage my ads for me?

No. It gives you automation you configure and supervise. Rules fire on thresholds you set, and someone still owns targets, structure, launches and the calls that depend on margin or inventory. That is the difference between a tool and a managed service, and it is worth being clear about before you buy either.
