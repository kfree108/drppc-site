Sellerboard pricing: the four plans, and which one you actually need

Sellerboard costs $19, $29, $39 or $79 a month for Standard, Professional, Business and Enterprise. Billed annually those drop to $15, $23, $31 and $63. Every plan carries the same core features. What separates them is monthly order volume, seats and connected accounts. The trial is one month, no card required.

The four plans, taken from sellerboard's own site

Monthly price first, then the annual-billing equivalent and the yearly total:

  • Standard — $19/mo, or $15/mo billed annually ($179/yr). Up to 3,000 orders a month, 1 user, 4 additional seller accounts, 150 follow-up emails.
  • Professional — $29/mo, or $23/mo annually ($279/yr). Up to 6,000 orders, 2 users, 6 accounts, 6,000 emails.
  • Business — $39/mo, or $31/mo annually ($369/yr). Up to 15,000 orders, 4 users, 8 accounts, 6,000 emails.
  • Enterprise — $79/mo, or $63/mo annually ($759/yr). Up to 50,000 orders, 4 users, 16 accounts, 30,000 emails.

The trial is one full month with no credit card. That is unusually generous for this category and it is enough time to load your costs and see whether the numbers match your own bookkeeping.

The mistake almost every comparison page makes

Search sellerboard pricing and you will repeatedly see "starts at $15 a month". That is the annual rate presented as the monthly one. Pay month to month and Standard is $19.

The same slip runs up the ladder. We found a well-ranked review listing the plans as $15, $23, $39 and $63 — a mix of annual rates for two tiers and monthly for another. Anyone budgeting from that table is wrong on three of four lines.

This is worth more than a pedantic correction. It tells you how much of the published comparison content in this category is copied rather than checked. When a page cannot get a public price right, treat its feature claims with the same suspicion. You are buying order volume and seats, not features

This is the part that decides your plan. Sellerboard does not gate its core product behind higher tiers. Profit dashboard, inventory management, reimbursement tracking for lost and damaged stock, listing change alerts, the PPC dashboard and the lifetime-value view are on every plan, including the $19 one.

So the sizing questions are narrow:

  • How many orders a month? This is the hard ceiling. Cross 3,000 and you move up whether you want to or not.
  • How many people need logins? Standard gives one. If your bookkeeper and your ops lead both need access, you are on Professional at minimum.
  • How many seller accounts and marketplaces? Standard covers four additional accounts, which is more than most sellers expect at that price.
  • Do you send follow-up emails? Standard's 150 a month is the one genuinely tight limit at the bottom tier.

Practical advice: size on your peak month, not your average. A business averaging 2,400 orders that does 4,000 in November is a Professional customer, and finding that out in the middle of Q4 is not the moment.

What the subscription does not cover

Sellerboard is profit analytics, and it is genuinely good at that. It calculates net profit per order with Amazon fees, FBA costs, ad spend, refunds and COGS accounted for, which is a harder job than it sounds and a job most sellers do badly in a spreadsheet.

But it reports on advertising rather than running it. There is a PPC dashboard; there is no bid management and no campaign creation. Sellerboard will show you that a product is losing money after ad spend. Deciding which keywords to cut, what the new bids should be and whether to defend or retreat is a separate tool and a separate skill.

That is not a criticism — it is a scope choice, and at $19 a month it is a defensible one. It just means the sellerboard line is rarely your only software line. Most people running it also pay for something on the advertising side, and the honest budget is both numbers together.

Common questions

How much is sellerboard per month?

$19 for Standard, $29 Professional, $39 Business and $79 Enterprise when billed monthly. Annual billing brings those to $15, $23, $31 and $63 a month. Figures from sellerboard's own pricing table, checked 20 August 2026.

Which sellerboard plan should I choose?

Pick on your peak monthly order count and the number of logins you need. Standard covers 3,000 orders and one user; Professional 6,000 and two; Business 15,000 and four; Enterprise 50,000 and four. The features are the same across all of them.

Is the annual plan worth it?

It saves roughly 20%, so about $49 a year on Standard. Worth it once you have run the trial and confirmed your COGS import works. Less obviously worth it in your first month, when you are still deciding whether the tool fits.

Does sellerboard manage Amazon ads?

No. It reports on advertising through a PPC dashboard, showing profit after ad spend, but it does not set bids or build campaigns. You will need a separate tool or service for that, which is worth including when you compare total cost.

Is there a free version?

There is a one-month free trial with no credit card required, but no permanently free tier. After the trial you pick one of the four paid plans.
