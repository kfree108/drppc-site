Perpetua Pricing: Half the Number Is on the Page

Perpetua publishes three tiers: Essentials at $695/month for up to $10,000 in monthly ad spend, Growth at $695/month plus a percentage of ad spend above $10,000, and Premium at custom pricing for accounts over $500,000 in spend. The percentage itself is not published.

The published price list

Straight from Perpetua's own pricing page:

  • Essentials — $695/month. For accounts up to $10,000 in monthly ad spend. No percentage component listed.
  • Growth — $695/month plus a percentage of ad spend. For accounts over $10,000 in monthly ad spend. The percentage is not stated.
  • Premium — custom. For accounts over $500,000 in monthly ad spend.

There is no published free trial and no published contract term. The page does not state whether the percentage applies to all spend or only to spend above the $10,000 line, which is a materially different bill and is worth pinning down in writing.

Note the entry point. At $695 a month, Essentials is the most expensive published starting price of the tools in this comparison set, and it is aimed at accounts spending up to $10,000 — where a $695 fee is 7% of spend at the top of the band and considerably more below it.

The undisclosed percentage is the actual price

Once you cross $10,000 in monthly spend, the base fee stops being the interesting number. The variable does all the work, and it is blank.

Run it yourself. At $100,000 a month in ad spend, every single percentage point is $1,000 a month — $12,000 a year. The difference between a 2% rate and a 4% rate is $24,000 annually, and you cannot see which one you are being offered until you have taken the call and shared your account details. At $300,000 in spend, one point is $36,000 a year.

We charge a percentage ourselves, so the model is not the objection. The objection is that a buyer cannot build a comparison spreadsheet from a blank cell. If Perpetua's rate is competitive, publishing it would help them; if it varies by negotiation, that is worth knowing too.

Why other sites quote completely different numbers

Search around and you will find Perpetua described as $250/month for a Starter plan, $550/month for Growth, four tiers instead of three, and various other combinations. Those do not match the vendor's current page, which lists three tiers with a $695 entry.

This happens constantly in this category. Pricing pages get revised, roundup articles do not, and the stale figure then gets copied into the next roundup. We checked another vendor in this set whose own page had moved to a different currency entirely while third-party posts were still quoting the old dollar amounts.

The rule is simple and it applies to us too: before you budget, open the vendor's own pricing page and read it yourself. If the number you need is not there, that is data as well.

What Perpetua genuinely does well

Perpetua earned its reputation. Its goal-based campaign structure — set a target and let the system build and manage toward it — was one of the better ideas in Amazon advertising software and it is still cleanly executed. The interface is one of the most usable in the category, which matters if a junior team member is going to live in it.

Coverage is broad: Sponsored Ads, Amazon DSP, and multiple marketplaces beyond Amazon. It is backed by Ascential, which means resourcing and roadmap continuity most independent tools cannot match. For an in-house team that wants to own campaign strategy and needs strong automation underneath it, Perpetua is a legitimate answer and has been for years.

The honest caveat is scope. Perpetua optimises advertising. It does not read your margins, your landed cost, your inventory position and your fee structure and tell you which products deserve spend at all. That decision stays with your team.

Which one you should actually pick

Perpetua suits in-house teams that want to own advertising strategy and need strong goal-based automation underneath it, especially across DSP and multiple marketplaces — it is a well-built product with real backing. It suits you less if you need a full cost before a sales call, or if nobody internally will own campaign strategy day to day.

Common questions

How much does Perpetua cost per month?

Essentials is $695/month for up to $10,000 in monthly ad spend. Growth is $695/month plus an unpublished percentage of ad spend for accounts above $10,000. Premium is custom-quoted above $500,000 in monthly spend.

What percentage of ad spend does Perpetua charge?

Perpetua does not publish it. You will be given a rate on a sales call. Ask whether it applies to all spend or only to spend above the $10,000 threshold — the two produce very different bills.

Does Perpetua have a free trial?

No free trial is published on their pricing page. The entry point is a demo. If hands-on evaluation matters to you, ask for a paid pilot with a defined exit.

Why do some sites say Perpetua is $250 or $550 a month?

Those figures do not match Perpetua's current pricing page, which lists a $695 entry tier. Third-party roundups often carry stale numbers for years. Check the vendor's page before budgeting.

Perpetua or Dr. PPC?

Different purchases. Perpetua is software your team drives toward goals it sets. Dr. PPC is the account being run for you, with Orbit's software included, at $300/month plus 3% of ad spend, capped, month-to-month. If you have a capable in-house PPC operator, Perpetua may well be the better fit.
