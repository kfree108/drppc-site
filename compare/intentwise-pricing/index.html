There is no Intentwise price list, and that is deliberate

We checked intentwise.com directly. There is no pricing page, no plan table, no starting-at figure anywhere on the site. Every path ends at Request a demo or Schedule a discovery call.

This is normal for enterprise commerce analytics and it is not a trap. Intentwise sells data pipelines and multi-retailer reporting to brands and agencies whose setups differ enormously. A single-marketplace seller with one ad account and an agency running forty clients across six retailers are not buying the same product, so there is no honest single number to print.

What it does mean is that every price you find in a listicle is third-hand. G2's listing and several review sites report Intentwise starting somewhere between $500 and $1,500 a month depending on which product you take, and at least one reports a $1,500 setup fee covering configuration, data-flow validation and consultation hours, with additional data destinations billed around $500 a month. Intentwise has not published any of that. Treat those numbers as directional and get your own figure in writing.

What third parties report, and why we are not printing it as fact: Intentwise publishes no pricing. Every plan is quoted after a discovery call, and the number moves with your ad spend, retailers and data destinations. Third-party listings report entry points around $500 to $1,500 a month plus a setup fee, but Intentwise has never confirmed those figures publicly. We have not been able to confirm any of that on the vendor's own pages, so treat it as a starting point for the conversation rather than a price.

What you are actually being quoted for

Intentwise is not one product, which is the main reason quotes vary so widely. The suite splits three ways:

  • Foundation — data unification and pipelines. Standardising commerce data across platforms so it can feed a warehouse or a BI tool.
  • Intelligence — Product 360 and Intentwise Explore, their Amazon Marketing Cloud layer. Dashboards, diagnostics, anomaly detection.
  • Optimize — the execution side, covering Amazon DSP, Walmart, Criteo, Instacart, Target Roundel and TikTok.

They also ship an AI Gateway over MCP that pulls their analytics into assistants like Claude and Copilot, which is a genuinely forward-looking piece of engineering and worth asking about.

Before the call, decide which of those three you actually need. A brand that wants AMC answers is buying something very different from an agency that wants a warehouse feed, and walking in with the wrong scope is how quotes balloon.

What moves the number on your quote

Based on how this category prices generally, and on what the review sites report, expect these to be the levers:

  • Ad spend under management. Almost every platform in this space bands its pricing by spend.
  • Number of retailers and data sources. Amazon only is cheaper than Amazon plus Walmart plus Criteo.
  • AMC instance access. Explore is listed as its own line by third parties.
  • Data destinations. Piping into your own warehouse is reportedly a separate recurring charge per destination.
  • Seats and client accounts if you are an agency.

Ask for the quote broken out by line, not as one blended monthly figure. It is the only way to tell later which part you are actually using.

The cost that is not in the quote

The subscription is the visible half. The other half is who drives it.

Analytics platforms surface problems. They do not fix them. Someone on your side still has to read the anomaly report, decide whether a rising ACOS on a hero ASIN is a bid problem, a competitor problem or an out-of-stock problem, then go make the change and watch what happens. At the spend levels where Intentwise makes sense, that is usually a full-time analyst or an agency retainer on top.

When you compare platforms, compare the total: licence plus setup plus the person. A cheaper licence that needs two more analyst days a week is not cheaper.

Which one you should actually pick

Intentwise suits brands and agencies selling across several retailers who need commerce data unified and landed in their own systems — that is real engineering and worth paying for. It suits Amazon-only sellers far less well. If you want the analytics without a separate subscription, Orbit covers that ground and comes free with Dr. PPC, which is priced in public and month-to-month.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. On the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Whatever you buy — a seat, a service, or nothing — that number is the one it has to move, and a cheaper tool nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

How much does Intentwise cost per month?

Intentwise does not publish a figure. Third-party listings report entry points in the $500 to $1,500 a month range depending on product, with a setup fee reported separately, but none of that is confirmed by Intentwise. Check their site and get a written quote for current pricing.

Does Intentwise offer a free trial?

No free trial is advertised on their site. The entry point is a demo or discovery call. If trialling before committing matters to you, that is a real difference — Dr. PPC's first 30 days are free.

Is Intentwise worth it for a single-marketplace Amazon seller?

Usually not. Its strength is unifying data across many retailers and feeding it somewhere else. If everything you sell is on Amazon and your question is which bids and search terms to change this week, you are paying for range you will not use.

What should I ask on the Intentwise demo call?

Ask for the quote itemised: platform, AMC access, each data destination, seats, setup. Ask what the renewal price looks like in year two. Ask which product the number covers, since Foundation, Intelligence and Optimize are separate. And ask who on your team is expected to operate it day to day.
