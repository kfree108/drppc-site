Scale Insights pricing: the per-ASIN ladder, and when to skip it

Updated 2026-08-20 · 1347 words · Written against what currently ranked for “scale insights pricing”
The short answer

Scale Insights prices by the number of ASINs you automate, not by ad spend. Plans run from $78 a month for 5 ASINs up to $688 for 100, with a 1%-of-ad-spend plan for unlimited ASINs. Billing yearly takes 20% off. The trial automates one product in one country.

The ladder, taken from their own pricing page

Seven fixed tiers, each defined by how many ASINs you let the software automate. Monthly, then the annual price:

  • Scale 5 — $78/mo, $748/yr, 5 ASINs
  • Scale 10 — $98/mo, $940/yr, 10 ASINs
  • Scale 20 — $178/mo, $1,708/yr, 20 ASINs
  • Scale 35 — $288/mo, $2,764/yr, 35 ASINs
  • Scale 50 — $388/mo, $3,724/yr, 50 ASINs
  • Scale 75 — $548/mo, $5,260/yr, 75 ASINs
  • Scale 100 — $688/mo, $6,604/yr, 100 ASINs

Above that there is the 1% plan: one percent of monthly ad spend, unlimited automated ASINs. Annual billing is roughly 20% cheaper across the board. Every tier includes mass campaign creation, ads insights and sales insights — you are not buying features as you climb, you are buying ASIN slots.

The count is automated ASINs, not catalogue ASINs. If you sell 300 products but only 12 carry meaningful ad spend, you are shopping in the Scale 20 tier, not looking for an enterprise quote. That distinction saves people a lot of money and it is the single most misread thing about their pricing.

Cost per automated ASIN is the honest comparison

Divide each tier by its ASIN count and the shape of the pricing becomes obvious:

  • Scale 5 — $15.60 per ASIN
  • Scale 10 — $9.80 per ASIN
  • Scale 20 — $8.90 per ASIN
  • Scale 35 — $8.23 per ASIN
  • Scale 50 — $7.76 per ASIN
  • Scale 100 — $6.88 per ASIN

The bottom rung is by far the worst value per unit, and the curve flattens hard after Scale 20. Practically, that means two things. If you are at 4 or 5 ASINs, you are paying a premium for the privilege of starting small. And if you are at 18 ASINs, stepping to Scale 20 costs $80 more than Scale 10 and buys ten more slots — usually worth it, whereas jumping from 50 to 100 to cover three extra products is not.

Check your slot count against reality before you renew. Paused ASINs and seasonal products quietly occupy slots you are paying for.

The point where the 1% plan beats the ladder

The 1% plan is usually pitched as the enterprise option, but the arithmetic says otherwise. Divide any tier price by 0.01 and you get the monthly ad spend at which the 1% plan costs the same:

  • Scale 5 ($78) — break-even at $7,800/mo ad spend
  • Scale 10 ($98) — $9,800/mo
  • Scale 20 ($178) — $17,800/mo
  • Scale 35 ($288) — $28,800/mo
  • Scale 50 ($388) — $38,800/mo
  • Scale 100 ($688) — $68,800/mo

Below the break-even, the 1% plan is cheaper and gives you unlimited ASINs. A seller spending $12,000 a month who needs 35 ASINs automated pays $288 on the fixed tier or $120 on the 1% plan. Above the break-even, the fixed ladder wins and the gap widens fast.

So the rule is the opposite of the intuition: low spend across many products favours the 1% plan; high spend across few products favours a fixed tier. Run your own numbers before the renewal date.

What the subscription does not buy you

Be precise about what Scale Insights is. It is a rules engine for bid and campaign automation — dayparting, bid adjustments, negative-keyword workflows, campaign templates, across Sponsored Products, Brands and Display. It is very good at executing rules quickly and consistently, at a level of granularity most tools do not reach.

What it does not do is decide the rules. Nobody at Scale Insights tells you that a 28% ACOS target is wrong for a product with a 41% contribution margin, or that your hero ASIN's problem this month is inventory rather than bids. The strategy is your job, and the tool amplifies whatever you give it, including the mistakes. Reviewers consistently flag a steep initial learning curve for exactly this reason.

It also stays inside advertising. No repricing, no listing work, no profitability accounting across the wider business.

Which one you should actually pick

Scale Insights suits hands-on sellers and agencies who already know their strategy and want granular, cheap rule automation — at $178 a month for 20 ASINs it is genuinely good value. It suits people who want the thinking done far less well. Dr. PPC is for those who would rather buy the strategy and the analytics together, month-to-month.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. On the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Whatever you buy — a seat, a service, or nothing — that number is the one it has to move, and a cheaper tool nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

How much is Scale Insights per month?

From $78 a month for 5 automated ASINs to $688 for 100, with a 1%-of-ad-spend plan covering unlimited ASINs. Annual billing is about 20% cheaper. Figures taken from their own pricing page.

Does the ASIN count mean my whole catalogue?

No. It counts only the ASINs you set the software to automate. A 300-product catalogue where 12 products carry the ad spend fits comfortably inside a 20-ASIN plan.

Is the free trial actually usable?

Partly. You get full feature access with no credit card, but automation is restricted to one product in one country. That is enough to judge the interface and the rule builder, not enough to see portfolio-level effects.

Is Scale Insights cheaper than Dr. PPC?

On licence cost, yes, and clearly so. It is software you run yourself. Dr. PPC at $300/month plus 3% of ad spend includes the strategy work and Orbit, so the comparison only holds if you also price in whoever writes and maintains your rules.

Does Scale Insights handle Sponsored Brands and Display?

Yes, it covers Sponsored Products, Brands and Display. What it does not cover is anything outside advertising — no repricing, no listing management, no wider profitability accounting.
