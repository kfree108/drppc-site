Helium 10 vs Jungle Scout: same shelf, different bill once you start advertising

Updated 2026-08-21 · 2600 words · Written against what currently ranked for “helium 10 vs jungle scout”
The short answer

Helium 10 has the deeper toolset, especially on keyword research, and gates its advertising product behind Diamond with a 2% fee on managed ad spend. Jungle Scout is easier to learn, cheaper to enter, and charges no percentage of spend at any tier. Which is cheaper depends entirely on how much you advertise.

Both price lists, both billing tabs, in one place

Read from each vendor's own pricing page on 20 August 2026. Every figure below names its billing basis, because reading the wrong side of a toggle is how most published comparisons of these two go wrong — including one of ours, which we unpublished rather than patch.

Helium 10: Platinum is $129/month billed monthly or $99/month on annual billing. Diamond is $359/month billed monthly or $279/month on annual billing. Enterprise starts at $1,499/month, quoted on annual billing.

Jungle Scout Catalyst: the three tiers are $49, $79 and $149 a month billed monthly, or $29, $49 and $129 a month on annual billing, with annual totals of $348, $588 and $1,548 printed alongside. The page advertises a saving of up to 40% on annual plans and the arithmetic supports it — $29 against $49 is a reduction of nearly 41%.

Jungle Scout also publishes a seven-day money-back guarantee, no questions asked. We found no free trial advertised on Helium 10's pricing page as we read it.

One habit worth taking from this section and applying everywhere: do the annual multiplication yourself. Twelve times the annual per-month rate should equal the annual total. When it does not, you have misread a tab rather than found a scandal — assume your own error first.

The difference that actually decides your bill

Compare the subscriptions and these two products look close. Compare the total cost of running ads through them and they are not close at all, because only one of them charges for spend.

Helium 10's pricing page states that Diamond customers incur a 2% management fee on PPC spend managed through Helium 10 Ads. Jungle Scout charges no percentage of ad spend on any Catalyst tier.

Play that forward. At $10,000 of monthly managed spend, the Helium 10 fee is $200 a month on top of the $279 Diamond line, while Jungle Scout's top Catalyst tier stays at $129. At $50,000 of spend, the fee alone is $1,000 a month and Jungle Scout is still $129. At $200,000, the fee is $4,000 a month against a Catalyst subscription that has not moved.

Two honest readings of that, and both are worth holding at once.

The first: on pure cost, Jungle Scout wins decisively at high ad spend, and no feature comparison changes that arithmetic. If you are spending heavily and the ad features you need are covered, the flat subscription is the cheaper structure by a wide margin.

The second: you are not buying the same thing. Helium 10 Ads is a fuller advertising product than Catalyst's advertising analytics and negative keyword harvesting, and a percentage fee is how a vendor prices something whose cost to them scales with your account. Percentage-of-spend pricing is the norm at the top of this market — it is also how Dr. PPC prices, at 3% — and it is not a mark against anyone. It just needs to be in the comparison, and in most published comparisons it is not.

Somewhere in the low five figures the Helium 10 Diamond line starts pulling away from the Jungle Scout line, and it never comes back.

That is the honest content of "which is cheaper", and it is why a static comparison table cannot answer the question. It also explains why two experienced sellers can give you flatly contradictory advice about these two products and both be right — they are advertising at different scales.

Where Helium 10 is genuinely the better product

Keyword research. Cerebro and Magnet are the deepest tools of their kind on the market and it is not particularly close. If your work involves reverse-engineering competitor keyword sets weekly, this is the reason to buy Helium 10 and the reason people who switch away often switch back.

Breadth of toolset. Listing optimisation, inventory and profit tools, alerts, refunds, operations — Helium 10 covers more of the seller's week than Catalyst does. Whether that is worth the price difference depends entirely on how many of those tools you open, which is a question only your own login history answers.

Marketplace and channel reach. Helium 10 extends further beyond Amazon than Catalyst does, which matters if a real share of your revenue sits elsewhere.

A fuller advertising product. Helium 10 Ads is a genuine ad management layer rather than an analytics view. That it sits behind Diamond and carries a 2% fee is the price of it being a bigger piece of software, not a penalty.

Where Jungle Scout is genuinely the better product

Sales estimation. This is the company's oldest asset and its best one — more than a decade of refining estimates against real outcomes. If your decisions turn on "how many units does this actually sell", Jungle Scout's number is the one most experienced sellers trust.

Learnability. Underrated to the point of being ignored in most comparisons. A cheaper tool that your team opens beats a deeper tool that intimidates them into never logging in. Catalyst gets a new user productive faster, and for a two-person business that is often the whole decision.

Cost of entry, and cost at scale. $29 a month on annual billing at the entry tier is a low barrier, and as shown above, the absence of any spend-based fee makes it the cheaper option at high ad spend too. It wins at both ends.

A seven-day refund, no questions asked. A published, unconditional exit window is worth more than it sounds when you are choosing between two annual commitments.

Cobalt, if you outgrow Catalyst. There is an enterprise path within the same brand for category benchmarking and share-of-market work, positioned for brands above $1M in annual revenue. Downstream was absorbed into it, so any article offering Downstream as a third option is offering you this.

Choose on what you will open weekly, not on the feature grid

Both feature lists are long enough that a checklist comparison will always favour Helium 10, and that comparison is close to useless. Nobody uses forty tools. Here is a more reliable way to decide.

  • Are you still choosing what to sell? Both work. Jungle Scout gets you moving faster; Helium 10 gives you more to grow into. At this stage the money is small either way — pick the interface you like.
  • Do you reverse-engineer competitor keywords weekly? Helium 10. This is what Cerebro is for and nothing else does it as well.
  • Is a meaningful share of revenue outside Amazon? Helium 10 reaches further.
  • Is your ad spend well into five figures a month? Price the 2% fee before you commit, and compare the total rather than the subscription.
  • Is your team small and non-technical? Jungle Scout, and do not feel bad about it. Adoption is a feature.
  • Do you already know what you sell and just need the account run better? Neither of them is the answer to that, and the next section is about why.

One more weekly job, and it is the one that most reliably pays for the subscription. Attention concentrates on hero products because that is where the revenue is, and the rest of the catalogue keeps running on defaults somebody set years ago. In one mid-market consumer-goods catalogue we audited, the products outside the top twenty were receiving about $2,000 of monthly ad spend between them while producing roughly $30,000 of sales — individual listings funded at $2 to $35 a month. That is a single catalogue rather than a benchmark, but the exercise that finds it takes half an hour: pivot the business report against ad spend at parent-ASIN level, monthly. The correction is undramatic too — raise a handful of starved listings to a real budget and watch them daily for a fortnight. Either suite gives you the data. Neither will book the half hour.

What neither one does, and what that costs

Both are software you operate. The subscription buys capability; it does not buy attention. Someone on your side still opens the tool, reads it, decides and acts, every week, indefinitely — and the size of that job is consistently underestimated because it does not appear on any pricing page.

Here is the size of that job, read across our own managed book rather than one account: 47 brands and 3,086,624 search terms that took a click, in Amazon search data from 1 May 2026 onward.

Inside it, 48.5% of all search spend went to terms that returned no orders — $4,962,963 of $10,243,379 — and 83% of every term that took a click produced no sale. That is why no automation rule catches it: a threshold aggressive enough to reach four terms in five would negate your future winners along with your losers. And 0.9% of 891,585 terms drove 80% of all sales, meaning the highest-value work in an account was never exclusion at all. It was defending a few hundred terms.

No research suite finds that for you, and no rules engine does either. One account carrying that shape could be dismissed as badly run. Forty-seven cannot.

The other half of the job is structure, and it is worth one story because the numbers surprise people. On one account takeover — a wellness supplement brand — we cut weekly ad spend by 67.7% and total revenue fell 3.7%; TACOS went from 26.79% to 8.99% and ACOS from 65.4% to 29%. Two-thirds of the spend was buying almost nothing. The rebuild put 136 campaigns into four clear lanes — branded, non-branded domination, discovery and growth — so each carried its own efficiency target instead of competing inside one blended average. That is one account and not a result anyone should expect. The transferable part is the diagnosis: the problem was not that somebody overspent, it was that the account had no lanes, and without lanes there is no level at which waste is visible. A research suite reports on the structure you have. It does not give you one.

Which one you should actually pick

Choose Helium 10 if you will use deep keyword research weekly, want the broadest toolset, or need reach beyond Amazon — and price its 2% ads fee into the total before you commit. Choose Jungle Scout if you want trusted sales estimates, an interface your team will actually open, the lowest entry cost, or the cheaper structure at high ad spend. Choose neither if the gap is nobody running the account, which is a different purchase entirely.

What to do with this

Neither of these decides your ACoS on its own — how much of the work gets done each week does. Common questions

Which is cheaper, Helium 10 or Jungle Scout?

Jungle Scout, at both ends. Its entry tier is $49 a month billed monthly or $29 on annual billing against Helium 10's $129 or $99, and because it charges no percentage of ad spend it stays cheaper as you scale — Helium 10's 2% fee on spend managed through Helium 10 Ads adds $1,000 a month at $50,000 of spend. Cheaper is not the same as better value; check which tools you would actually open.

Is Helium 10 better than Jungle Scout?

On keyword research and breadth of toolset, yes. On sales estimation, learnability and cost at scale, Jungle Scout. The honest version of the answer is that they are optimised for different buyers: Helium 10 for someone who will use a deep, dense toolset weekly, Jungle Scout for someone who wants a good answer quickly. A tool your team will not open has an effective value of zero, whatever it scores on a feature grid.

Does Helium 10 charge a percentage of ad spend?

Yes, on the Diamond tier: their pricing page states that Diamond customers incur a 2% management fee on PPC spend managed through Helium 10 Ads. Jungle Scout does not charge any percentage of spend. This is the largest cost difference between the two products once you advertise seriously, and it appears in very few published comparisons of them.

Can I use both Helium 10 and Jungle Scout?

Some sellers do, usually keeping Helium 10 for keyword work and Jungle Scout for sales estimates. It is a defensible setup if both get used, but two subscriptions covering overlapping ground is the most common piece of quiet waste in a seller's software budget. Check your login history before renewing both — the honest test is which one you opened last week.

Which one manages my Amazon ads for me?

Neither. Helium 10 Ads executes rules you configure and Catalyst gives you advertising analytics and negative keyword harvesting, but the strategy, the exceptions and the weekly attention stay with you in both cases.
