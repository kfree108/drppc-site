The 3× Table: when to kill a search term, for every conversion rate

Updated 2026-10-22 · free, no sign-up · runs in your browser
How it works

Three times your clicks per order, still no sale: it’s out. Clicks per order is 1 ÷ your ad conversion rate, so at 10% the line is 30 clicks. A term that truly converts that well shows a sale before then about 95% of the time, whatever you sell.

Find your line

Use your ads conversion rate (ad orders ÷ ad clicks), not your listing’s.

How to use it

  1. Pull your search term report and sort it by clicks.
  2. Find your ad conversion rate in the table. Its “Negate after” number is your line.
  3. Past the line with no sale: add it as a negative exact. Under the line: let it run.
  4. Ten clicks and no sale at a 10% rate isn’t a bad keyword. It’s an average one.

Good to know

  • The odds: a term that really converts at 10% still goes 20 clicks with no sale about 12% of the time (0.9²⁰), and 30 clicks about 4% (0.9³⁰).
  • Three times clicks per order works out near 95% sure at any rate (about e⁻³), and a little surer at high rates.
  • One number for every product (say, 15 clicks) cuts slow converters too early: at 5%, 15 clicks isn’t even one order’s worth.
  • Use a long enough date range that every term has had its clicks, and leave out the last few days, which are still filling in.

Dr. PPC runs this as a standing rule: no search term is negated before it has had its clicks, and every negative waits for your approval.
