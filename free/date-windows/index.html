The Date Windows Sheet

Updated 2026-10-22 · free, no sign-up · runs in your browser
How it works

Amazon credits a Sponsored Products sale to your ad for 7 days after the click (Sponsored Brands and Display: 14), and dates the sale back to the day of the click. So your newest days are always still filling in, and yesterday’s ACoS almost always looks too high. Judge on windows that end a few days back, change one thing at a time, and wait before you judge it.

How to use it

  1. Pick the window from the table for the report you’re reading.
  2. Leave out the newest days: they’re still filling in.
  3. Change one thing per campaign, and never move a bid more than 25% at once.
  4. Log it: date, what changed, why, and the day to judge it.
  5. On that day, compare before and after on complete days only.

Good to know

  • Example: Monday you spend $100. Tuesday morning, Monday shows $150 in sales: 67% ACoS. A week later the late orders have landed: $400 in sales, 25%. Same day, same spend. Cut that bid on Tuesday and you cut a winner.
  • Attribution as of October 2026: Sponsored Products 7 days for sellers (14 for vendors); Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display 14 days. Sales are reported on the click date.
  • “Most late orders are in after 3 days” is a rule of thumb, not an Amazon rule. For 14-day ads we wait a week.
  • “Last 7 days, through yesterday” always looks worse than it is, because its newest days are still filling in.

Dr. PPC works the same way: it judges on complete days and logs every change with the reason, so you can check it a week later.
