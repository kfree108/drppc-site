When To Stop Paying For Your Own Brand Name

Updated 2026-08-19 · 1017 words · Written from 3 first-party data point(s) in our source bank
The short answer

Stop bidding hard on your own brand name once Search Query Performance shows your purchase share on those terms above roughly 95%. At that point you already win the click without paying for it. Cut branded bids first, defensive campaigns second, and expect branded ACoS to fall by roughly ten points.

Why does branded PPC look so good and cost so much?

Branded campaigns always report the best ACoS in the account, because the shopper typing your brand name was going to buy from you anyway. That is not a performance signal, it is an attribution artefact. The ad simply intercepts a purchase that organic search would have delivered for free.

The trap is that branded spend scales quietly. It never looks like the problem in a weekly report — it looks like the thing propping the report up. Meanwhile total advertising cost of sale drifts upward and nobody can point to the campaign responsible, because every individual campaign looks efficient.

We hit exactly this in a textured-hair-care account. TACoS was stuck above 21% and the brand owner told us plainly they were tight on cash flow because of the spend increase. Branded campaigns were the healthiest-looking line in the account and the single biggest thing we ended up cutting.

What is the purchase-share threshold that says stop?

Search Query Performance reports purchase share per query: of everyone who bought after searching that term, what percentage bought from you. On your own brand name that number should be very high. When it sits at or above roughly 95%, the auction is not contested in any meaningful way and paying to be first is paying for a click you already own.

In the account above, purchase share on branded terms read 97% in SQP week 32. That single number was the whole argument for cutting spend — not a hunch about brand defence, a measured share of a market we had already won.

  • Above ~95% purchase share: cut branded bids aggressively. You are buying your own organic traffic.
  • 85–95%: hold a lean defensive presence, keep bids low, monitor weekly.
  • Below ~85%: something is genuinely competing for your brand name — a reseller, a hijacker, or a competitor conquesting. Do not cut; investigate first.

What order do you cut it in?

Cutting branded spend badly is how accounts get hurt. The sequence matters more than the size of the cut, because you want to remove the spend that buys nothing before you touch the spend that defends something.

  1. Pull SQP for the branded term set and confirm purchase share is above 95% for at least two consecutive weeks. One week is noise.
  2. Pause every branded keyword with ACoS above 50%. These are not defending anything — they are stacking bids inside an auction you already win.
  3. Pause bleeders at both keyword and ASIN level, wherever ACoS is worst. Do this before touching bids, so you are not shrinking bids on targets that should be off entirely.
  4. Reduce bids — do not pause — on the remaining branded and defensive campaigns. A thin presence still blocks the obvious conquesting attempt.
  5. Run a conversion-rate audit across the catalogue in parallel: find every ASIN spending money with low PPC and low overall conversion rate. In our case that was the real driver of high TACoS, and cutting branded spend alone would have masked it.
  6. Hold the new structure for four to eight weeks before judging it. Never read a branded cut on seven days of data.

What happens to organic sales when you cut?

Expect the paid line to fall and the organic line to rise, roughly in step. That re-attribution is the point: the same order now arrives without an ad cost attached. What you are watching for is total units and total profit dollars holding, not paid revenue holding.

If total units drop materially, your purchase share was not as dominant as SQP suggested, or you cut a defensive campaign that was genuinely holding a competitor off. Restore the defensive layer first, at low bids, and re-measure.

The related failure mode is the mirror image of this one: an account ranked number one organically whose search-revenue TACoS approaches 100% because its ads are buying sales it already had. If that sounds like your account, the fix is the same family of moves — see the cannibalization page.

What to do with this

Pull SQP this week and read purchase share on your own brand name. If it is above 95%, you have a ten-point ACoS improvement sitting in your branded campaigns, and the only thing standing between you and it is a report that makes branded spend look like your best-performing line.

Frequently asked

Should I turn branded campaigns off completely?

Almost never. Keep a thin, low-bid presence so an obvious conquesting attempt does not go unanswered. The move is to stop stacking high bids inside an auction you already win, not to vacate it.

How do I know if a competitor is bidding on my brand name?

Purchase share below roughly 85% on your own brand terms is the signal. Combined with a rising CPC on those terms, it usually means a reseller, a hijacker or a competitor is actively conquesting — that is a case for holding spend, not cutting it.

What branded ACoS should I expect after the cut?

Across accounts we manage, healthy branded ACoS runs from about 2% on a mature brand with strong organic rank up to roughly 13% on a first-year brand still building recognition. In the account described here, branded ACoS moved from 26% to 15% after the cut.

Does this apply to Sponsored Brands as well as Sponsored Products?

Yes, and Sponsored Brands is usually where the waste is largest, because the headline placement on your own brand name is the most expensive way to buy a shopper who already typed your name.
