Sponsored Brand Ads: What They Are and How to Make Them Pay

The short answer

Sponsored Brand ads are Amazon's brand-level ad format: a custom headline, logo, and multiple products (or a video) shown at the top of search results, priced per click or per thousand viewable impressions, that send shoppers to your Brand Store instead of a single product page.

What a Sponsored Brand ad actually is

A Sponsored Brand ad is not a bigger version of a Sponsored Product ad. Sponsored Products promotes one ASIN at a time and drops shoppers on that product's detail page. Sponsored Brands promotes the brand itself — your logo, a headline you write, and either a set of products or a short video — and it's built to land people on your Brand Store or a custom page, not a single listing.

There are three creative formats. Product collection shows your logo, a headline, and up to three products with a lifestyle image behind them. Store spotlight shows up to three subpages of your Brand Store, which means your Store needs at least three subpages with a distinct product on each to qualify. Video runs a 6-to-45-second autoplay clip in search results and clicks go straight to the product page.

Eligibility is narrower than Sponsored Products too: you need to be enrolled in Brand Registry, either as the seller, the vendor, or an agency running the account on their behalf.

Where the ads show up and why that changes your strategy

Sponsored Brands ads run at the top of desktop and mobile search results, inside search results, and on the Amazon home page. That top-of-search real estate is the whole point — it's the first thing a shopper sees before any organic or Sponsored Products result, which is why the format is sold on awareness and consideration, not just last-click sales.

Video has its own auction, separate from every other Sponsored Brands or Sponsored Products campaign you're running. That matters practically: a video campaign competing for that inventory does not cannibalize budget or impressions from your standard product-collection campaigns, so you can run both without them fighting each other for the same auction.

How the pricing works — a worked example

Sponsored Brands has no minimum spend and supports two bidding models: CPC (you pay per click) and vCPM (you pay per thousand viewable impressions). Amazon suggests at least $10 a day as a floor to keep the ad showing through the day — treat that as a floor, not a target; size the budget to your catalog and margin, not to the suggestion.

Here's the arithmetic, using round numbers so you can run your own: say you set a $50/day budget on CPC bidding, and your average CPC lands at $1.10. Over a 30-day month that's $1,500 in spend, funding roughly 1,364 clicks. If 9% of those clicks convert at a $42 average order value, that's about 123 orders and $5,166 in revenue — an ACOS around 29%.

That ACOS will usually run higher than a comparable Sponsored Products campaign, and that's expected, not a failure. Sponsored Brands keywords tend to be broader and earlier in the shopping journey — someone searching a category term, not a specific model number. Judging it on ACOS alone misreads what the format is for.

The mistakes that quietly waste the budget

The most common one: routing the ad to a single product page instead of the Brand Store. If you're paying for a brand-awareness format and landing shoppers on one SKU, you've bought Sponsored Products creative at Sponsored Brands prices — you lose the cross-sell into the rest of your catalog that the Store gives you for free.

The second: comparing Sponsored Brands ACOS directly against Sponsored Products ACOS and pulling budget when it looks worse. It almost always looks worse. The metric worth watching is new-to-brand orders and repeat-purchase rate through the Store, not just cost of sale on the click.

The third, and one we've made ourselves before catching it in an account review: setting the same CPC bid across broad, exact, and category targeting inside one ad group. Broad match on a top-of-search format burns through budget on tangential queries fast, and without splitting bids by match type you can't see which targeting is actually earning the placement. Full Circle has managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, and this is the single most repeated fix we make in a new account's first month.

When the numbers say it's not working — what to check first

  • Impressions are low: your bid probably isn't competitive for that placement, or the daily budget is capping delivery before the day ends. Check the budget-vs-spend pace before touching the bid.
  • CTR is fine but conversion is weak: the problem is almost never the ad — it's the landing page. Check whether the Store subpages selected are your actual bestsellers, not whatever was preselected by default.
  • Video CPC is high relative to product-collection: video runs its own auction with different competition, so don't budget it the same way you budget the other formats — test it as its own line item first.
  • ACOS is climbing month over month with flat sales: pull the search term report before cutting budget. Broad keyword bleed is the usual cause, and it's fixable with negative keywords, not with a smaller budget.

Which one you should actually pick

Sponsored Brands earns its budget when you have more than one product worth showing together and a Store worth sending traffic to. If you sell a single SKU with no catalog story, Sponsored Products alone will do more per dollar. If you're building a brand shoppers come back to, Sponsored Brands is the format designed for exactly that.

What to do with this

Shortlist on the job, not the feature grid. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. Then ask each vendor on your list what they would do about it in week one, and see who answers with a process rather than a screenshot.

Common questions

Is Sponsored Brands the same as Sponsored Products?

No. Sponsored Products promotes a single ASIN and lands the click on that listing. Sponsored Brands promotes the brand — logo, headline, multiple products or video — and lands the click on your Brand Store or a custom page. They run in separate auctions and are usually judged on different metrics.

Do I need Brand Registry to run Sponsored Brands?

Yes. Sponsored Brands is available to sellers enrolled in Brand Registry, to vendors, and to agencies managing the account on a registered brand's behalf. If you're not enrolled, this format isn't available to you yet regardless of budget.

What's a reasonable budget to start with?

Amazon's own floor recommendation is around $10 a day, purely to keep the ad live through the day rather than exhausting the budget by noon. Treat that as a minimum to avoid gaps in delivery, not as a target — size the real budget to your catalog and margin.

Should I bid CPC or vCPM?

CPC charges you per click and suits campaigns where you want measurable traffic to the Store. vCPM charges per thousand viewable impressions and suits pure awareness plays where you're paying for eyeballs on the creative regardless of clicks. Most catalogs start on CPC because it ties spend directly to traffic you can measure.

Why is my Sponsored Brands ACOS higher than my Sponsored Products ACOS?

Because it's built for a different job. Sponsored Brands keywords are typically broader and earlier in the shopping journey, and the format is optimized for discovery and new-to-brand orders, not just last-click conversion. Watch new-to-brand rate and repeat purchases through the Store alongside ACOS, not instead of it.
