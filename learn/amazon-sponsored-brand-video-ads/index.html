What a Sponsored Brands video ad actually is

It's one video creative, 6 to 45 seconds, attached to a Sponsored Brands campaign. You pick one of two destinations when you set it up: send clicks to your Brand Store or send clicks to a specific product page. The ad slots in at the top of search results and, depending on placement, inside product detail pages themselves. The video autoplays without sound once 50% of it is visible on screen, and shoppers tap a mute toggle in the corner to turn audio on. Amazon overlays your logo, a line of headline text, and a call-to-action button on top of the video.

Billing depends on which objective you pick at setup: some objectives run on cost-per-click (CPC), others on viewable cost-per-thousand-impressions (vCPM), where you pay for views whether or not anyone clicks. There's no minimum spend requirement on Amazon's side — campaigns can start with budgets as low as $10, per Amazon's own guidance. That's a real structural difference from CPC-only formats: a vCPM campaign can burn budget on impressions with zero clicks, which is normal for the format but confuses people expecting a pure pay-per-click bill.

The specs — and where Amazon's own pages disagree with each other

The technical requirements are fixed and worth getting right before you brief an editor, because a file that misses spec gets rejected in review, not flagged at upload.

Here's the part the spec sheet won't tell you: Amazon's own documentation is inconsistent on aspect ratio. The ad-specs page states video creative must be 16:9 only. The separate 'get started' guide says Sponsored Brands supports both horizontal (16:9) and vertical (9:16) video, and recommends vertical for a more native, mobile feel. Both are live Amazon pages. Until you've confirmed which formats your specific marketplace and campaign type accept, build to 16:9 as the safe default — it's the one every placement definitely takes — and treat vertical as something to test, not assume.

Keep the bottom-right corner clear. That's where the mute toggle sits, and text or a logo placed there gets covered by Amazon's own UI element, not by anything you control.

How it's different from Sponsored Products video

These get confused because both are 'video ads on Amazon,' but they're built for different jobs. Sponsored Products video is feature-focused: one to five short clips per ASIN, minimum seven seconds with no maximum, no logo overlay, and it lives inside an existing Sponsored Products campaign with the same keyword and budget controls you already use. It's built to answer a shopper's product question mid-search.

Sponsored Brands video is brand-first: one video, 6-45 seconds, branded with your logo and a CTA, running at the top of search or driving to your Store. If the goal is 'help someone decide this specific product works for them,' that's Sponsored Products video. If the goal is 'get someone who doesn't know your brand to notice it and land on your Store,' that's Sponsored Brands video. Running both isn't redundant — they sit at different points in the same search session.

A worked example: what the bid actually buys you

Say you set a vCPM campaign at an illustrative $12 viewable-CPM bid, and it delivers 40,000 viewable impressions in a month. That's roughly $480 spent on views alone, before a single click. If 2% of those viewers click through, that's 800 clicks — and your effective cost-per-click, backed into from the vCPM spend, is $0.60. Whether that's good depends entirely on what those clicks do downstream: Store visit rate, add-to-cart rate, eventual sales. A vCPM campaign judged only on 'clicks per dollar' against a CPC benchmark will always look expensive, because it's pricing a different thing — attention, not action.

Before you swap a creative or change a bid on a live video campaign, apply the same discipline you'd want from anyone touching your account: know the evidence that says the change is needed, decide in advance how you'll measure whether it worked, and set a rollback trigger before you flip it live. That's not a slogan — it's the actual sequence that keeps a video test from quietly draining budget for three weeks before anyone notices.

The mistakes that actually happen

The most common one: burying the product or hook past the first few seconds because the brand logo comes first. Amazon's own creative guidance says the opposite — lead with the product or something visually interesting, save the logo for the end card. A video that opens on a logo loses the shopper before the mute toggle even matters.

  • Silent-first video with the message only in the voiceover. Every Sponsored Brands video starts muted. If the core message lives in narration and there's no on-screen text carrying it, most viewers never hear it.
  • Reusing a 30-second TV or social cut unchanged. A video built for a captive audience doesn't work in a scrolling search results feed, where you have roughly two seconds to earn attention before someone scrolls past.
  • Budgeting a vCPM video campaign like a CPC campaign. We've made this one ourselves early on: setting a video campaign's daily budget based on click-volume assumptions carried over from a static Sponsored Brands campaign, then being surprised when spend paced differently. vCPM spends on views, not clicks — budget it that way from the start.
  • Assuming a video isn't playing when it's actually just below the fold. Autoplay only triggers once 50% of the pixels are on screen. A placement that looks 'dead' in reporting is sometimes just a placement nobody scrolled to.

Which one you should actually pick

Sponsored Brands video suits brands already in Brand Registry with a built-out Store and a video asset worth the 6-45 second format — it's an awareness and consideration tool, not a direct-response one. If the goal is answering a specific product question at the point of search, Sponsored Products video or standard image-based Sponsored Brands will usually serve better and cheaper.

Common questions

How much do Sponsored Brands video ads cost?

It depends on the campaign objective you choose at setup: some run on cost-per-click, others on viewable cost-per-thousand-impressions (vCPM). Amazon states there's no minimum spend requirement, and campaigns can start with budgets as low as $10. There's no separate 'video surcharge' — you're bidding the same auction, just against a different metric depending on the objective.

Why doesn't my Sponsored Brands video have sound?

All Sponsored Brands video plays muted by default. A mute toggle sits in the bottom-right corner of the video for shoppers to tap on. If your key message is carried only in a voiceover with no on-screen text, most viewers will never hear it — build the message to work silently first.

Can I use a vertical (9:16) video for Sponsored Brands?

Amazon's ad-specs documentation lists 16:9 as the accepted ratio; a separate getting-started guide says vertical is also supported for a more native feel on mobile. The two pages don't fully agree. Build to 16:9 as the safe default and test vertical in your own account before relying on it.

What's the difference between Sponsored Brands video and Sponsored Products video?

Sponsored Products video is feature-focused: one to five clips per ASIN, minimum seven seconds, no branding overlay, running inside an existing Sponsored Products campaign. Sponsored Brands video is brand-focused: one video, 6-45 seconds, with a logo and call-to-action, sending shoppers to your Store or a product page. They serve different points in the shopper's search.

Why isn't my video autoplaying in reports?

Autoplay triggers once 50% of the video's pixels are visible on screen. A placement that shows low view counts is sometimes just a placement that never scrolled into view for most shoppers, not a broken creative.
