What actually shows up when you search Amazon for this

Search "dragons love tacos" on Amazon and you get over 1,000 results, but only two of them are the real numbered books: Dragons Love Tacos (2012, Book 1) and Dragons Love Tacos 2: The Sequel (2017, Book 2). Everything else — the Definitive Collection, the toy set, the plush, the Tonies audio figurine, a dozen taco-themed t-shirts — is a bundle, a licensed spin-off, or an unrelated product that happens to match the keyword.

The numbers tell you which listing is the real center of gravity: Book 1 carries over 20,900 ratings at 4.7 stars and shows as a #1 Best Seller in its category. The sequel sits around 4,900 ratings at 4.7 stars. The Definitive Collection — both stories bound into one hardcover — has 272 ratings at 4.9 stars. If you're buying for a kid who already read the first book, the sequel or the Definitive Collection are the two legitimate next steps. Nothing else in the results is a third book.

Why the price on your screen won't match the price on someone else's

Pull this listing up twice and you can get two different numbers, sometimes in two different currencies. That's not a glitch. Amazon prices each listing live, by marketplace, by session, and by which third-party seller currently holds the buy box. A hardcover shown as "from $1.63" in one pull can show as roughly the same used-copy price converted to Mexican pesos in another, because the page localized to a different marketplace. Format matters too — Kindle and audiobook prices sit separately from print, and a used paperback from a marketplace seller can undercut a new hardcover by 80% or more.

This is the same instability that makes a single screenshot unreliable for anyone running ads on Amazon, not just a parent buying a picture book. A brand's own price and buy box shift for the same reasons — seller competition, regional catalog differences, timing. The mistake that actually costs people: buying the wrong book

The riskiest mistake isn't overpaying for a used copy. It's buying an imitator. This exact search also surfaces titles like Dragons Love Tacos: The Great Tacos Quest Storybook For Kids by a different author, Dragons Love Farts, Dragons Love Taco Tuesday, and How to Eat a Taco: A Dragon's Cookbook — none written by Adam Rubin or illustrated by Daniel Salmieri. Some are free with Kindle Unlimited, which makes them look like a free version of the real book. They aren't. They're separate, unrelated titles written to catch traffic from a well-known name.

Check the author and illustrator line before you check out. If it doesn't say Adam Rubin and Daniel Salmieri, it's not the book with 20,900 ratings — it's one of the dozen products riding that book's search volume.

Common questions

Is Dragons Love Tacos 2 on Amazon?

Yes. Dragons Love Tacos 2: The Sequel, published in 2017, is Book 2 of 2 in the series and is sold in hardcover, paperback, Kindle, and audiobook on Amazon, carrying roughly 4,900 ratings at 4.7 stars.

How many Dragons Love Tacos books are there?

Two numbered books — Dragons Love Tacos (2012) and Dragons Love Tacos 2: The Sequel (2017) — plus a Definitive Collection that bundles both, and spin-off products like Dragon's First Taco that share the branding but aren't part of the numbered series.

Why does the price on the Amazon listing keep changing?

Amazon renders price by marketplace, session, and current buy box holder, so the same listing can show different figures, or a different currency, between visits. Check the live listing at checkout rather than trusting a remembered price or a screenshot.

Is the free Kindle Unlimited version the real book?

Not always. Titles like Dragons Love Tacos: The Great Tacos Quest Storybook For Kids are free with Kindle Unlimited but are written by a different author and are not the Adam Rubin and Daniel Salmieri book. Check the author line before assuming it's the same story.

What age is Dragons Love Tacos appropriate for?

The original book lists a reading age of roughly 2-5 years and a Pre-K to grade 1 range depending on edition. The sequel is listed slightly older, around 3-6 years.
