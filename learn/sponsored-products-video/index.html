What a Sponsored Products Video Actually Is (And How to Get It Seen)

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1648 words · Written against what currently ranked for “sponsored products video”
The short answer

A Sponsored Products video is an Amazon ad format that lets you upload one to five short feature clips, minimum seven seconds, to a single ASIN inside an existing Sponsored Products campaign. Shoppers see relevant clip thumbnails in search results and can click through without leaving the page.

What it is, and what makes it different from other Amazon video ads

Sponsored Products video is not a new campaign type. It's a video format you attach to a Sponsored Products ad group you already have, using the same targeting and the same budget. You upload between one and five feature clips per ASIN, each with a short text overlay describing what it shows. Amazon then picks up to three of the most contextually relevant clip thumbnails to show a given shopper, based on their search term and browsing history, and displays them under the main video. A shopper can click the video or the product image, whichever they trust more.

The minimum clip length is seven seconds and there's no stated maximum, but in practice the format rewards a fast payoff, not a long one. It launched in the US in November 2025 for both sellers and vendors, accessible through Campaign Manager or the Ads API.

It sits next to two other video formats that get lumped in with it and shouldn't be: Sponsored Brands video, which is one clip built for brand storytelling and premium placement, and Display or Streaming TV video, which runs on and off Amazon (Twitch, IMDb, third-party apps) mainly to re-engage people who already know you. Sponsored Products video is the only one of the three built to answer a specific question a shopper typed into search. The table below lays out the difference in specs.

How to set one up, and what the lift actually looks like on real numbers

Setup is three steps: turn on video for an existing or new Sponsored Products ad group in Campaign Manager, upload one to five feature clips per ASIN with a text overlay on each, and optionally apply a video bid adjustment if you want to raise your odds of winning that placement. Nothing about targeting or budget structure changes.

Here's what the reported lift means in practice. Amazon's own figures put Sponsored Products video at 67% higher click-through and 9% higher conversion versus the same campaign without video. Say an ad group runs 50,000 impressions a month at a 0.35% baseline CTR and an 8% conversion rate with no video — that's 175 clicks and 14 orders. Apply the reported lift: CTR moves to roughly 0.58%, which is about 292 clicks, and conversion moves to roughly 8.7%, which is about 25 orders. Same impressions, same spend efficiency assumption, nearly double the orders — if your account behaves like Amazon's aggregate data, which it may not.

The other number worth building around: shoppers who watch five seconds or more see an 8x CTR jump and are twice as likely to buy the brand within seven days. That's the design cue. The feature payoff has to land inside the first five seconds, not the fifteenth.

Why an approved video can still get zero impressions

This is the part most guides skip. Amazon approving your video means it passed moderation. It does not mean Amazon will serve it. Video ads compete for placement the same way any other ad does, and a video that's technically live can simply lose every auction it's entered into — which looks identical to "nothing happened" from the seller's side.

The gates that usually cause this: weak keyword-to-ASIN relevance (the ad group is targeting terms the clip doesn't answer), a feature mismatch (the clip shown doesn't match what the shopper searched), an uncompetitive bid, or poor inventory and price signals on the listing itself. Any one of these can suppress delivery without ever generating a rejection notice.

If your video isn't getting impressions, check in this order: confirm the clip meets spec (frame composition, length, text overlay), confirm the ad group's search terms actually match what the clip demonstrates, check whether the bid is competitive for that placement, and confirm the listing's price and inventory health aren't flagged elsewhere in the account. Fix the mismatch before you touch the bid — raising a bid on a poorly-targeted clip just spends more money losing the same auction faster.

Creative specs, and the mistake most sellers make with this format

Do: put the product in frame doing the thing the search term implies, put one feature per clip instead of stacking three into one, keep the text overlay legible and short, and match each clip's feature to the ad group's actual keyword. Don't: open with a logo or a scene-setting shot before the product appears, try to make one clip cover the whole value proposition, or reuse a single hero video across every ad group when the format is built to reward multiple, distinct clips.

The mistake we see most, and one we've made ourselves early on: treating this like a brand film. A polished 30-second product story with a voiceover and a slow build is the wrong asset here. Amazon's own guidance and the format's mechanics both point the same direction — a plain, fast demonstration of one feature outperforms a produced piece that takes ten seconds to get to the point, because ten seconds is longer than most of these auctions give you credit for.

How to measure it without guessing

Don't just add video and check ACOS a week later. Split the comparison by ad group with video versus without, and watch CTR and CVR separately — a CTR gain that doesn't move CVR usually means the clip is getting attention it can't convert, not that the format failed.

The discipline we apply to every change on an account, video included, is the same: every proposed change carries three things before it runs — the evidence behind it, a measurement plan, and a rollback trigger. For a video test that means deciding in advance what result justifies keeping it (a CVR move, not just a CTR move), how long you'll let it run before judging, and what number triggers pulling it back to a static ad group if it doesn't show up.

Where this fits if you're not managing it clip by clip

Video is one more variable in an account that already has bids, budgets, search terms, and inventory to watch — and it's exactly the kind of change that should come with evidence and a rollback plan, not a launch-and-hope. Which one you should actually pick

Sponsored Products video suits anyone already running Sponsored Products who has footage proving a specific feature, and wants search-stage shoppers to see it before they click. Sponsored Brands video suits brand storytelling and Store traffic. Display or Streaming TV suits re-engaging shoppers who already bought. Most accounts need more than one, run against evidence, not all three run blind.

What to do with this

Shortlist on the job, not the feature grid. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. Then ask each vendor on your list what they would do about it in week one, and see who answers with a process rather than a screenshot.

Common questions

Can I reuse a video I already have on my product page?

Yes. Sponsored Products video accepts existing product video assets as long as they meet the spec (minimum seven seconds, product prominent in frame, text overlay). You don't need to shoot new footage specifically for the ad; you need footage that isolates one feature clearly.

Does turning on video cost more than a regular Sponsored Products campaign?

There's no separate fee for the format itself — it runs inside your existing Sponsored Products campaign with the same targeting and budget. The only added cost lever is the optional bid adjustment for video placement, which you control and which behaves like any other bid modifier.

My video is approved but has almost no impressions. What's wrong?

Approval means it passed moderation, not that it's winning auctions. Check keyword-to-ASIN relevance first, then whether the clip actually matches the search terms it's attached to, then bid competitiveness, then whether the listing has a price or inventory flag suppressing delivery elsewhere in the account.

How many feature videos should I upload per product?

You can upload up to five. Fewer, sharply distinct clips — each proving one feature to one kind of search intent — tend to outperform five overlapping variations of the same shot, because the format's value comes from matching a specific clip to a specific query.

Is this format available for vendors, or only third-party sellers?

Both. It launched for sellers and vendors in the US in November 2025, accessible through Campaign Manager or the Ads API. Availability outside the US wasn't part of the initial rollout.
