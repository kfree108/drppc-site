What Sponsored Brands Actually Is

Sponsored Brands is the Amazon ad format built around your brand, not a single product. Instead of one product tile, you get your logo, a headline you write yourself, and either a handful of products or a video — placed above the search results, inside them, or on the Amazon home page. Click it, and the shopper lands on your Brand Store, a custom landing page, or a single product page.

It sits next to Sponsored Products (single-ASIN ads that look like a normal listing) and Sponsored Display (retargeting on and off Amazon). Sponsored Brands is the one where you control the creative — the headline, the image, the video — instead of bidding on a listing that already exists as-is.

You need Brand Registry to run it. That's the actual gate: vendors, sellers enrolled in Brand Registry, and agencies working on their behalf can use it. If it's not showing up as an option in your console, that's almost always why.

The Three Formats, and What Each One Actually Does

Amazon groups Sponsored Brands into three creative types, and they behave differently enough that picking the wrong one for the goal is the first mistake most sellers make.

The detail almost everyone misses on video: it runs its own auction. A Sponsored Brands video campaign does not compete against your standard Sponsored Brands or Sponsored Products campaigns for the same impression — it has a separate placement and its own bidding pool. Budgeting it as an extension of your existing self-service spend is a mistake; it's a different line item competing against different sellers for a different slot.

What It Costs, With the Actual Math

Sponsored Brands has no minimum spend and two pricing models: cost-per-click (CPC), where you pay when someone clicks, and cost-per-thousand-viewable-impressions (vCPM), used for the more awareness-focused placements. Amazon recommends budgeting at least $10 a day so the ad has room to show throughout the day — that's a floor for visibility, not a target to hit and stop.

Run the math before you commit a budget, using round numbers as a check, not a promise. Say you set a $60 daily budget and your winning CPC lands around $1.20 — that's roughly 50 clicks a day, before conversion rate even enters the picture. If your product page converts at 10%, that's 5 orders a day from this campaign. That's the calculation to run before launch: clicks you can afford, divided by your bid, tells you whether the budget is even large enough to learn anything in a week.

Nobody, including Amazon, publishes a universal CPC — it moves with category, competition, and keyword intent. Treat any number quoted online as a starting bid to test, not a price you're guaranteed to pay.

The Mistakes That Waste the Budget

The most common failure isn't the bid — it's the creative. Sellers reuse packaging photos built for a Sponsored Products listing, drop them into a Sponsored Brands ad set, and then wonder why click-through is flat. This format is judged on the headline and the image working together to say something a plain listing can't.

  • Sending traffic nowhere useful. A Store Spotlight ad pointed at a Store with two live subpages when Amazon requires three simply won't run.
  • No separation between video and non-video budgets. Since video runs its own auction, folding it into an existing budget hides whether it's actually working.
  • Judging it on ACOS alone. Sponsored Brands is a consideration and new-to-brand tool. Measuring it the same way you'd measure Sponsored Products misreads what it's built to do.

The discipline that actually prevents this isn't complicated: before any change runs — a new headline, a bid jump, a pause — write down the evidence behind it, how you'll measure whether it worked, and what would tell you to reverse it. That's the same standard behind every proposed change inside Dr. PPC: the evidence behind it, a measurement plan, and a rollback trigger. Most sellers skip that step for a $500 test the same way they'd skip it for a $50,000 one — and that's usually where the budget actually gets wasted.

When Sponsored Brands Is the Right Tool

It's built for consideration, not last-click conversion. If you're launching a product and need shoppers who don't know your brand yet to stop and look, or you've built a Brand Store and want to actually send people there, Sponsored Brands earns its budget. If you're defending one ASIN against a competitor bidding on your exact match, Sponsored Products is the sharper, cheaper tool to test first.

Amazon's own partner directory lists automation built specifically for running this day to day — Perpetua for AI-driven bid execution, AiHello for keyword discovery and continuous bid changes, Helium 10 for sellers who want to manage the account themselves. All three are legitimate ways to run Sponsored Brands hands-on, and worth using if direct bid control matters more to you than time saved.

Which one you should actually pick

Sponsored Brands suits brands with a built Store and something worth showing — new-to-brand reach, a catalog worth browsing, a story one product tile can't tell. Sellers defending a single ASIN on exact-match terms get more from Sponsored Products. Common questions

Do I need Brand Registry to run Sponsored Brands?

Yes. Sponsored Brands is only available to vendors, sellers enrolled in Amazon Brand Registry, and agencies working on their behalf. If you're not enrolled, the campaign type won't appear as an option in your console — enroll first, then come back to this.

What's the difference between Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Products?

Sponsored Products advertises a single ASIN and looks like a normal listing in search results. Sponsored Brands advertises the brand itself — your logo, a headline you write, and either multiple products or video — and sends clicks to your Store or a landing page instead of just one product page.

Is there a minimum budget for Sponsored Brands?

No official minimum. Amazon recommends at least $10 a day so the ad has room to show throughout the day, but that's a floor for visibility, not a guarantee of results. The right budget depends on your CPC and how many clicks you need before you can judge whether it's working.

Does Sponsored Brands video compete with my other campaigns for the same impression?

No. Sponsored Brands video runs in its own placement with its own auction, separate from standard Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Products campaigns. That's why it needs its own budget line and its own performance read — folding it into an existing budget hides whether it's actually working.

What length should a Sponsored Brands video be?

Amazon accepts 6 to 45 seconds and recommends 20 seconds or under. The video autoplays muted by default, so the first few seconds need to work without sound — get to the product or the hook before you get to the logo.
