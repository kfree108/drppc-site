What Quartile Actually Is

Quartile is an AI-based bid optimization platform for Amazon Sponsored Ads, paired with a managed service team. Its mechanical core is campaign structure: it builds single-keyword, single-ASIN, single match-type campaigns, so a keyword gets its own bid instead of sharing a budget and a bid ceiling with twenty other terms in the same ad group.

On top of that structure it pulls Amazon Marketing Stream — the hourly event feed Amazon publishes for clicks, spend, and conversions — and re-bids against it, in some cases several times a day rather than once every 24 hours the way older rule-based automation does. It also pulls Amazon Marketing Cloud data for cross-device path-to-purchase signals, which it says it uses to build full-funnel targeting and shopper personas.

The managed service half is a human account team that reads the algorithm's output, sets the guardrails — target ROAS, TACOS, budget bands — and handles anything the bidding engine shouldn't touch alone: new campaign structure calls, budget moves across products, judgment rather than a bid nudge.

How the Hourly Bidding Actually Works

To see why hourly matters, look at the math any bid engine runs constantly. Say a keyword sells a $30 product with a 25% break-even ACOS — the ceiling cost-per-click before that sale stops paying for the ad is roughly $7.50 divided by clicks-per-sale. At one sale per 30 clicks, break-even CPC is $0.25. At one sale per 10 clicks — a top-of-search placement catching real intent for a few hours — break-even CPC jumps to $0.75. An engine that checks once a day misses that window; conversion rate has already moved back by the time it re-bids. Checking hourly, in theory, catches both the spike and the drop.

That speed and granularity is the genuinely strong part of Quartile's pitch — reacting to real-time data at the keyword level is hard to do by hand across a large catalog. The gap is what feeds the model on day one: if historical performance is thin, seasonal, or the account's match types are a mess, the engine inherits that mess and optimizes toward it.

What It Costs — and What Quartile Doesn't Publish

Quartile does not list pricing on its own site. The structure that surfaces most often — including from a competing agency's own comparison page — is a percentage of ad spend rather than a flat fee. That's a normal way to price ad management; plenty of agencies do it, including ours. But a percentage with no published rate means the real cost only shows up once you're already in a sales conversation. At $50,000 a month in spend, the gap between a 5% fee and an 8% fee is $18,000 a year. Ask for the number, and ask what it's a percentage of — gross spend, net spend, and spend above a threshold all produce different bills.

One thing worth naming plainly: a rival agency's marketing page claims some Quartile users report difficult cancellations and billing continuing after notice. We haven't verified that ourselves and it comes from a competitor's own sales page, so treat it as a reason to read the cancellation terms before you sign — not as an established fact about Quartile.

What Quartile Is Genuinely Good At — and Where the Job Stops

Quartile publishes scale numbers on its own site: $2B+ in annual retail ad spend managed, $25B+ in annual managed sales, and 5,300+ customers across 32 countries, plus self-reported average gains of 41% ROAS and 33% conversion rate lift across its customer base. Those are Quartile's own figures, not independently audited, but the underlying claim — a lot of accounts running through one bidding engine for years — is plausible.

What it's built for: keyword and bid management at hourly granularity, campaign structure automation, and cross-channel reporting for teams whose creative and catalog are already in decent shape. What it isn't built for: writing bullet points, fixing a main image that isn't converting, or catching an inventory problem before it caps sales. That's not a knock unique to Quartile — it's true of any bid-optimization tool, including ours when a client sets bidding to fully manual. An engine can only spend well against a listing that's already doing its job; it can't make the listing do that job.

The Mistake Sellers Make With Bid Automation

The most common mistake isn't picking the wrong tool. It's assuming automated bidding is solving a problem it was never pointed at. If click-through rate is weak because the main image doesn't stand out in a search grid, adjusting the bid every hour won't fix that — the engine just spends less on a keyword that keeps under-converting, quietly capping a product a new image could have unlocked. We've watched this on accounts elsewhere: a team kept tightening bids on a keyword for months when the real fix was a listing change no bidding algorithm was ever going to propose on its own.

The fix isn't more automation. It's checking, before touching a bid, whether the problem is a bid problem at all — traffic and conversion data usually tells you which one you're looking at.

Common questions

Does Quartile manage Amazon PPC automatically or do I still run it myself?

It's a hybrid. The bidding engine adjusts bids on its own using Amazon Marketing Stream data, but a dedicated account team sets the rules, guardrails, and targets it optimizes toward, and you can import your own rules to run alongside or instead of the AI.

What campaign structure does Quartile use?

Single-keyword, single-ASIN, single match-type campaigns. Each keyword gets its own bid and its own campaign rather than sharing a budget inside a larger ad group, which is what gives the engine room to react at the keyword level instead of the group level.

How much does Quartile cost?

Quartile doesn't publish pricing on its site. The structure most commonly reported is a percentage of ad spend rather than a flat monthly fee. Get a quote and ask specifically what the percentage applies to — gross spend, net spend, or spend above a set threshold — since each produces a different bill.

Will Quartile fix a listing with a low click-through rate?

No. Bid automation adjusts spend around the click-through and conversion rate a listing already has; it doesn't rewrite copy, redesign images, or fix catalog issues. That's true of bid-only tools generally, not a gap specific to Quartile.

Is Quartile only for Amazon?

No. Quartile's own site describes it as a cross-channel platform managing spend and sales across multiple marketplaces and channels, with customers reported across 32 countries, so Amazon is one channel among several it optimizes.
