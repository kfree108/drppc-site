What a Sponsored Brands video ad actually is

A Sponsored Brands video is a short, auto-playing video ad that Amazon slots into search results and into product detail pages. It's a variant of Sponsored Brands, sitting alongside the static, headline-plus-product version of that format, and it's a different unit from Sponsored Display video, which shows up on product pages and off-Amazon but not inside search results.

The video starts muted and begins playing once at least half of it is visible on screen; shoppers tap a small speaker icon to unmute. You choose one of two destinations for the click: your Brand Store, or a single product detail page. You can't split the click across multiple products from one ad — pick the product this creative is built to sell, or build the ad to sell the Store as a whole.

Both routes require Brand Registry. There's no required minimum budget; Amazon states campaigns can start at around $10 a day, though at that level you're testing, not learning much yet.

The specs that get creative rejected, and why they exist

Amazon's technical bar is specific and mostly exists for a reason. Video runs 6 to 45 seconds, but Amazon's own guidance recommends staying at or under 20 seconds — attention on a search results page is short, and a video that hasn't made its point by the eight-second mark has usually lost the shopper already.

The file has to be 16:9 per Amazon's current spec page, MP4 or MOV, under 500MB, at least 1 Mbps video bitrate (4 Mbps or higher recommended), H.264 or H.265, with audio at a minimum of 96 kbps and 44.1 kHz if you're using sound. None of that is arbitrary — it's what renders cleanly at the small size inside a search result and the larger size on a product page without artifacting or dropped frames during autoplay.

Two rejection reasons show up more than any other: black or empty frames at the start or end of the file (the ad loops, so a black frame becomes a visible flicker on repeat), and text or a logo placed in the bottom-right corner — that's exactly where Amazon overlays the mute toggle, and anything placed there gets covered.

How it's billed, with a worked example

Sponsored Brands video runs on one of two models depending on the campaign objective you pick at setup: cost-per-click (CPC), where you pay only when someone clicks, or vCPM (viewable cost per thousand impressions), where you pay for reach whether or not anyone clicks. Store-destination and product-page campaigns can each run on either model.

The two models buy different things, so it's worth running the arithmetic before choosing. Say you have $50 a day and an $8 vCPM bid. At full spend that's roughly 6,250 viewable impressions a day ($50 ÷ $8 × 1,000). At a 0.3% click-through rate — a modest floor for video — that's about 19 clicks. Run the same $50 on CPC at a $1.20 bid instead, and you buy roughly 41 clicks, but you lose guaranteed reach; on a slow day you could win zero impressions if you're outbid. vCPM buys certainty of exposure. CPC buys clicks and nothing else. The choice should follow the job: awareness or direct response, not whichever model the campaign wizard defaults to.

One frequently cited analysis, run in 2021 across 15 brands, found branded Sponsored Brands video click-through rates running several times higher than other Amazon ad formats in that sample, with lower cost-per-click — plausibly because the format was newer and less bid up at the time. Treat that as directional. The auction has gotten more competitive since, and your result depends on your category and your creative, not on someone else's 2021 sample.

The creative mistake that shows up in almost every account

The most common failure isn't a spec violation — it's sequencing. Brands put their logo and name in the first two seconds because that's how a television spot is built, then wonder why the video doesn't hold attention. Amazon's own guidance is blunt about this: show the product or something visually interesting immediately, and save the branding for later in the video, not the opener. A shopper decides whether to keep watching in the first second or two, before audio even matters, since the video starts muted.

Second most common: a video built to showcase three products, clicking through to a Store page that leads with a fourth product entirely. If the video promises something specific, the landing page needs to deliver that specific thing on the first screen after the click, or the click was wasted regardless of how cheap it was to win.

What to do when the video campaign isn't performing

Before killing it, find out which part actually failed — the fix depends on the diagnosis, not the general sense that "it's not working."

  • Low impressions, decent CTR when it does show: that's a bid problem, not a creative problem. You're losing the vCPM or CPC auction. Raise the bid before touching the video.
  • Good impressions, low CTR: that's the creative. Check whether the product appears in the first two seconds, whether there's a black frame at the loop point, and whether anything important sits in the bottom-right corner under the mute toggle.
  • Good CTR, weak conversion after the click: that's the landing page, not the ad. Check whether the Store or product page a shopper lands on actually matches what the video showed them.
  • It worked for two weeks and then flattened: that's creative fatigue on a repeat audience. Swap the video before you touch the bid.

If none of that moves the number inside a fair test window, the honest answer is that video isn't the right format for this product or query, and a static Sponsored Brands or Sponsored Products campaign will do the job at lower production cost. Not every listing needs video — the ones that show a use, a transformation, or an unboxing moment benefit most.

Which one you should actually pick

Sponsored Brands video suits brands with a product worth showing in motion — a use, an unboxing, a transformation — and the willingness to iterate creative when a diagnosis says the video is the weak link. It suits less well a brand chasing a quick multi-product push, where a static Sponsored Brands campaign is cheaper to produce and just as fast to launch. Either way, the spec sheet and the bid math above hold regardless of who runs the account.

Common questions

Is a Sponsored Brands video different from a regular Sponsored Brands ad?

Yes. Regular Sponsored Brands uses a static headline image; Sponsored Brands video swaps that for an auto-playing muted video, 6 to 45 seconds long. Both run in the same auction and both can point to a Store or a product page, but the video version has its own file specs and its own creative rules around pacing and safe zones.

How long should a Sponsored Brands video be?

Amazon accepts 6 to 45 seconds but recommends 20 seconds or under. In practice, the strongest creative gets the product on screen in the first two seconds and has a clear beginning and end, since the ad loops and a weak cut point becomes a visible flicker on repeat.

Can I use vertical (9:16) video for Sponsored Brands?

This depends on which Amazon page you check, which is exactly the problem. Amazon's current technical ad-specs page lists 16:9 as the only accepted aspect ratio for Sponsored Brands video; other Amazon guidance elsewhere references vertical formats more broadly. Don't take either page's word as final — check the spec page at the moment you upload, and default to producing in 16:9 since that's what the acceptance criteria state today.

What's the minimum budget for Sponsored Brands video?

There's no flat minimum spend requirement. Amazon states campaigns can start at roughly $10 a day. It's an auction, billed on CPC or vCPM, so the real constraint is whether your bid is competitive for the terms you're targeting, not whether you've hit some budget floor.

Does the video need sound to work?

No — design against that assumption. The video starts muted by default and most shoppers never tap to unmute. Put anything essential to the message in on-screen text or captions; treat audio as a bonus layer for the minority who turn sound on, never as the delivery mechanism for your main point.
