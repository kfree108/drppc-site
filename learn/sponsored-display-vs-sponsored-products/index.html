Sponsored Display vs Sponsored Products: What Each One Actually Targets

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1283 words · Written against what currently ranked for “sponsored display vs sponsored products”
The short answer

Sponsored Products targets keywords and ASINs, putting a single product into search results on Amazon only. Sponsored Display targets shoppers based on behavior — views, purchases, category — on and off Amazon, with no keyword targeting available. Sponsored Brands sits between them, selling the brand at the top of search.

The One-Sentence Difference

Sponsored Products targets keywords and ASINs — it puts a single product into search results and onto other product pages, and it only runs on Amazon. Sponsored Display targets shoppers, not search terms — it uses signals like what someone viewed, bought, or browsed in a category, and it runs on Amazon and off it, on apps, Twitch, Prime Video, and other sites. There is no keyword targeting available in Sponsored Display at all.

That's the whole mechanical difference. Cost, placement, and which one to fund first all follow from that single fact: one is search-based, the other is audience-based.

Where Sponsored Brands Fits Into the Comparison

Add Sponsored Brands and you have three tools, not two, and each does something the others structurally cannot. Sponsored Brands is keyword-based like Sponsored Products, but it sells the brand rather than just the ASIN — it needs a logo and a headline, usually requires Brand Registry, and runs across the top of search and on product pages, on Amazon only.

  • Sponsored Products: keyword/ASIN targeting, single product, on-Amazon only.
  • Sponsored Brands: keyword/ASIN targeting, brand plus product collection, on-Amazon only, Brand Registry typically required.
  • Sponsored Display: audience and product targeting — views, purchases, category, remarketing — on and off Amazon, no keyword targeting.

The practical read: if a shopper is searching, Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands are competing for that search. If a shopper isn't searching right now — they looked at your listing three days ago and left, or they're reading reviews on a competitor's page — Sponsored Display is the only one of the three built to reach them there.

A Worked Example: Splitting a $3,000 Monthly Budget

Take a seller putting $3,000 a month behind one hero SKU with steady reviews and decent sell-through. A reasonable split, and the reasoning behind it:

  • $1,800 to Sponsored Products — this is where buying intent already exists. Someone typed the search term; don't lose that sale to a competitor's ad above yours.
  • $600 to Sponsored Brands — defends the top-of-search placement and cross-sells the rest of the catalog to people who already searched your category.
  • $600 to Sponsored Display — remarkets to shoppers who viewed the listing and didn't buy, and targets the detail pages of two or three direct competitors.

The exact split isn't a formula — it moves as reviews, price, and the competitive set change. But the order of operations holds: fund the search result first, defend the brand second, then go get the shopper who already looked and left. Display budget spent before Sponsored Products is funded properly is money spent chasing people who haven't shown any intent yet.

The Mistake We See Most (Including One We've Made)

The most common mistake is running a Sponsored Display product-targeting campaign against a competitor's ASIN and expecting a Sponsored Products conversion rate. It won't happen — someone who searched your brand name is a different shopper than someone currently looking at a rival's listing. Judge Display campaigns against Display benchmarks, not against the search campaign sitting next to it.

The mistake we've made ourselves: pulling a new Sponsored Display campaign after a week because the reported ACoS looked twice as high as the Sponsored Products campaign beside it, without accounting for Display's longer attribution window on some placements. Reported performance climbs for days after launch as sales attribute back. Judge it at fourteen days, not seven, before deciding it isn't working.

What to Check Before You Pull the Budget

If a Sponsored Display campaign looks bad, check three things before killing it: the attribution window (give it the full fourteen days), the targeting type (audience remarketing behaves differently than category prospecting — don't judge them against each other), and whether it's competing with your own Sponsored Products campaign for the same product-page real estate.

This is also where a measurement discipline matters more than the platform. Every proposed change to a live campaign should carry three things before it runs: the evidence behind it, a measurement plan, and a rollback trigger. That's true whether a person is making the call or software is — the discipline is what tells you whether "it isn't working" is real, or just a seven-day read on a fourteen-day metric.

Which one you should actually pick

Sponsored Products suits anyone with buying intent already searching — fund it first on any new listing. Sponsored Display suits retargeting and competitor prospecting once you have reviews and traffic to work with. Sponsored Brands suits catalog cross-sell and top-of-search defense. Most established sellers eventually run all three.

Common questions

Does Sponsored Display require Brand Registry?

Not always — the hard requirement is usually a professional seller or vendor account, and eligibility can vary by marketplace and account type. Brand Registry is the firmer requirement for Sponsored Brands. Check your account's eligibility directly in Amazon Ads before assuming either way.

Can competitors target my listing with Sponsored Display?

Yes. Product targeting lets any advertiser target a competitor's ASIN or category, and that includes your listing unless you're specifically excluded from someone's targeting. It's a normal, permitted part of the platform, not a violation.

Which one should I start with on a brand-new listing?

Sponsored Products. Display remarketing needs someone to remarket to, and category prospecting works better once you have reviews and a listing that converts. Fund the search campaign first, then layer Display in once there's traffic and data to work with.

Does Sponsored Display show up in Amazon search results?

Generally no. Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands compete for search results; Sponsored Display places ads on product pages, in other placements on Amazon, and off Amazon, based on audience and product targeting rather than a search query.

Can I run Sponsored Products and Sponsored Display on the same ASIN at once?

Yes, and most established sellers do. They're not competing for the same auction — one wins the search result, the other reaches people outside the search, so running both is standard practice rather than overlap to worry about.
