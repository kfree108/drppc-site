The Dimensional-Weight Audit That Adds Two Points Of Margin

Updated 2026-08-19 · 901 words · Written from 3 first-party data point(s) in our source bank
The short answer

FBA bills on the greater of unit weight and dimensional weight, so an oversized box on a light product pushes you into a higher fee tier. Shrinking the box below the tier threshold saved roughly $1.94 to $2.56 per unit on one product — worth more than most bid optimisations.

Why is your best seller your worst margin?

Because fees are charged on the package, not the product. We audited a fishing-tackle catalogue where the best-selling variant carried a $5.18 FBA fee and about 20% margin, while its siblings ran 44% to 53%. Nothing about the product explained the gap.

The unit weighed 1.119 lb. The box measured 6.77 by 6.38 by 6.06 inches, which produced a dimensional weight of 1.883 lb and pushed billable shipping weight to 2.00 lb. Amazon billed the box.

This pattern hides well. It never shows up in an advertising report, it does not look like a listing problem, and the product is selling — so nobody goes looking. It shows up as a catalogue where your volume leader is quietly your least profitable line.

What thresholds are you aiming for?

The audit is arithmetic, not judgment. You are trying to get billable weight below the next tier boundary, which usually means attacking volume rather than mass.

On the product above, the target was total shipping weight under 1.75 lb, which meant keeping total box volume under roughly 243 cubic inches. That drops the fee into the $2.62–$3.24 band and banks $1.94 to $2.56 per unit.

A related version of the same problem is dimensional rather than weight-based: we have seen a package sitting at 0.8 inches against a 0.75-inch small-standard threshold. Five hundredths of an inch, one fee tier. Redesign the packaging, do not argue with the scanner.

  1. Pull the fee preview for every ASIN and sort by fee as a percentage of price, not by fee in dollars. The worst offenders are cheap products with roomy boxes.
  2. For each of the worst, compare actual unit weight against dimensional weight. Where dimensional weight is winning, the box is the problem.
  3. Identify the next tier boundary below your current billable weight and calculate the volume you need to hit, not just the weight.
  4. Test a smaller box or a flatter pack on a low-volume variant first. Packaging changes have downstream consequences for damage rates and returns.
  5. Run an interim price test in parallel. On the tackle product we tested $9.99 specifically because sub-$10 pricing interacts with the low-price FBA fee band.
  6. Re-pull the fee preview after the change lands and confirm the tier moved. Do not assume it did.

When should you not file a dispute?

When Amazon is undercharging you, which happens. A client once pushed hard for an FBA measurement dispute; our audit found Amazon was under-billing nine SKUs, at $2.52 against a correct $3.02. Filing would have invited a remeasurement that cost money.

The expertise here is knowing when not to act. Run the transaction-level audit before you escalate — the same audit that found roughly $65,000 of wrong inbound shipping deductions in one month on another account also found the undercharge. The audit is the asset; the dispute is just one possible output.

What is this worth against other levers?

Two points of margin on your highest-volume SKU is typically larger and far more durable than an equivalent effort spent on bid optimisation, because it recurs on every unit forever and requires no ongoing management.

It also stacks with the other fee levers. Splitting inbound shipments across five or more locations removes inbound placement fees entirely — we took one account from roughly $2,400 a month in placement fees to $760 and then to zero. Bulk referral-fee discounts saved another account $2,000 a month, which its owner correctly described as a 10% boost to the bottom line.

None of that is advertising work, and all of it lands in the same profit line advertising is judged on.

What to do with this

Sort your catalogue by FBA fee as a percentage of selling price this week. If your volume leader is near the top of that list, the money is in the box, not the bids.

Frequently asked

How do I find dimensional weight for my products?

Amazon's fee preview report shows the dimensions and the billable weight it is using, which is what you need. Compare that billable figure against your actual unit weight — if billable is materially higher, dimensional weight is driving your fee.

Is it worth redesigning packaging for one fee tier?

On a high-volume SKU, almost always. Roughly $2 per unit on a product doing thousands of units a month is a permanent margin gain with no ongoing cost. On a slow mover it usually is not worth the tooling and testing.

Do these fee bands change?

Yes, and 2026 brought several changes including price-banded pick-and-pack fees. Verify every figure against Amazon's current rate card before you build a business case — we treat published third-party fee numbers as a starting point to check, not a source to cite.
