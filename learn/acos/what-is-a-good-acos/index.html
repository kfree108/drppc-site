What's A Good ACoS On Amazon? Benchmarks By Category

Updated 2026-08-19 · 834 words · Written from 3 first-party data point(s) in our source bank
The short answer

A good ACoS is any figure below your break-even ACoS that still buys the growth you want. Across our managed accounts, healthy non-branded ACoS runs 25–30% while branded runs 2–13%. Blending them into one number is what makes the question feel unanswerable.

Why does nobody give you a straight number?

Because the honest answer depends on two figures nobody outside your business knows: your contribution margin and your growth intent. A 40% ACoS is a disaster on a 30% margin product and a bargain on one with a 76% break-even.

We have measured a break-even ACoS of 76% in a live account, against a $36 target cost per acquisition the founder was happy with because he was paying more on other channels. In that account, ACoS figures that would panic most sellers were simply correct.

So the useful question is not what a good ACoS is. It is: what is my break-even ACoS, and how far below it do I want to sit given what I am trying to do this quarter.

What are the bands healthy accounts run in?

Split branded from non-branded before you compare anything. Blended ACoS is the number that makes this question unanswerable, because it averages an auction you already win with one you are trying to enter.

Across managed accounts, branded ACoS on a healthy brand runs from about 2% on a mature brand with strong organic rank to roughly 13% on a brand in its first year. Non-branded sits far higher by design — 25% to 30% paired with a 10–12% total advertising cost of sale is what a healthy, profitable supplement account looks like.

During a deliberate rank push we accept much worse and say so up front: an ACoS ladder of roughly 45%, then 30–35%, then 25% as position consolidates. Outside a push, we treat 200% ACoS as the threshold for killing a target rather than nursing it.

  • Branded, mature brand: ~2–6%
  • Branded, first-year brand: ~8–13%
  • Non-branded, steady state: ~25–30%
  • Non-branded, active rank push: 45% falling to 25% over the push
  • Kill threshold outside a push: ~200% ACoS

How do you calculate your break-even ACoS?

Break-even ACoS is your contribution margin expressed as a percentage of selling price. If $10 of a $40 product is profit after cost of goods, Amazon fees and fulfilment, your break-even ACoS is 25% — spend more than that per incremental sale and the sale costs you money.

Two things people get wrong here. First, they use gross margin rather than contribution margin, which ignores referral fees, fulfilment, storage and returns processing. Second, they calculate it once and never again, while fee changes and price changes move it every quarter.

Third, and most consequential: they forget lifetime value. If a meaningful share of first orders convert to Subscribe & Save, your true break-even on a first order is much higher than the single-order arithmetic suggests. See how to justify a painful CPC with LTV math.

What if your ACoS is far better than these bands?

Then you are probably underspending, and that is a growth problem rather than a win. We have seen an account sitting at a 5% total advertising cost of sale while sales fell 40% — beautifully efficient, steadily shrinking.

The test is whether your non-branded impression share is growing. If ACoS is excellent and non-branded share is flat, your efficiency is coming from harvesting demand you already had. That is the blended-ACoS illusion in its purest form, and the client question it eventually produces is the one every agency dreads: what exactly are you doing for my non-branded growth?

What to do with this

Work out your break-even ACoS from contribution margin, then split branded from non-branded reporting. Those two moves turn an unanswerable benchmarking question into two concrete targets you can manage against.

Frequently asked

What is the average ACoS on Amazon?

Any single average is misleading because branded and non-branded campaigns behave completely differently. In our managed accounts branded ACoS runs roughly 2–13% and non-branded roughly 25–30% in steady state — the blended figure in between describes no real campaign.

Is a 100% ACoS ever acceptable?

During a deliberate launch or rank push, yes, for a defined window. As a steady state it means you are buying revenue at cost. The distinction that matters is whether there is an end date and a rank target attached.

Should I optimise ACoS or TACoS?

Use ACoS to judge individual campaign decisions and TACoS to judge whether the whole programme is earning its place — and optimise profit dollars over both. We have seen a TACoS move from 15% to 20% cost $5,000 of profit while looking like nothing much on a chart.
