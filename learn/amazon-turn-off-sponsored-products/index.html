How to Turn Off Sponsored Products on Amazon — For Shoppers and Sellers

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1484 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon turn off sponsored products”
The short answer

There's no single 'off' switch. Shoppers can only opt out of interest-based ad targeting in Advertising Preferences — Sponsored Products still show. Sellers turn off Sponsored Products by pausing or archiving campaigns in Campaign Manager; there's no account-wide kill switch.

Two Different Questions Hiding in One Search

"Amazon turn off sponsored products" gets typed by two completely different people. One is a shopper who's tired of seeing ads and assumes there's a toggle. The other is a seller who wants to stop a campaign from spending money. The answer is different for each, and conflating them is why the top result for this search — Amazon's own Advertising Preferences page — leaves so many people annoyed.

That page controls interest-based ad targeting: whether Amazon uses your browsing history to pick which ads you see, on Amazon and off it. It has nothing to do with Sponsored Products listings showing up in search results. Turn that preference off and you will still see sponsored listings — they're just no longer personalized to your interests.

If you're a seller looking to stop your own Sponsored Products campaigns from running, skip that page entirely. It's built for shoppers, and it doesn't touch Seller Central or Campaign Manager.

If You're a Seller: There's No Master Switch

There is no account-level toggle that turns off Sponsored Products for a seller. You stop it campaign by campaign, in Campaign Manager: open the campaign, change its status from Enabled to Paused. Paused campaigns stop bidding on new auctions immediately and keep their history, so you can turn them back on later without losing performance data.

Archiving is different and effectively permanent — once archived, a campaign can't be reactivated. You'd have to rebuild it from scratch. Most sellers who type "turn off" actually mean pause, not archive.

Worked example: say a campaign runs a $50 daily budget, and by noon it's already spent $32. You pause it. That $32 is gone — it's already-served spend, not refundable — but the remaining $18 of budget simply never gets used, and nothing rolls over to tomorrow. If instead you'd cut the daily budget to $1, the campaign could still spend up to that reduced amount before the change fully takes effect. Pausing is the faster, cleaner way to actually stop spend; cutting the budget is a lever for controlling spend, not stopping it.

The Five Levels You Can Actually Turn Off

"Turn off Sponsored Products" can mean five different things depending on how much of the account you're trying to affect. The table below is the real hierarchy — match the level to the problem, because pausing the wrong one is the most common mistake we see, including mistakes we've made ourselves early in an account.

The mistake worth naming directly: a seller wants to stop one bad keyword and ends up pausing the entire campaign, which also stops every keyword and product that was working fine. A single unprofitable search term almost never justifies shutting down a campaign that's profitable everywhere else.

Settings That Look Like Off but Aren't — and What to Check When Pausing Didn't Work

A few statuses get mistaken for "off":

  • Out of Budget — the campaign hit its daily cap and stopped serving for the day. It's not paused; it starts spending again at midnight Pacific time. If the same campaign goes Out of Budget every day, that's a sign it's underfunded relative to demand, not a sign it's turned off.
  • Dayparting or budget rules — these narrow when or how much a campaign can spend. They reduce exposure; they don't stop it.
  • Negative keywords or negative ASINs — these block specific matches, not the campaign itself. Useful for precision, useless if the goal is to stop everything.

If you've paused a campaign and it's still showing impressions, give it a little time first — status changes can take a few hours to fully propagate through Amazon's ad delivery systems, and a handful of impressions can come from auctions already in progress when you paused. If it's still serving after that, check whether you paused the campaign itself or just one ad group inside it — a campaign can be enabled while every ad group under it is paused, and the reverse. It's an easy setting to change by mistake.

If You're a Shopper: What the Ad Preferences Toggle Actually Does

Amazon's Advertising Preferences page lets you choose "Do not show me interest-based ads." Turning it off means Amazon won't use your on-Amazon activity to target you with ads elsewhere, and won't use advertiser-provided data to target you on Amazon. That's a real, meaningful privacy control.

What it doesn't do: stop Sponsored Products from appearing in your search results. Amazon says this plainly on its own page — even with the setting off, "you may still see ads provided by Amazon, they just will not be based on your interests." Sponsored Products are triggered by what you search and browse in the moment, not by a stored interest profile, so this toggle leaves them untouched. There is currently no shopper-facing setting that removes sponsored listings from Amazon search results.

Common questions

Does pausing a Sponsored Products campaign delete my data?

No. Pausing keeps campaign history, keywords, bids, and performance data intact, and you can re-enable it any time. Archiving is the one that's effectively permanent — Amazon doesn't let you reactivate an archived campaign, so you'd start over.

How long does it take for a paused campaign to stop showing ads?

Usually minutes, sometimes a few hours. Amazon's ad delivery systems don't update instantly across the whole account, and any auction already in progress when you paused will still complete.

Can I turn off Sponsored Products for one product without pausing the whole campaign?

Yes. Remove or pause that specific product within the ad group rather than pausing the campaign. That stops ads for that ASIN while everything else in the campaign keeps running.

If I set my daily budget to $0, does that turn off the campaign?

Not reliably. Amazon typically requires a minimum daily budget above $0, and even a very low budget can still spend before the change fully takes effect. Pausing is the direct, immediate way to stop a campaign; budget is a lever for controlling spend, not stopping it.

Will turning off interest-based ads in my Amazon account stop me from seeing Sponsored Products?

No. That setting only affects whether ads are personalized to your interests. Sponsored Products are tied to your search and browse activity in the moment, and there's currently no consumer setting that removes them from search results.
