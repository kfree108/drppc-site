Who Mina Elias Is

Mina Elias built a supplement brand while working full-time as a chemical engineer, funding the ad spend himself and learning Amazon PPC the slow way — trial, error, and his own money on the line. That brand still runs today, and it's the credibility behind Trivium Group, the agency he started after other sellers began asking him to manage their accounts.

He later packaged what he learned into PPC University, a paid course with 300+ students and 60+ modules covering campaign structure, targeting, bid strategy, search term reports, and Amazon DSP. He's also active on LinkedIn, where his more recent posts talk about building AI agents to speed up the account audits he used to sell as a $5,000, three-week deliverable.

None of this makes him unique in being a practitioner-turned-teacher — plenty of Amazon PPC educators have that arc. What sets his content apart is how mechanical it is: he teaches the actual sheet-level workflow, not just concepts.

The Core Method: Harvest, Negate, Repeat

The backbone of his teaching is the search term report, pulled and read regularly rather than glanced at monthly. The logic is simple: a search term that's already converting under a broad or auto campaign is proof of demand, and it deserves its own exact-match campaign with its own bid. A search term burning clicks with no orders is proof of the opposite, and it gets negated before it costs more.

Here's the arithmetic that makes the decision, not just the theory. Say a search term shows 40 clicks, 3 orders, and $52 in ad spend against an average $60 sale price — that's roughly 29% ACOS at the term level, worth pulling into its own exact match campaign at a bid you control. A neighboring term with 85 clicks and zero orders isn't underperforming, it's telling you to cut it before it costs another $100 this month.

The mistake most sellers make applying this isn't the math — it's the patience. Pulling the trigger on 15 clicks and no sale isn't a pattern yet on most search volumes; it's noise. We've made this exact error ourselves: killing a keyword at eight clicks because ACOS spiked, only to see later that it converts reliably by click ten. The report tells you what happened. It doesn't tell you the sample size was too small to act on.

Using PPC to Move Organic Rank

The other piece of his framework that doesn't show up in most beginner content is using PPC deliberately to influence organic rank, not just to drive direct sales. The idea: a keyword that converts well under a paid click also tells Amazon's algorithm something about relevance, and sustained ad-driven conversions on a term can pull the organic ranking for that same term upward over time.

This only works if you're tracking organic rank on the specific keywords you're pushing spend against — not just watching total sales. Without that tracking, you can't tell whether a budget increase actually moved rank or just bought more of the same clicks at a higher cost. This is the piece sellers skip most often, because it requires a rank tracker running alongside the ad account, not just the Amazon ad console.

Bid Math and Bulk Sheets

At scale, his method moves from the campaign manager UI into bulk sheets — spreadsheets that let you push bid changes across hundreds of keywords in one pass instead of clicking through them one by one. The logic is target-ACOS driven: raise bids on keywords running under target, cut bids (or pause) on keywords running over, and check placement reports separately, since top-of-search and rest-of-search often carry very different efficiency for the same keyword.

  • Placement-level ACOS — top of search usually costs more per click but converts better; blending it with rest-of-search numbers hides which one is actually working.
  • Bulk sheet cadence — weekly is the rhythm he teaches; monthly is too slow to catch a bleeding keyword before it costs real money.
  • Bid step size — small, frequent adjustments beat large infrequent ones, because a big bid cut can also kill a keyword's delivery entirely.

Common questions

Is PPC University, Mina Elias's course, worth taking?

It depends on what you need. If you want to understand the actual mechanics of running Amazon PPC yourself — campaign structure, search term reports, bulk sheets, DSP basics — it's a genuinely detailed course built by someone who ran his own account for years. It won't manage your account for you, and it won't replace the weekly hours the method requires once you're applying it.

Does Mina Elias personally manage PPC accounts?

He built Trivium Group as the agency arm of his work and trained a team to apply his methods to client accounts. The course and the agency service are two separate offers — one teaches you to do it, the other does it for you.

Can I run his search-term harvesting method without an agency?

Yes. It's a spreadsheet-and-discipline process, not proprietary software — a search term report, a bulk sheet, and a consistent weekly cadence get you most of the way there. The limiting factor is usually time, not access to the method.

How is automated PPC management different from what he teaches?

His method is manual and educational — you learn the audit, you run it yourself. Automated management, like Dr. PPC, applies the same underlying logic (harvest, negate, adjust bids, check rank) continuously across an account, with evidence and a rollback attached to each change, without requiring someone to run the spreadsheet by hand every week.

What's the biggest mistake people make copying this method?

Acting on too little data — pausing or harvesting a keyword after a handful of clicks instead of waiting for a sample size that actually means something. The search term report tells you what happened, not whether it's a pattern.
