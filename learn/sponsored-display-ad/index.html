What Is a Sponsored Display Ad, and How Does It Actually Work?

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1430 words · Written against what currently ranked for “sponsored display ad”
The short answer

A Sponsored Display ad is Amazon's audience-targeting ad format — it doesn't bid on keywords. It targets shoppers by browsing behavior, product views, or interests, and runs on Amazon pages, competitor listings, and third-party sites and apps. Amazon now calls the category 'display ads,' but the mechanics are unchanged.

What a Sponsored Display ad is (and why the name just changed)

Sponsored Display is the Amazon ad type that doesn't bid on a single keyword. It shows your product to shoppers based on what they've browsed, what they've shown interest in, or which product page they landed on — on Amazon, on a competitor's listing, or off Amazon entirely on a third-party site or app.

Amazon folded Sponsored Display into a broader "display ads" bucket inside its unified ad console. If you already run Sponsored Display campaigns, nothing changes — they keep serving exactly as before under the campaign manager. If you're setting up a new campaign, you'll click "Display" instead of "Sponsored Display," but the targeting options, the bidding, and the placements underneath are the same mechanism with a new label on the button.

To run it you need to be Brand Registered or a Vendor. There's no minimum spend requirement — you set a daily budget and a bid, same as Sponsored Products.

How the targeting and the bidding actually work

There are three targeting options, and eligibility differs by seller type:

  • Product targeting — show your ad on a specific product's page or category. Available to vendors and Brand Registered sellers. This is the option people use to appear on a competitor's listing.
  • Views remarketing — show your ad to shoppers who viewed your product page, or a similar one, in the last 30 days and didn't buy. Same eligibility.
  • Audience interests — reach shoppers who've shown interest in a related category, independent of whether they've ever seen your listing. Vendor-only.

Pricing runs on one of two models: CPC, where you pay per click, or vCPM, where you pay per 1,000 viewable impressions. Say you run a CPC campaign with a $0.75 bid and a $50 daily budget. If every dollar of budget converted straight to a click at that bid, you'd get roughly 66 clicks a day before the cap hits — in practice you'll get fewer, because you're bidding against other advertisers for the same placement and you don't win every auction you enter. The arithmetic is simple; the auction dynamics underneath it aren't, and that's the part a definition page never shows you.

Amazon's own starting guidance is a $1 default bid for CPC and $5 for vCPM, adjusted from there once you can see performance, with $40–$60 a day suggested as a starting daily budget for smaller businesses.

Sponsored Display video ads

Sponsored Display can run as video — Amazon groups this in as online video (OLV) inside the display format. It's a different creative unit from Sponsored Brands video, which sits at the top of search results and requires a keyword. Sponsored Display video rides the same audience targeting as the standard image unit: product targeting, views remarketing, or interests.

Creative can be auto-generated by Amazon from your listing images and copy, or you can upload your own. If you add several products to one campaign, Amazon rotates the creative most likely to convert automatically — you're not manually A/B testing which image runs.

Display ads vs Sponsored Products vs Sponsored Brands

The question underneath "display ads vs sponsored ads" is really: which one uses a keyword, and which one uses a shopper's behavior? Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands are both keyword-and-ASIN auctions that show up inside search results. Sponsored Display is the only one of the three that runs on audience signals instead, and the only one that can follow a shopper off Amazon onto a third-party site or app.

They aren't substitutes for each other. Most accounts running full-funnel end up using all three, at different stages of the same purchase journey.

The mistakes that waste budget here — including ones we've made

The most common mistake is treating Sponsored Display like a ranking tool. It isn't. Because it doesn't target keywords, it does nothing for your organic search rank, no matter how much you spend on it. If you need rank movement, that's a Sponsored Products conversation, not a Sponsored Display one.

The second mistake — one we've made too — is turning on product targeting against every competitor ASIN in a category without checking whether those listings actually convert on click-through. Aggressive bids on high-traffic competitor pages burn budget fast if the shopper never had intent to switch. Check the placement report before you scale a product-targeting group, not after it's already spent the week's budget.

If a Sponsored Display campaign isn't converting, don't just raise the bid. Pull the placement report and split spend between on-Amazon and off-Amazon placements — they perform differently, and vCPM campaigns in particular can carry a low viewable-impression rate that never shows up in the top-line ACOS. Before you change anything, decide what you'll roll back if the change doesn't work. Every proposed change carries three things before it runs: the evidence behind it, a measurement plan, and a rollback trigger — that discipline matters more here than in Sponsored Products, because Sponsored Display's audience pools are smaller and noisier, and a bad week can look like a bad campaign when it's actually a bad sample size.

Common questions

Do Sponsored Display ads target keywords?

No. That's the defining difference between Sponsored Display and the other two ad types. It targets based on product views, shopping behavior, or audience interests, never a search term you bid on.

Is Sponsored Display the same as Amazon's new 'display ads'?

Yes. Amazon renamed the category as part of unifying its ad console with Amazon DSP workflows. Existing Sponsored Display campaigns keep running unchanged; new campaigns are created under the "Display" label instead. The targeting options and pricing models underneath are the same.

Can Sponsored Display ads help my Amazon SEO or ranking?

No, not directly. Ranking is driven by conversion rate and relevance on keyword search, and Sponsored Display doesn't target keywords or influence that search algorithm. It's built for reach and retargeting, not for moving a listing up a search results page.

What's the minimum budget for a Sponsored Display campaign?

There's no required minimum. Amazon's own guidance suggests $40–$60 a day as a starting daily budget for smaller businesses, adjusted up or down once you can see the placement report and click-through data.

Do I need Brand Registry to run Sponsored Display?

Third-party sellers need to be enrolled in Brand Registry. Vendors have separate eligibility and are the only account type that can use audience-interest targeting rather than product targeting or views remarketing.
