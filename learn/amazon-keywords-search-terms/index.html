Keywords vs. search terms: two different things wearing one name

People use "Amazon keywords" and "Amazon search terms" interchangeably, but sellers mean three different things by them, and mixing them up is where most keyword research goes wrong.

  • Keywords — the actual words and phrases shoppers type into Amazon's search box. These live in Amazon's data, not yours.
  • Search terms field — the backend metadata box in Seller Central where you type words that support discoverability but don't belong in your visible title or bullets.
  • Search term report — a PPC reporting feature that shows the exact customer queries that triggered your Sponsored Products ads. This is a different tool for a different job, and it's the one most keyword guides skip entirely.

If you're publishing a book through KDP, none of this applies the way it does for a physical product listing — KDP's keyword field has its own, separate rules and a 50-character limit per field, unrelated to the Seller Central search terms box. Confusing the two is a common source of wasted effort.

Where keywords actually work on a listing

Amazon's indexing algorithm reads several fields, and it doesn't weight them equally. Title and bullets carry the most indexing weight and are what shoppers actually read, so they need to work as sentences, not keyword lists. The backend search terms field exists specifically to catch synonyms, misspellings, and phrasing you can't fit into visible copy without it reading like it was written by a bot.

The table below is the structure — use it before you touch a spreadsheet of keywords.

Backend search terms: a worked example on the byte limit

Amazon's backend search terms field is capped at 249 bytes total, not characters — in plain English, that means about 249 letters if you're only using standard English text with no accented characters. Here's the mistake nearly every new seller makes: they repeat words already in the title, and run out of room before adding anything new.

Take this string: "reusable water bottle insulated leak proof gift for camping hiking" — count it out and it comes to 66 bytes. That leaves roughly 183 bytes of the 249 unused. If your title already contains "reusable water bottle," repeating those two words here wastes 21 bytes for zero indexing gain — Amazon already indexes them from the title. The field is for the words you couldn't say naturally on the page: regional spellings, category slang, gift-occasion terms, use-case phrases. Spend the bytes on words the customer would type but you wouldn't write.

The other search terms: what the PPC report is actually for

Separate from the backend field, Amazon's search term report (inside Sponsored Products reporting) shows you the literal queries that triggered an impression or a click on your ad. This is where most of the real decision-making happens once a listing is live — deciding which customer search terms to add as exact-match keywords, and which to negate because they're burning spend on the wrong intent.

This is a live, ongoing read of the account, not a one-time list you build and forget. Every proposed change carries three things before it runs: the evidence behind it, a measurement plan, and a rollback trigger — that discipline matters more for search term decisions than almost anywhere else in an account, because a bad negative keyword can quietly cut off a converting query for months before anyone notices the volume drop.

Common mistakes — including ones we've made

  • Keyword stuffing bullets. Cramming search phrases into bullet points instead of writing benefit-led copy hurts conversion rate, and Amazon's algorithm increasingly weighs conversion signals alongside relevance. A keyword-dense bullet that nobody reads to the end doesn't help you.
  • Repeating title words in the backend field. Covered above — it's the single most common waste of the 249-byte allowance.
  • Adding competitor brand names as search terms. This risks a listing suppression or a policy flag, and it rarely converts even when it's not caught.
  • Treating PPC negative keywords as permanent. We've done this ourselves early on with accounts — negating a broad-looking search term that was actually converting for a specific segment, because the aggregate ACOS looked bad without segmenting by term. The fix is to check conversion rate on that specific term before negating it, not just its cost.
  • Assuming more keywords always means more visibility. Amazon doesn't index by volume of terms present, it indexes by relevance and rewards listings that convert on the traffic they already get.

When your keywords aren't working — troubleshooting

If a backend term isn't indexing after 24–48 hours, check three things in order: byte count (are you actually within 249, counted correctly), restricted words (subjective claims, competitor names, and certain regulated terms get silently dropped), and relevance (Amazon does not index terms it judges unrelated to the product category, no matter how many bytes you have left).

If a term is indexing but rank isn't moving, the problem usually isn't the keyword — it's conversion rate on that keyword, price relative to the category, or image quality on the search results thumbnail. Sellers often keep adding search terms to fix a problem that a keyword can't fix. If you've confirmed indexing and traffic is arriving but not converting, the next lever is the listing itself, not the keyword list.

Common questions

How many keywords can I put in the Amazon backend search terms field?

The field is capped at 249 bytes, not a word count — roughly 249 characters if you're writing in plain English with no accented letters. Once you subtract words already covered by your title and bullets, most sellers have far less usable space than they think.

Does Amazon show search volume for keywords?

No. Amazon doesn't publish an official search volume metric the way Google Ads does. Third-party keyword tools estimate volume from autocomplete frequency and other signals, which is useful for prioritization but should be read as a directional estimate, not a guaranteed number.

Can I use a competitor's brand name as a search term?

You can add it, but it carries real risk — Amazon can suppress a listing for policy violations, and even when it isn't caught, competitor-brand traffic rarely converts to your listing. It's a fragile tactic, not a keyword strategy.

What's the difference between backend search terms and a PPC search term report?

The backend field is metadata you write once to help organic indexing. The PPC search term report is Amazon showing you, after the fact, which customer queries actually triggered your ads — it's a live decision-making tool for exact-match and negative keywords, not something you fill in and leave.

Why isn't a new keyword showing up in Amazon search yet?

Check byte count and restricted words first, then relevance — Amazon quietly ignores backend terms it judges unrelated to the product, regardless of remaining space. If it's indexed but rank isn't improving, the issue is usually conversion rate or price, not the keyword itself.
