Why the -63% Sponsored Display Impression Recount Is Happening

For years, Amazon Sponsored Display reported raw impression counts that were structurally inflated compared with stricter viewable-impression standards. Ad slots loaded below the fold on dense detail pages, rapid-scroll placements, and multi-call ad requests were counted as impressions even when a shopper never meaningfully saw the unit.

Effective January 1, 2027, Amazon aligns Sponsored Display reporting to a tighter, standardized impression-counting rule. Across typical accounts, that cleanup removes roughly 63% of previously reported impression volume. Nothing breaks in the auction and your ads do not suddenly stop serving — the ruler changed, not the room.

The real operational danger is not the recount itself. The danger is what happens in Week 1 of January 2027 when unprepared teams, legacy BI dashboards, and rigid rules engines mistake a measurement reset for a campaign collapse:

  • False-alarm budget cuts: Stakeholders see "-63% Display Impressions" and "+170% CPM" in a Monday scorecard and pause profitable views-remarketing and product-targeting campaigns.
  • Runaway bid automation: Scripts or third-party rules keyed to week-over-week impression drops or CPM caps auto-slash bids or dump budgets into the wrong campaigns.
  • Permanent YoY blindness: Once Amazon deletes legacy Display reports on January 31, 2027, any brand that failed to back up its 2025–2026 baseline can no longer reconcile pre-recount versus post-recount reporting for audits, Q1 board reviews, or agency handoffs.

How -63% Impressions Changes CPM, CTR, CPC, ACoS, and ROAS

Every metric that uses impressions in its formula moves mechanically by roughly 2.7× (+170%) because the denominator shrinks from 100% to 37%. Every metric built strictly from spend, clicks, orders, and revenue stays completely flat.

The 5-Step Legacy Report Backup Checklist (Complete Before January 31, 2027)

Do not wait until the final week of January. Run this 5-step checklist once in late December 2026 and verify the final December full-month export in the first week of January — well before Amazon permanently deletes legacy Display reports on January 31, 2027.

  1. Export every 2025–2026 Sponsored Display legacy report (CSV + API backup). Pull both daily and monthly granularity for Campaign, Ad Group, Targeting, Advertised Product, and Purchased Product reports across the maximum available lookback window. Store immutable copies in cloud storage (Google Drive, S3, or BigQuery) with clear filenames like sd-campaign-legacy-2026-pre-recount.csv.
  2. Separate vCPM vs. CPC, creative formats, and targeting lanes before archiving. Make sure your exported tables preserve bid optimization type (vCPM vs. CPC), targeting type (Product Targeting, Views Remarketing, Purchases Remarketing), and click-through vs. view-through conversions so you can explain mix shifts later.
  3. Build a normalization bridge in your reporting stack. Add a permanent vertical annotation at 2027-01-01 in Looker Studio, Excel, Google Sheets, or Orbit labeled "Amazon Display Impression Recount (-63%)". After two full settled weeks in January, compute your account’s empirical ratio (Jan Daily Impressions ÷ Dec Daily Impressions at constant spend — typically around 0.37×) so analysts never compare unadjusted 2026 vs. 2027 impressions or CPM.
  4. Audit and pause impression- or CPM-triggered bid rules before December 31. Inspect every automated rule, third-party PPC tool, and Slack/email alert in your stack. Any rule reading "if impressions drop > 40% WoW", "if CPM > $10 pause targeting", or "if CTR > 0.25% raise bid" will misfire on January 1–7. Switch rules to clicks, spend, orders, ACoS, and TACoS — or recalibrate CPM/CTR thresholds by ~2.7×.
  5. Send a 1-paragraph pre-read to finance, leadership, and clients before Q1 starts. Show them the worked table above before January 1. Five minutes of proactive framing prevents an entire week of panicked Slack threads asking why Display CPM almost tripled overnight.
Operator takeaway

Treat January 1, 2027 as a reporting cutover and January 31, 2027 as a data-preservation deadline. Judge Sponsored Display on complete 14-day attribution windows, spend, orders, ACoS, and account-level TACoS — never on raw impression volume alone.

Frequently asked questions

Why are Amazon Sponsored Display impressions dropping by ~63% on January 1, 2027?

Starting January 1, 2027, Amazon updates how Sponsored Display impressions are counted to standardize measurement and filter out non-qualifying legacy ad loads. Because the new counting standard strips roughly 63% of previously counted raw impressions, reported Display impression volume drops sharply on January 1 even when actual shopper reach, clicks, spend, and orders stay steady.

Does the 2027 Display impression recount change ACoS, ROAS, CPC, or actual ad sales?

No. Actual spend, clicks, CPC, attributed orders, sales, ACoS, and ROAS do not change as a result of the recount itself. Only impression-denominated metrics shift mechanically: because reported impressions fall by ~63% (to ~37% of the old figure), apparent CPM rises by roughly +170% (~2.7×) and apparent CTR rises by roughly +170% (~2.7×) on identical delivery.

What happens on January 31, 2027 if I do not export my legacy Display reports?

On January 31, 2027, Amazon permanently deletes legacy pre-recount Sponsored Display reporting files and historical legacy report endpoints. Brands and agencies that fail to archive their 2025–2026 campaign, targeting, and placement CSVs before January 31 lose the pre-recount baseline needed for accurate year-over-year Q1 and Q4 comparisons.

How does the recount affect vCPM versus CPC Sponsored Display campaigns?

CPC campaigns bill strictly per click, so your cost per click and daily spend pacing remain unaffected unless you have automated rules tied to impressions, CTR, or CPM. For vCPM campaigns, review your viewable impression volume and cost per thousand viewable impressions closely across the first two weeks of January and recalibrate bid ceilings and pacing alerts against post-recount baselines rather than 2026 numbers.

How should we compare 2026 and 2027 Sponsored Display performance in executive reporting?

Do not plot raw 2026 and 2027 Display impressions, CPM, or CTR on an unannotated continuous trendline. Add a January 1, 2027 methodology break marker to every dashboard, keep spend, clicks, orders, sales, ACoS, and TACoS as your continuous YoY KPIs, and compute an account-specific normalization factor from your archived December 2026 legacy reports versus January 2027 post-recount delivery.
